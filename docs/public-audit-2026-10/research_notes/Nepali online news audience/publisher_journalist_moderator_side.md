# Operator-side pain points and expected practices for Nepali online publishers (publishers, editors, journalists, citizen reporters, moderators, fact-checkers), 2022-2026

Research date: 2026-10-14. Scope: what the people who run and write for Nepali online news/information portals face, and the legal/ethical obligations a volunteer-run civic-information site in Nepal inherits. Nepal-specific, 2022-2026 preferred; older items are flagged. Nepali-calendar dates are given with the Gregorian equivalent where the source supplied one.

## Key Question 1: Legal and regulatory framework (registration, Press Council code, ETA Section 47, Social Media Bill, defamation, privacy, RTI, press accreditation)

### Takeaway
Any "online journalism" operation in Nepal is expected to register with the Department of Information and Broadcasting (DoIB) under the Online Media Operation Directive 2017 and, since the November 2025 amendment to the Press and Publication Rules, to pay Rs 5,000 to register and Rs 2,500 per year to renew, run as a company/firm with a named editor and at least three staff, display registration number, addresses, editor/journalist names and a privacy policy on the site, and correct errors publicly. The main criminal exposure for operators and their users is Section 47 of the Electronic Transactions Act 2063 (2008) (up to 5 years and NPR 100,000 fine, used repeatedly against journalists and online critics 2024-2026), plus criminal defamation and contempt (jail sentences handed to online editors in 2024 and 2026); the 2025 Social Media Bill was withdrawn in February 2026.

### Cited Findings

**Registration regime (who, where, documents, fees)**
- The Online Media Operation Directive, 2017 (Online Media Operation Directive 2073) requires "any person or firm wanting to do online journalism" to apply to the Department of Information; existing online media and print/broadcast outlets running online editions had 60 days to register; renewal is due by mid-July each year with a tax clearance certificate and previous fiscal year's audit report; outlets must appoint an editor, journalists, manager and employees paid at least the Working Journalists Act minimum; online journalists must obtain a press accreditation card; outlets must keep a copy of everything published; non-compliant outlets "shall not" be registered/renewed and "will not be entitled to the facilities to be provided by the government" — [The Himalayan Times, "Online media operation code issued", 22 Mar 2017](https://thehimalayantimes.com/kathmandu/online-media-operation-code-issued/); directive PDF (Nepali) at [research.butmedia.org OnlineDirective2073.pdf](https://research.butmedia.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/OnlineDirective2073.pdf)
- The Directive was issued under Clause 45 of the Good Governance (Management and Operation) Act 2008, a law unrelated to media; Clause 6(3) of the Directive (allowing blocking of services not renewed annually) was nullified by the Supreme Court on 13 April 2021, yet DoIB continued annual renewal; a July 2021/22 DoIB notice said non-renewed outlets would not be cancelled or blocked but their staff would not get press credentials — [CMR-Nepal / research.butmedia.org, "State continues attempt to tighten grips on online media"](https://research.butmedia.org/?p=861)
- The AHN Legal summary of the "Online Media Operation Regulation, 2017": anyone operating online journalism must register; documents include company registration certificate, articles of association, PAN, domain registration certificate; renew annually within three months after the end of each fiscal year; content must not incite violence/terrorism/crime, name unrelated persons, or violate privacy; outlets must verify authenticity of news, materials and external links before publishing, separate news from ads/opinion, protect sources; the media house is "fully responsible" for content it publishes and for comments on its platform and must prevent vulgar, dishonest or irrelevant comments; penalties range from warning to temporary suspension to cancellation of the licence of the journalist or media house, plus possible fines/imprisonment under other laws — [AHN Legal, "Online media regulation in Nepal"](https://www.ahnlegal.com/legal-updates/legal/online-media-regulation-in-nepal)
- Mid-2025 churn: an amendment to the Printing and Publication Act published in the Nepal Gazette on 30 July 2025 moved online media registration from the Department of Information to district authorities, reportedly surprising the minister — [Asia News Network / Kathmandu Post, "Amendment to Nepal's media law raises a host of questions"](https://asianews.network/amendment-to-nepals-media-law-raises-a-host-of-questions/); DoIB then stopped registering and renewing online media (August 2025 notice) — [B360, "DoIB stops online media registration and renewal"](https://www.b360nepal.com/detail/25971/doib-stops-online-media-registration-and-renewal); the Nepal Press Federation asked government to settle online renewal/registration — [eKantipur, 7 Aug 2025](https://ekantipur.com/news/2025/08/07/en/request-to-govt-to-discuss-and-conclude-online-renewal-and-registration-30-54.html)
- Current rules (Press and Publication (Second Amendment) Rules, amended Kartik 13, 2082 BS, under the Press and Publication Act 2048 as amended by the Some Nepal Laws Amendment Act 2082, which had halted online registration/renewal from Shrawan 14, 2082): registration re-centralised at DoIB under the Ministry of Communications and IT; documents: registration certificate and memorandum of association, citizenship of editor and directors, proof of editor's education or journalism experience, tax clearance and audit report, domain approval, written commitment to pay salaries through a bank; outlet must be a company or firm with PAN/VAT; editor plus at least three employees; new registration fee Rs 5,000, renewal Rs 2,500 (existing outlets to pay from end of Poush); late renewal surcharge 10% in first month rising to 200% from the seventh month, then cancellation; no name/domain similar to an existing outlet; websites must display registration number, office and operator addresses, contact details, editor and journalist names and a privacy policy; errors must be corrected promptly and publicly; local authority and Press Council may monitor; 5,135 online media were registered (renewal of all would yield about Rs 1.28 crore, which DoIB Director Minkumar Sharma said goes to the treasury as tax) — [eKantipur (Nepali), Daya Dudraj, "The government's skewed approach to online media registration", 9 Nov 2025](https://ekantipur.com/news/2025/11/09/online-media-registration-fees-regulating-or-curbing-freedom-15-27.html) (English summary page: [link](https://ekantipur.com/news/2025/11/09/en/online-media-registration-fees-regulating-or-curbing-freedom-15-27.html))
- Criticisms of the 2025 rules: Nepal Press Federation (Secretary General Ram Prasad Dahal) raised five objections including "double payment" (company registration plus media registration) and discrimination because print outlets do not renew annually; researcher Ujjwal Acharya said fees are inappropriate during a crisis for these outlets; Mediakurakani editor Raviraj Baral said the rules burden small and alternative outlets and create barriers for new entrants — same [eKantipur article](https://ekantipur.com/news/2025/11/09/online-media-registration-fees-regulating-or-curbing-freedom-15-27.html)
- A 2026 law-firm guide says DoIB resumed registering and renewing online media at the end of 2025 and remains the authority in 2026, and advises confirming editor qualifications and minimum-capital details directly with the Department — [Lawalpine, "Online media registration in Nepal"](https://lawalpine.com/blog/online-media-registration-in-nepal)
- Provincial parallel track: FNJ statement of 15 July 2026 says renewal for 327 online media and journalists' press passes registered under Bagmati Province's Communication Registrar's Office had not started as FY 2083/84 approached — [FNJ press release 980](https://fnjnepal.org/en/press/980)
- DoIB maintains records of registration/operation of online media and provides them regularly to the Press Council — [The Himalayan Times, "Online media registration from DoI"](https://thehimalayantimes.com/nepal/online-media-registration-from-doi)

**Press Council Nepal (PCN) code and enforcement powers**
- PCN issued a 13-clause Code of Journalistic Ethics, 2016 on 22 Sept 2016 covering print, broadcast and online. Prohibitions include: information that adds to the agony of victims; "scary photos"; presenting ads as news or news as ads; naming people not associated with an incident; content affecting sub-judice cases; accepting gifts from government/non-government agencies; unnecessary rumours about health; content hurting the self-respect of disabled and minority communities. Sanctions: revoke press accreditation cards; recommend removal of the outlet from print/electronic/online classification; recommend denial of government advertisements and PSAs — [The Himalayan Times, "PCN issues new code of journalistic ethics"](https://thehimalayantimes.com/kathmandu/press-council-nepal-issues-new-code-journalistic-ethics/); FNJ hosts the Journalist Code of Conduct 2016 at [fnjnepal.org/np/resource/42](https://fnjnepal.org/np/resource/42)
- The Patan High Court rejected an interim order sought by Diyo Media and let PCN continue regulating and acting against online portals spreading unverified/misleading news — [Radio Nepal, "Patan High Court allows Press Council for regulating online news portals"](https://radionepalonline.com/en/?p=399256)
- PCN enforcement pattern: in April 2023 PCN asked the Nepal Telecommunications Authority to block 15 unregistered portals that had republished News Agency Nepal content; Freedom Forum called blocking beyond PCN's jurisdiction — [IFEX](https://ifex.org/?p=340846); PCN gives listed outlets clarification notices (e.g., 17 outlets given 24 hours) and orders unlisted ones to remove content; Freedom Forum criticised the lack of evidence — [Freedom Forum, "Press Council Nepal continues diktat on online media"](https://freedomforum.org.np/press-council-nepal-continues-diktat-on-online-media-directs-to-remove-contents); four outlets blacklisted in one November statement for publishing without fact-checking — [Freedom Forum news 774](https://freedomforum.org.np/news/detail/774)
- PCN complaints volume: 163 complaints registered by mid-Poush of FY 2082/83 (Jan 2026), handled via 77 rebuttals published, 52 responses communicated, 14 apologies/corrections, 5 contents withdrawn, 10 explanations sought; monitoring tightened ahead of elections — [eKantipur, 22 Jan 2026](https://ekantipur.com/news/2026/01/22/en/163-complaints-registered-with-press-council-in-6-months-48-41.html); [The Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/nepal/press-council-registers-163-complaints-tightens-media-monitoring-ahead-of-elections)
- July 2026 (Sunsari "Captainjanj" incident): PCN reviewed 207 items, found 90 violated the Code, ordered removal by phone of 59 items from 34 media outlets and 2 items from 2 unregistered accounts, and referred 25 items from 18 non-media social accounts to the Nepal Police Cyber Bureau — [eKantipur, 31 Jul 2026](https://ekantipur.com/news/2026/07/31/en/press-council-directs-to-remove-90-materials-that-violate-journalistic-code-of-conduct-14-24.html)
- Freedom Forum's position: PCN is authorised to monitor registered media and seek clarification, but acting against social media pages/YouTube channels and its separate "procedural guide for monitoring social media platforms" exceed the Press Council Act 1992 — [Freedom Forum, "FF urges council to not go beyond its jurisdiction"](https://freedomforum.org.np/ff-urges-council-to-not-go-beyond-its-jurisdiction-while-monitoring-content-on-social-media); PCN has also circulated lists of 43 YouTube channels to Nepal Police — [Radio Nepal](https://radionepalonline.com/en/?p=391226)

**Electronic Transactions Act (ETA) Section 47**
- Section 47(1) covers electronic publication of material prohibited by existing law, "contrary to the public morality or decent behaviour", or spreading hatred/jealousy against groups by caste, tribe or community; penalty up to NPR 100,000 fine and/or up to 5 years' imprisonment; 47(2) escalates repeat offences by one and a half times — [Setopati](https://en.setopati.com/political/165095); [Lawalpine, "Electronic Transaction Act Nepal"](https://lawalpine.com/blog/electronic-transaction-act-nepal); one Himalayan Times item gives the fine as Rs 200,000 (conflict; verify against the Law Commission text) — [The Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/nepal/mcd-alerts-public-regarding-social-media-crimes)
- Article 19 and Media Action Nepal (UPR submission, Oct 2025) state Section 47 "criminalises the right to freedom of expression with vague provisions", has been used "to arbitrarily arrest and detain or otherwise penalise journalists and those who criticise the government", and call for its repeal — [Article 19 / Media Action Nepal UPR submission (PDF)](https://www.article19.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/UPR-Nepal-7.2025-A19_MAN.pdf)
- Cases: journalists Aishwarya Kunwar and Puskar Bhatt arrested in Kanchanpur, Feb 2024, investigated under Section 47 over social media posts on police misconduct — [CPJ, Feb 2024](https://cpj.org/2024/02/nepali-journalists-aishwarya-kunwar-puskar-bhatt-arrested-under-cybercrime-law/); Kathmandu District Court arrest warrant (11 Jun 2025) against Dil Bhusan Pathak over a YouTube report on a political family's business dealings; Patan High Court blocked arrest 16 Jun but police summoned him anyway; the same court ordered Nepal Khabar and Bizmandu to take down stories about the SEBON chair — [FORUM-ASIA](https://asianhrds.forum-asia.org/en/entity/btys6a5ndfe); [The Himalayan Times, IFJ/HRW condemnation](https://thehimalayantimes.com/nepal/ifj-hrw-condemn-latest-move-targeting-journalists-and-online-media-in-nepal); [IFJ SAMSN](https://samsn.ifj.org/nepal-critical-journalism-targeted-by-court-defamation-allegations/); April 2026 arrest warrant under cybercrime charges against Basudev Dhami (Kanchanpur) after a local official's complaint over critical reporting — [IFEX / Freedom Forum](https://ifex.org/nepal-freedom-forum-condemns-the-arrest-warrant-against-journalist-pathak/) and [FORUM-ASIA](https://asianhrds.forum-asia.org/en/entity/aeq2d4f2lck)
- Freedom Forum's Annual Press Freedom Report 2026 (May 2025-April 2026) notes continued legal action "under outdated laws, including Section 47", 8 journalists and 4 media houses facing lawsuits, and that mass media and social media are treated alike under content regulation — [Freedom Forum news 840](https://freedomforum.org.np/news/detail/840)

**Social Media Bill 2025 and Media Council Bill**
- The Social Media Bill (registered in the National Assembly, Jan 2025) proposed up to 5 years' imprisonment or Rs 500,000 fine for content disturbing national sovereignty/security; FNJ, IFJ, Nepal Press Union demanded withdrawal; Freedom Forum's Taranath Dahal said it violated constitutional fundamental freedoms — [The Himalayan Times, 31 Jan 2025](https://thehimalayantimes.com/environment/social-media-bill-draws-criticism-from-press-freedom-advocates); [IFJ SAMSN](https://samsn.ifj.org/?p=9553); [CIVICUS Monitor](https://monitor.civicus.org/explore/nepal-attempts-tighten-its-grip-journalists-and-social-media-new-bills/)
- CPJ joined 20+ groups on 5 Mar 2025 urging revision of the bill ("overreaching powers", "overbroad and vague provisions") and of the newly established Media Council — [CPJ, 7 Mar 2025](https://cpj.org/2025/03/cpj-joins-call-for-nepal-to-revise-new-media-council-social-media-bill/)
- Article 19/MAN: the Media Council Bill (2024) would replace PCN with a body whose appointments are controlled by the Ministry of Communication and IT, "undermining the independence and impartiality of the Council" — [Article 19 UPR PDF](https://www.article19.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/UPR-Nepal-7.2025-A19_MAN.pdf)
- Platform-block episode: the government blocked 26 unregistered platforms (Facebook, YouTube, X, etc.) on 4-8 Sept 2025 after an 25 Aug Cabinet registration directive; the block was lifted 9 Sept — [TechCrunch, 4 Sep 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/04/nepal-blocks-facebook-instagram-youtube-x-over-rule-breach-amid-censorship-concerns); [CPJ, 10 Sep 2025](https://cpj.org/2025/09/nepal-protests-media-outlets-torched-and-journalists-injured-in-deadly-unrest/)
- Withdrawal: the interim (Sushila Karki) cabinet decided on 6 Feb 2026 to withdraw the Social Media Bill; the National Assembly unanimously endorsed withdrawal on 9 Feb 2026 — [Kathmandu Post, 7 Feb 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/02/07/government-moves-to-withdraw-social-media-bill); [Himal Press](https://en.himalpress.com/?p=81316); [Hivos EU SEE](https://eusee.hivos.org/?p=4625)

**Defamation and contempt**
- Sidhakura.com (30 Sep 2024): Supreme Court extended full bench jailed publisher Yubraj Kandel and executive editor Nabin Dhungana for 3 months for contempt (reducible to 7 days on apology, corrigendum and undertaking), because they "published it repeatedly without fulfilling a minimum responsibility of the media to verify the content's authenticity" even though they did not create the audio; the audio's supplier got 6 months and Rs 5,000 fine — [Asia News Network / Kathmandu Post](https://asianews.network/?p=168780)
- Rajdhani Daily former editor Saroj Mishra sentenced by Kathmandu District Court to 4 months' imprisonment and Rs 1,000 fine for criminal defamation over a 2023 report that ran in print and online (compensation reported as Rs 500,000 total by Kathmandu Post, Rs 250,000 by eKantipur); media groups called it a blow to press freedom — [Kathmandu Post, 1 Jul 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/07/01/former-editor-sentenced-for-defamation-as-media-groups-decry-ruling-as-blow-to-press-freedom); [eKantipur, 1 Jul 2026](https://ekantipur.com/news/2026/07/01/en/editor-sentenced-to-four-months-in-prison-for-defamation-08-34.html)
- Court-ordered takedowns: Kathmandu District Court interim order (June 2025) requiring Nepal Khabar and Bizmandu to remove articles about the SEBON chair — [IFJ SAMSN](https://samsn.ifj.org/nepal-critical-journalism-targeted-by-court-defamation-allegations/); [Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/world/nepal-court-orders-media-to-delete-published-news-raising-concern-over-press-freedom/amp)

**Privacy law relevant to naming/photographing people**
- National Penal Code 2074, Section 295: taking or distorting a person's photograph without consent, up to 2 years' imprisonment or Rs 20,000 fine or both; Section 296: giving, selling or publishing a person's photo without consent to annoy/harass or for undue/commercial benefit, up to 3 years or Rs 30,000 or both — [nepallaws.com, Section 295](https://nepallaws.com/Laws/the-national-penal-code-act-2074/part-3-offences-against-individual-privacy-and-prestige/chapter-1-offences-against-privacy/section-295-prohibition-of-taking-or-disfiguring-photograph-of-any-person-without-his-or-her-consent/); [Section 296](https://nepallaws.com/Laws/the-national-penal-code-act-2074/part-3-offences-against-individual-privacy-and-prestige/chapter-1-offences-against-privacy/section-296-prohibition-of-giving-or-selling-ones-photograph-to-another-without-consent/)
- Individual Privacy Act, 2018 (2075) protects private matters (biometric identity, gender identity, sexuality, illness) and bars publication that harms or insults personal life except with consent; consent is required when collecting personal/family data — [Pioneer Law, Individual Privacy Act 2018 (2075)](https://pioneerlaw.com/individual-privacy-act-2018-2075); English text [ADB Law and Policy Reform](https://lpr.adb.org/sites/default/files/resource/%5Bnid%5D/nepal-the-privacy-act-2075-2018-english.pdf). No journalism carve-out was found in any source reviewed.
- PCN's Code of Journalistic Ethics 2003 (amended 2008), Clause 6 "Don't penalise the victims" and Clause 7 "Non disclosure" bar revealing identity of sexual-crime victims without consent; PCN chair Borna Bahadur Karki said in March 2016 that even a dead victim's identity and photo should not be revealed — [The Himalayan Times, 25 Mar 2016](https://thehimalayantimes.com/ampArticle/152187)

**Right to Information (RTI) Act 2064 (2007)**
- Constitution Article 27 and RTI Act Clauses 3 and 7(1) are the basis journalists use; Freedom Forum sent RTI requests to all 753 local levels, only 54 furnished information, and complaints were registered against 699 information officers — [Republica, "Complaints filed against 699 officials not providing information"](https://myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/complaints-filed-against-699-officials-not-providing-information)
- In a 2024 case, a Jhapa activist seeking municipal audit reports by email was abused by the chief administrative officer of Shivasatakshi Municipality — [Freedom Forum news 647](https://freedomforum.org.np/news/detail/647)

**Press accreditation ("press pass")**
- Under the 2017 Directive, online journalists must obtain a press accreditation card and non-renewed outlets' staff do not get press credentials — [The Himalayan Times 2017](https://thehimalayantimes.com/kathmandu/online-media-operation-code-issued/); [CMR-Nepal](https://research.butmedia.org/?p=861)
- Press identity card issuance/renewal was devolved to district post offices in Dec 2020 — [Radio Nepal (Nepali), 10 Dec 2020](https://onlineradionepal.gov.np/2020/12/10/119607.html); a 2026 op-ed reports that for freelancers the Department of Information requires a bachelor's degree in journalism, a 300-word essay and 60 published works over 10 consecutive years (secondhand account; verify) — [The Rising Nepal, 28 Jul 2026](https://risingnepaldaily.com/news/79306)
- Foreign journalists covering the Bhote Koshi flood (Aug-Sep 2026) were required to obtain DoIB accreditation via its online service — [Journalism Pakistan](https://www.journalismpakistan.com/nepal-tightens-press-pass-rules-for-foreign-journalists); [New Kerala](https://www.newkerala.com/news/a/nepal-makes-press-accreditation-mandatory-foreign-journalists-covering-922.htm)

### Inferences
- A volunteer civic-information site that publishes news-like content (even curated or crowd-sourced) risks being treated as "online journalism" and therefore as an unregistered outlet; PCN's documented practice is to order unlisted outlets to remove content and refer non-media accounts to the Cyber Bureau. Registration (Rs 5,000 + Rs 2,500/yr) plus the display obligations (registration number, addresses, editor/journalist names, privacy policy, public corrections) is the lower-risk path, but registration brings the Working Journalists Act minimum-wage and bank-salary commitments, which a volunteer operation cannot meet.
- The Sidhakura contempt ruling makes clear that republishing third-party material without verification is treated as the publisher's own act; AHN Legal's reading of the Directive also makes the operator responsible for user comments. Moderation and verification are legal exposure management, not just ethics.
- ETA Section 47 is the operative risk for a site with user-generated content; prosecutions have followed complaints from local officials, and courts have ordered takedowns via interim orders within a day.

## Key Question 2: Ethics for disaster and death coverage (PCN guidelines, missing-persons lists, photos of the dead, consent, phone numbers, child protection)

### Takeaway
PCN's "Editorial Guidelines for Humane and Responsible Disaster Reporting, 2083" (4 Sept 2026) is the most specific Nepali standard: verify deaths/injuries/missing/damage with official or reliable sources before publishing; verify social-media material on source, location, time, context and independent evidence; do not show mangled bodies, faces of the dead, grieving families or children; no gruesome thumbnails/autoplay previews; label AI symbolic images; seek informed consent; correct errors immediately with an explanation. The Child-Friendly Communication Directive 2073 and UN Nepal's September 2026 statement add parental consent for under-18s and verification of missing-persons photos before publication. No Nepal Red Cross or NDRRMA media guidance on missing-persons lists was found.

### Cited Findings
- PCN "Editorial Guidelines for Humane and Responsible Disaster Reporting, 2083" (विपद्का बेला सकारात्मक, मानवीय तथा जिम्मेवार सञ्चारसम्बन्धी सम्पादकीय निर्देशिका, २०८३), issued Bhadra 19, 2083 (4 Sep 2026): deaths, injuries, missing persons, damage, risk zones and rescues to be disseminated only after confirmation from official or other reliable sources ("आधिकारिक वा अन्य विश्वसनीय स्रोतबाट पुष्टि भएपछि मात्र सम्प्रेषण गर्नुपर्नेछ"); before republishing social-media material verify source, location, time, context and independent evidence, with special caution for old or out-of-place footage, edits, AI visuals, unconfirmed death/missing counts and fake relief or government notices; generally avoid mangled bodies, visible body parts, bloody images, faces of the dead, close-ups of the seriously injured, private scenes of grieving families or children; if sensitive visuals are necessary, conceal faces/identities, limit what is shown, give advance warning; no gruesome visuals in thumbnails, covers or autoplay previews on websites, Facebook, YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, X; AI-generated symbolic images must not look real and must be labelled "एआईद्वारा सिर्जित सांकेतिक दृश्य"; identify yourself and purpose, seek informed consent, do not pressure or repeatedly question distressed people; avoid close-ups of crying children and personal details that could endanger them; report safe locations, weather/early warnings, hospitals, relief centres, emergency contacts; do not obstruct rescue; correct wrong information immediately, explain what was wrong and apologise if necessary. The article describes these as directions and requests and does not state penalties — [eKantipur (Nepali), 4 Sep 2026](https://ekantipur.com/news/2026/09/04/clear-disclosure-is-required-when-using-ai-generated-symbolic-imagery-04-46.html); English summary [eKantipur](https://ekantipur.com/news/2026/09/04/en/clear-disclosure-is-required-when-using-ai-generated-symbolic-imagery-04-46.html)
- Bhotekoshi flood (26 Aug 2026): PCN found photos/videos undermining the dignity and privacy of flood-affected children (portraying helplessness, revealing identities), citing the Child-Friendly Communication Directive, 2073 BS, and directed removals and warnings; Communications Minister Bikram Timilsina told media to coordinate before entering holding centres, not publish identifying details of children/vulnerable people without consent, rely on official sources (Home Ministry, district administrations, security agencies, local disaster committees), avoid AI-generated/unverified content, and not repost distressing images or personal details of the dead — [Republica](https://myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/minister-timilsina-urges-media-to-protect-dignity-privacy-of-bhotekoshi-flo-12-87.html)
- PCN told 72 social media users to remove inappropriate content over four days after the Bhotekoshi flood; its complaints portal had 101 complaints; 67 personal accounts were reported for gruesome scenes; PCN chair Umesh Shrestha said mainstream media had largely avoided misleading information and excessive graphic visuals — [Kathmandu Post, 31 Aug 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/08/31/in-disaster-aftermath-nepal-battles-a-flood-of-misinformation)
- The Cyber Bureau formed a 10-member team to investigate users posting abusive/hateful comments about Bhotekoshi flood survivors, compiling profiles even without formal complaints, after a warning from PM Balendra Shah — [Kathmandu Post, 7 Sep 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/09/07/nepal-moves-against-online-abuse-targeting-flood-victims)
- UN in Nepal statement (reported 9 Sep 2026) on Rasuwa flood coverage: missing-persons photos and information can help reconnect families but must be verified before publication and used mainly for humanitarian purposes; particular caution with images of the deceased, injured and grieving; weigh graphic images against public interest, dignity, privacy and distress; informed consent before collecting/sharing personal stories, photos or video; parental consent for children under 18 — [The Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/ampArticle/1040196)
- Official practice on missing persons: Nepal Police published the missing list and a QR-linked gallery of unidentified bodies on its Facebook page; families also pinned photos on a "wall of the missing" at TU Teaching Hospital — [The Star (Bernama)](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/08/28/nepal-floods-police-release-names-of-missing-malaysians); [The Star, 1 Sep 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/09/01/nepal039s-wall-of-missing-offers-last-hope-after-himalayan-flood)
- Earlier precedent: after the Trishuli bus accident PCN sought clarification from eight online portals for gruesome photographs of the deceased, citing clause 4(9) of the Journalist Code of Conduct 2060 (amended 2064), and asked them to remove the pictures under Clause 5 — [Republica](https://www.myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/index.php/news/pcn-seeks-clarification-from-8-news-portals-for-publishing-gruesome-photographs)
- PCN "taking action against misleading and provocative content" generally — [Annapurna Express](https://theannapurnaexpress.com/story/66838/)

### Inferences
- For a civic site that hosts missing-persons or need lists, the PCN 2083 guidelines plus Penal Code s.295-296 imply: publish names/photos of missing people only with family consent and after verification, never publish faces of the dead, pull listings once resolved (fact-checkers flagged "trapped person" footage still circulating after rescue), and keep children's identifying details off the public page.
- Enforcement has moved from media-only to social media users (72 takedown orders, Cyber Bureau profiling), so user-generated posts on a civic site are within the regulators' practical reach even if the legal basis is contested.

### Gaps
- No Nepal Red Cross Society or NDRRMA document on media handling of missing-persons lists, dead bodies or survivors' phone numbers was found; the UN statement and police practice are the only Nepal-specific references. The PCN 2083 guidelines PDF itself was not retrieved (only eKantipur's summary).
- No Nepali source addresses publishing phone numbers of survivors or helpers.

## Key Question 3: Economic pressures (revenue scarcity, government welfare ads, delayed pay, paid news, Working Journalists Act, volunteer burnout)

### Takeaway
Nepali newsrooms, including online ones, are in a documented pay crisis: in April 2026 journalists at Avenues TV, Ukaalo, Nagarik, Kantipur, Rajdhani, Annapurna and others reported arrears of 2-12 months, FNJ professional-insecurity complaints rose from 11 (2023) to 18 (2025), and the government in April 2026 routed all official advertising exclusively to state media. The Working Journalists Act minimum wage (last confirmed figure NPR 24,600/month; a 2023 proposal to raise it to NPR 34,125 was not confirmed as implemented) is widely unenforced; public-welfare ad eligibility now requires listing and renewal with the Advertisement Board.

### Cited Findings
- April 2026 survey of 27 journalists: Avenues TV staff up to 11 months unpaid; Ukaalo unpaid since December with 10+ newsroom resignations; some Nagarik journalists six months without regular pay; Kantipur dailies missed Falgun salaries for half a dozen journalists; Rajdhani five months; Annapurna Post/Express some owed 12 months; Prime TV three months; FNJ registered at least 18 professional-insecurity complaints in 2025 vs 11 two years earlier; Working Journalists' Union president Janmadev Jaisi: "If we enforce the Working Journalists Act, many of these problems would be resolved" and complaints to the Press Registrar are redirected to the Labour Office/Labour Court; KMG has ~650 staff (238 newsroom), revenue down 70% over a decade, dozens laid off with packages; Setopati and Baahrakhari described as reliable payers, Onlinekhabar had a ~50-day gap since cleared; on 2 Apr 2026 the PM's Office directed all official advertising exclusively to Gorkhapatra, Radio Nepal and NTV — [Kathmandu Post, 15 Apr 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2026/04/15/nepali-media-is-fighting-for-survival-its-journalists-are-fighting-for-their-paychecks)
- Working Journalists Act minimum wage: NPR 24,600/month (about USD 184) as of May 2023; the Minimum Wages Fixation Committee recommended NPR 34,125 (40% rise); the Act had not been amended since 2009; 56 media rights violations recorded May 2022-April 2023 — [IFJ, 25 May 2023](https://www.ifj.org/media-centre/news/detail/category/press-releases/article/nepal-minister-to-raise-journalist-minimum-wage-by-40-per-cent); [Republica](https://myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/fnj-recommends-to-govt-to-set-minimum-salary-of-journalists-at-rs-34-125). Earlier, a 2018 increase from Rs 19,500 to Rs 24,375 was stayed by the Supreme Court on 5 Feb 2019 — [Onlinekhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/supreme-court-stays-implementation-of-increased-minimum-wage-of-journalists.html)
- Minister Rekha Sharma said media houses not paying the set salary could lose public welfare advertising; FNJ reported ~700 wage complaints over three years; 38% of working journalists lack an appointment letter — [The Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/ampArticle/171602); [IFJ, FNJ protests non-payment](https://ifj.org/media-centre/news/detail/article/nepal-fnj-protests-non-payment-of-wages-and-illegal-dismissals)
- Advertisement Board: media and ad agencies must be listed under Section 26(1) of the Advertisement (Regulation) Act 2076 as amended by the Some Nepal Laws (Amendment) Act 2082; outlets not listed and renewed will not be paid for public welfare advertisements from FY 2083/84; digital media are graded by "number of viewers and readers" and must submit yearly rates — [Ratopati](https://english.ratopati.com/story/77190/newspapers-that-do-not-list-and-renew-their-advertising-on-the-advertisement-board-will-not-receive-public-welfare-advertising-funds); [Pradhan Law, Amendment to Advertisement Regulations 2080](https://pradhanlaw.com/publications/amendment-to-advertisement-regulations-2080-bs-2023-ad); [Pioneer Law, Advertisement (Regulation) Act 2019](https://pioneerlaw.com/resource/advertisement-regulation-act-2019/); the Board's mandate is to distribute government public-interest ads "in a fair and proportional manner based on approved criteria" — [ShareSansar, Jul 2026](https://www.sharesansar.com/index.php/newsdetail/government-invites-applications-for-chairperson-of-advertisement-board-2026-07-15)
- Pandemic-era baseline: online portals saw up to 60% fall in ad revenue, Kantipur daily shrank from 24-32 to 8 pages, KMG cut about a third of 1,200+ jobs — [Dhaka Tribune/DW](https://www.dhakatribune.com/world/asia/340643/press-in-crisis-in-nepal-as-media-revenue-dips); FNJ said over 10% of journalists faced layoffs/underpayment after the pandemic — [Record Nepal](https://www.recordnepal.com/exploitation-pay-cuts-and-layoffs-the-fate-of-journalists)
- Ad-money leverage and conflicts: CIAA found a ~Rs 9.5 lakh public-service-ad payment to a newly appointed Press Council member's magazine against the law and criteria — [Onlinekhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/appointment-dipak-pathak-press-council-member-law-ciaa-tells-nepal-government.html); PCN chair Bal Krishna Basnet warned outlets to watch for sponsored content from inside Nepal or abroad — [The Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/nepal/stay-alert-for-fake-news-press-council-nepal-chief)
- Local outlets: Radio New Nepal 98.2 (Aathbiskot) journalists unpaid for about two years, Rs 1 million owed — [INSEC Online](https://inseconline.org/?p=15840); Doti journalists assaulted by local representatives while also collecting unpaid municipal ad/information payments — [The Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/ampArticle/152182)
- Community radio (2025 study, 7 key informants incl. ACORAB): declining number of active stations, widespread dormancy, staff shortages, weak governance, financial instability, advertising shifted online, donor support fallen, government engagement "inconsistent and politicized" — [Pokharel, PCN Journal Vol 5 (2025)](https://www.nepjol.info/index.php/nmies/article/view/86004); an older (2008) study found 45% of community-radio personnel were volunteers — [Hussain, Global Media Journal AU](https://www.hca.westernsydney.edu.au/gmjau/archive/iss2_2008/Faheem_Hussain.html)
- Freedom Forum 2026 report lists "economic crisis in media, worsened by post-COVID challenges and new government advertisement rules" as a structural threat — [Freedom Forum news 840](https://freedomforum.org.np/news/detail/840)

### Inferences
- A registered online outlet is legally expected to pay an editor and three staff at least the Working Journalists Act minimum through a bank; the sector's open non-compliance means enforcement is weak, but the obligation becomes a lever (ad eligibility, renewal) if regulators choose to use it.
- Government advertising is both the main subsidy and the main pressure point; the April 2026 state-media-only directive removed it for private outlets entirely, which pushes small portals toward sponsored content and raises "paid news" risk.
- Volunteer burnout in community media is documented via dormancy and staff shortages rather than surveys; there is no Nepal-specific burnout study.

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 figure for total government ad spending or online media ad revenue was found.
- The current legally binding minimum wage figure (post-2023) could not be confirmed; no gazette notice located.
- No Nepal-specific "paid news" study or Election Commission action for the 2022 elections was found.

## Key Question 4: Editorial practice (speed vs verification, copy-paste, attribution, corrections, recurring misinformation patterns)

### Takeaway
Fact-checkers (NepalFactCheck.org/CMR-Nepal, Nepal Check, with Indian partners BOOM, Newschecker, Factly) describe the same recurring patterns across the Sept 2025 Gen Z protests and the Aug-Sep 2026 Bhotekoshi/Rasuwa floods: foreign or old footage misattributed to Nepal, exaggerated death/missing counts, miscontextualised security activity, religious/political falsehoods, and increasingly AI-generated video; even national outlets (National News Agency, Dudhauli FM) republished false items. Copy-paste without attribution is acknowledged as widespread; PCN's remedy sequence is rectify-and-republish, clarification notice, removal, blacklisting.

### Cited Findings
- CMR-Nepal/NepalFactCheck.org report on the Sept 2025 Gen Z uprising (Ujjwal Acharya, with Umesh Shrestha, Chetana Kunwar, Pravin Bhatta): five misinformation categories: (1) geographic misattribution of foreign videos (Maldives, Sikkim, Karnataka); (2) exaggerated violence/casualty claims (Bhatbhateni skeleton claim, "32 dead in Parliament" claim); (3) miscontextualised military/security activity (army vehicles, "coup"); (4) religious/cultural misinformation (Pashupatinath vandalism video, Hindu-nation narrative); (5) false political/leadership claims (ex-PM's wife's death, Balen Shah as PM). Misinformation also spread in closed groups "even those including opinion leaders"; Indian political misinformation spilled into Nepal; NepalFactCheck.org "with limited resources... it was impossible to fact-check all potential misinformation" so it prioritised malinformation likely to incite violence. A false report that the ex-PM's wife had died was carried by "National News Agency, several Indian media outlets and a popular" page. Recommendations for media/fact-checkers: scale capacity in crises, distribute debunks across platforms/formats, focus on malinformation, build partnerships, establish direct channels with platforms' trust and safety teams; for platforms: add friction to viral sharing, flag out-of-context and old media via metadata — [CMR-Nepal, "Misinformation during the Gen Z Uprising in Nepal" (PDF)](https://research.butmedia.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/GenZ_Protest_Misinformation_Report.pdf)
- Floods Aug-Sep 2026: Dudhauli FM's Facebook page posted Uttarakhand rescue footage as Home Minister Sudan Gurung in a tunnel rescue, later editing the caption; an X video with 7m+ views was rated 97.2% likely AI-generated (Hive Moderation) and debunked by BBC Verify; a "GLOF in Rasuwa" clip was from Columbia Glacier, Alaska; "before and after" settlement photos were fabricated (AAP); old footage of a trapped person kept circulating after rescue; graphic body images posted from personal accounts without warnings; a woman pleading for her missing husband was abused online. Chetana Kunwar (Nepal Fact Check): "They tend to believe whatever information reaches them first"; Deepak Adhikari (Nepal Check): "The primary focus should be rescue and relief" — [Kathmandu Post, 31 Aug 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/08/31/in-disaster-aftermath-nepal-battles-a-flood-of-misinformation)
- Cross-border fact-checks of the same flood: BOOM traced clips to Uttarkashi (Aug 2025); The Quint to a 2021 Japan landslide and a 2025 Russian mudslide; Newschecker Nepal traced a bridge video to Puerto Rico; Thai PBS recommends reverse-searching keyframes — [BOOM](https://www.boomlive.in/fact-check/viral-nepal-flash-flood-videos-claim-fact-check-old-unrelated-videos-32124); [The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/news/webqoof/old-unrelated-visuals-falsely-shared-as-clips-of-nepal-floods-false-claim-fact-check); [Newschecker Nepal](https://nepal.newschecker.co/en/english/fact-check-old-unrelated-clips-falsely-shared-as-devastation-caused-by-recent-floods-in-nepal); [Thai PBS Verify](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/verify/en/content/16860)
- Nepal protest footage was later recycled abroad (as a Chhattisgarh student protest, June 2026) — [Fact Crescendo](https://english.factcrescendo.com/2026/06/04/gen-z-protest-video-from-nepal-shared-as-students-protest-in-chhattisgarh/)
- Copy-paste culture: a Kantipur editor described plagiarism as "widespread in the Nepali media" — [iMediaEthics](https://www.imediaethics.org/nepali-editor-plagiarism-quotwidespread-in-the-nepali-mediaquot/); PCN asked NTA to block 15 unregistered portals that republished News Agency Nepal content (April 2023) — [IFEX](https://ifex.org/?p=340846); PCN staff say the first step against a guilty portal is to ask it to rectify and republish — [Annapurna Express, "All issues posted in social media are not news"](https://theannapurnaexpress.com/story/45684/)
- Correction norms now written into regulation: the 2025 Press and Publication Rules require errors to be corrected "promptly and publicly" — [eKantipur, 9 Nov 2025](https://ekantipur.com/news/2025/11/09/online-media-registration-fees-regulating-or-curbing-freedom-15-27.html); PCN's 2083 disaster guidelines require immediate correction with an explanation of what was wrong and an apology if necessary — [eKantipur, 4 Sep 2026](https://ekantipur.com/news/2026/09/04/clear-disclosure-is-required-when-using-ai-generated-symbolic-imagery-04-46.html); PCN's complaint remedies in H1 FY2082/83 were mainly rebuttals (77) and corrections/apologies (14) — [eKantipur, 22 Jan 2026](https://ekantipur.com/news/2026/01/22/en/163-complaints-registered-with-press-council-in-6-months-48-41.html)
- Sidhakura (2024) establishes that repeated republication of unverified audio is itself punishable (see Q1) — [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/?p=168780)
- Audience trust context (Sharecast 2022, n=5,582): only 3% trust social media content and 8% trust online portals; 64%+ rely on neighbours/friends/family for local news; Facebook as primary local-news source rose from 21% to 41% (2020-2022) — [Nepali Times, "What do Nepalis think of the media"](https://nepalitimes.com/what-do-nepalis-think-of-the-media); [Nepali Times, "Nepal's interface with information"](https://nepalitimes.com/nepal-s-interface-with-information)

### Inferences
- For a civic site, the practical verification minimum matches the PCN 2083 five-point check (source, location, time, context, independent evidence) plus reverse image search; the recurring failure mode in Nepal is not fabrication by newsrooms but republishing viral material.
- A visible, dated corrections log and apology practice is now both a regulatory expectation and the PCN's preferred remedy; sites without it fall straight to removal orders.

### Gaps
- No Nepal-specific study of correction/retraction practices at online portals or of attribution norms (beyond anecdote) was found.
- South Asia Check and AFP Fact Check Nepal outputs were not surfaced by searches; their pattern descriptions are not captured here.
- Election-period misinformation patterns (2022 local/federal polls) were not retrieved.

## Key Question 5: Local and community reporting (district correspondents, citizen journalism, palika news gaps, pressure on local reporters)

### Takeaway
Local reporting in Nepal depends on under-paid or unpaid district correspondents and community radios, with Freedom Forum's 2026 report identifying political leaders, cadres and public officials as the main hostile actors and government/corruption coverage as the most-targeted beats (35 and 31 cases). Local governments are poor at information management: only 54 of 753 local levels answered Freedom Forum's RTI requests, and local coverage is largely event-based and official-quote driven.

### Cited Findings
- Freedom Forum Annual Press Freedom Report 2026 (May 2025-Apr 2026): 97 violations, 145 media persons and 20 media houses affected; perpetrators mainly political leaders, their cadres and public officials; 35 cases of intimidation tied to government coverage, 31 threats tied to corruption reporting in public offices; Bagmati 40 incidents, Madhesh 21; more than half of affected journalists worked for online portals — [Freedom Forum news 840](https://freedomforum.org.np/news/detail/840)
- Local-official pressure examples: Dhangadhimai (Siraha) municipal chief threatened a Prime Television correspondent after a story on illegal fee collection (2024) — [FORUM-ASIA](https://asianhrds.forum-asia.org/en/entity/07via2tkk514); three journalists assaulted by elected representatives in Purbichauki rural municipality (Doti) while covering services and collecting unpaid municipal ad payments — [The Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/ampArticle/152182)
- Freedom Forum Media Mission Report 2018 (Jhapa/Ilam): journalists find it hard to get information about government affairs, newly created local levels manage information poorly and are unaware of RTI; a few reporters used RTI to expose local corruption — [Freedom Forum, Media Mission Report 2018 (PDF)](https://freedomforum.org.np/storage/wp-content/uploads/download-manager-files/Media%20Mission%20Report%202018.pdf)
- Only 54 of 753 local levels furnished information to Freedom Forum RTI requests; complaints against 699 information officers — [Republica](https://myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/complaints-filed-against-699-officials-not-providing-information)
- Pokhara study (2026): coverage of local governance by two Pokhara dailies is largely event-based or reliant on official remarks; local media need capacity building, professional autonomy and institutional collaboration — [INTELLIGENCE journal via NepJOL](https://www.nepjol.info/index.php/ijmr/article/download/94457/71612/277278)
- District correspondents (older, 2013, 20+ journalists from 14 districts): publishers delay salaries for months, reporters told to sell ads or collect debts, partial contracts across several outlets to avoid the minimum wage, threats from timber smugglers/criminal networks — [Nepali Times, "Periphery of news"](https://nepalitimes.com/periphery-of-news)
- Community radio: declining active stations, dormancy, staff shortages, politicised government engagement (2025) — [Pokharel, PCN Journal](https://www.nepjol.info/index.php/nmies/article/view/86004); community journalists' local news is among the most popular content and 45% of personnel were volunteers (2008) — [Hussain 2008](https://www.hca.westernsydney.edu.au/gmjau/archive/iss2_2008/Faheem_Hussain.html)
- Journalists outside Kathmandu during the Sept 2025 protests: Kalika FM, Chure Sandesh weekly and other offices in Chitwan vandalised/torched; Kapurbot.com journalist attacked in Bara; some district papers stopped printing citing threats while online and FM continued; Karnali Province recorded no attacks — [Freedom Forum, "Nepali media braves hostility" preliminary report (PDF)](https://freedomforum.org.np/download/nepali-media-braves-hostility-preliminary-report/file)

### Inferences
- Local correspondents are the most exposed and least paid part of the system; a civic site relying on them (or on citizen reporters) inherits their safety exposure to local officials, and must assume local governments will not readily supply verifiable information.
- Online journalists being over half of victims suggests portals, not legacy media, are now where local accountability reporting (and its backlash) occurs.

### Gaps
- No systematic 2022-2026 survey of district correspondent pay for Kantipur/Setopati stringers was found.
- No sources surfaced on CIJ Nepal, Ujyaalo or ACORAB citizen-journalism programmes in 2022-2026, nor a count of local governments without any dedicated coverage.

## Key Question 6: Technical and operational (Unicode/fonts, Devanagari SEO, hosting, hacking, platform dependence, comments and hate-speech moderation)

### Takeaway
The documented technical burdens are: legacy Preeti-encoded Nepali text that search engines index as gibberish (fix: Unicode + Noto Sans Devanagari/Mukta/Hind), CMS breaches that deleted and replaced investigative stories (Ukeraa, Dec 2024), structural dependence on Facebook for traffic (88% of surveyed Nepalis use Facebook; publishers "did not think about establishing themselves as a destination"), and a regulatory expectation that the operator is responsible for user comments. No Nepal-specific reporting on DDoS against news portals, YouTube copyright strikes, Facebook reach throttling or Nepali-language hate-speech moderation tooling was found.

### Cited Findings
- Preeti/legacy fonts store Latin ASCII that only renders as Devanagari with the font installed, so search engines and screen readers see gibberish (e.g., "Nepal" stored as "g]kfn"); remedy is Unicode conversion (with human checking for broken matras/reph) and Unicode webfonts such as Noto Sans Devanagari, Mukta, Hind — [WebsNP, "Nepali Unicode and Devanagari Typography on the Web"](https://www.websnp.com/help/nepali-unicode-fonts-website-typography); [Lamisema docs, "The Nepali PDF Encoding Problem"](https://lamisema.readthedocs.io/en/latest/encoding-types/); [Merokalam, Preeti to Unicode guide 2026](https://merokalam.com/blog/convert-preeti-documents-to-unicode-guide/); historical note that reading Kantipur, Gorkhapatra and Himal online once required three separate fonts — [Nepali Times archive](https://archive.nepalitimes.com/news.php?id=4767)
- CMS breach: the content management system of online portal Ukeraa was breached on 1 December (2024); stories on a party coordinator's citizenship/passport and a VP's son's cooperative fraud case were deleted and replaced with unrelated content; the editor republished via archived links — [Freedom Forum news 692](https://www.freedomforum.org.np/news/detail/692)
- National cyber context: hundreds of .gov.np sites knocked offline by DDoS (Jan 2024); SQL injection and DDoS are the leading vectors against Nepali infrastructure; 2025 breaches of G-Cloud and Nepal Police site with data offered for sale — [The Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/nepal/hackers-obstruct-govt-sites-for-four-hours); [Kathmandu Post, 1 Jan 2024](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2024/01/01/government-s-main-server-faces-cyberattacks); [Onlinekhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/nepals-website-vulnerable-cyber-attack.html); [Ratopati](https://english.ratopati.com/story/83107/nepal-in-digital-insecurity-weak-defense-makes-it-a-target-for-hackers)
- Platform dependence: 88% of Sharecast respondents use Facebook; Nepali mainstream publishers grew alongside Facebook and "did not think about establishing themselves as a destination", relying on platform referrals, and the revenue model "has collapsed" — [Nepali Times, "Monopoly on information"](https://nepalitimes.com/monopoly-on-information); [Nepali Times, "New news"](https://nepalitimes.com/new-news); Statcounter put Facebook at 87% of Nepal's social media usage and YouTube at 5% when the Sept 2025 block hit — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/04/nepal-blocks-facebook-instagram-youtube-x-over-rule-breach-amid-censorship-concerns)
- Operational shocks: the 4-9 Sept 2025 block of 26 platforms cut distribution; CPJ said it "would seriously hinder journalists' work and people's access to news" — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/04/nepal-blocks-facebook-instagram-youtube-x-over-rule-breach-amid-censorship-concerns); during the 9 Sept arson the Kathmandu Post website was down and staff worked from home; Kantipur TV resumed on 12 Sept from a temporary studio in front of the burned building — [CPJ](https://cpj.org/2025/09/nepal-protests-media-outlets-torched-and-journalists-injured-in-deadly-unrest/); [Freedom Forum preliminary report](https://freedomforum.org.np/download/nepali-media-braves-hostility-preliminary-report/file)
- Comments: under the Online Media Operation Regulation 2017 (as summarised by AHN Legal) the media house is responsible for comments on its platform and must prevent vulgar, dishonest or irrelevant comments — [AHN Legal](https://www.ahnlegal.com/legal-updates/legal/online-media-regulation-in-nepal); ETA s.47 covers content spreading hatred against caste/tribe/community groups — [Setopati](https://en.setopati.com/political/165095); the Cyber Bureau's post-flood profiling of abusive commenters shows state moderation of user speech — [Kathmandu Post, 7 Sep 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/09/07/nepal-moves-against-online-abuse-targeting-flood-victims)
- PCN's 2083 guidelines specifically prohibit gruesome visuals in thumbnails, covers and autoplay previews on websites and on Facebook, YouTube, TikTok, Instagram and X — [eKantipur, 4 Sep 2026](https://ekantipur.com/news/2026/09/04/clear-disclosure-is-required-when-using-ai-generated-symbolic-imagery-04-46.html)
- CMR-Nepal recommends media build direct channels with platforms' trust and safety teams, and that platforms flag old/out-of-context media via metadata — [CMR-Nepal Gen Z report](https://research.butmedia.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/GenZ_Protest_Misinformation_Report.pdf)

### Inferences
- A civic site should treat the CMS and admin accounts as the attack surface (the Ukeraa case was content deletion/replacement, not DDoS) and keep archive/backups that allow republication.
- Because the operator is deemed responsible for comments, pre-moderation or disabling open comments is the lower-risk design; no Nepali-language automated hate-speech moderation tool was identified in sources, so moderation is manual.
- Dependence on Facebook is both a reach and a continuity risk (the 2025 block shows distribution can vanish overnight); own-domain SEO in Unicode Devanagari is the mitigation.

### Gaps
- No source on hosting/CDN costs for Nepali portals, on DDoS against a Nepali news site, on Facebook page takedowns/reach throttling of Nepali outlets, or on YouTube copyright strikes against Nepali channels.
- No source on how Setopati/Onlinekhabar or others moderate or disable comments, nor on Nepali-language hate-speech classifiers.

## Key Question 7: Safety (online harassment especially of women, doxxing, threats during the 2025 protests, Freedom Forum and FNJ reports)

### Takeaway
2025 was the worst year in five for Nepali press freedom (Freedom Forum: 114 violations, 139 media persons affected, 68 of them online journalists, 35 incidents during the Gen Z movement alone, Kantipur and Annapurna Post torched, four journalists hit by rubber bullets or gunfire). Online harassment is pervasive (about 7 in 10 of 100 surveyed journalists in 2022), gendered (doxxing, trolling, sexual-violence threats), and largely unaddressed: 29 women left journalism in 2023-2024 and most newsrooms have no complaint mechanism.

### Cited Findings
- Freedom Forum Annual Media Report 2025 (calendar 2025): 114 violations, highest in five years; 139 media persons affected (13% female); 68 associated with online media, the largest group; 22 media houses attacked, mostly arson/vandalism; 38 threats of physical attack or death; 23 misbehaviour/obstruction; 7 court cases; ~12 TV broadcasts disrupted; 38 media persons' vehicles/properties destroyed; 35 violations during the Gen Z movement with 22 media persons (three women) attacked; Avenues TV photojournalist Suresh Rajak died in a 28 Mar 2025 arson during a pro-monarchy protest; recommendations include insurance for journalists and equipment, digital/AI training, extension of social security — [Freedom Forum news 826](https://freedomforum.org.np/news/detail/826); PDF: [FF's Annual Media Report 2025](https://freedomforum.org.np/storage/uploads/publications/1767507820_FF's%20Annual%20Media%20Report%202025.pdf)
- Freedom Forum Annual Press Freedom Report 2026 (May 2025-Apr 2026): 97 violations (up from 68), 145 media persons (15% female) and 20 media houses; 28 threat cases targeting 37 persons; 21 attacks on 30 journalists; 8 journalists and 4 houses sued; online portal journalists "particularly vulnerable"; recommends investment in "digital and psychosocial resilience" — [Freedom Forum news 840](https://freedomforum.org.np/news/detail/840); [IFEX summary](https://ifex.org/freedom-forum-report-highlights-eroding-press-freedom-in-nepal/)
- Sept 2025 protests: 35 incidents affecting 17 media houses; protesters burnt Kantipur Television/publication house and Annapurna Post; NTV/Radio Nepal, Radio Kantipur, Annapurna Media Network and Thaha Sanchar attacked; News24 and Prime TV vehicles torched and devices seized; four journalists hit by rubber bullets on 8 Sept (Shyam Shrestha of Kantipur TV shot in the arm; Dipendra Dhungana of Naya Patrika hit below the ear, hospitalised until 15 Sept; Umesh Karki of Nepal Press; Shambhu Dangal shot in the leg); Barsha Shaha of Deshsanchar struck by stones; NTV's Arati Chataut abused and threatened; Indian journalists (NDTV, TV9, Republic, News18) manhandled; online teams worked from home — [Freedom Forum preliminary report (PDF)](https://freedomforum.org.np/download/nepali-media-braves-hostility-preliminary-report/file); [CPJ, 10 Sep 2025](https://cpj.org/2025/09/nepal-protests-media-outlets-torched-and-journalists-injured-in-deadly-unrest/); [IFEX](https://ifex.org/nepal-preliminary-report-on-media-status-during-gen-z-protest/)

### Inferences
- Online portals and their staff are now the primary targets; a civic-information site with named volunteers should expect doxxing/trolling of contributors (particularly women) and has no state mechanism to rely on, so internal safety protocols (pseudonymous bylines where lawful, no personal contact details, documented incident handling) are the only practical protection.
- Physical identification with an outlet became a liability during the 2025 protests (logo removal, torching); this argues against prominent branding of field volunteers during unrest.

### Gaps
- No quantitative breakdown of online harassment (doxxing vs trolling vs threats) for 2024-2026 exists in the sources; Freedom Forum counts do not separate online harassment as a category.
- No FNJ-authored report on 2025 violations was retrieved (only its statements via IFJ); the Freedom Forum data stands in for both.
