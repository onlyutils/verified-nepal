# How Nepalis seek, share and trust disaster, relief, missing-person and civic/mutual-aid information online (2015–2026)

Research date: 2026-10-14. Scope: Nepal only. Events covered: 2015 Gorkha earthquake, 2021 Melamchi flood, 2023 Jajarkot earthquake, Aug 2024 Thame GLOF, Sept 2024 monsoon floods, and the 26 Aug 2026 Bhote Koshi / Rasuwa glacier-collapse flood (the largest and best-documented recent case; ~1,400 dead, ~6,000 missing by mid-Sept 2026). A recurring methodological hazard: web searches for "Nepal flood 2024" are now dominated by 2026 material; every finding below is dated so the report writer can keep 2015 lessons, 2023–24 reality and 2026 reality apart.

---

## Key Question 1: Where did people turn for information during each disaster, what rumours spread, and how were they debunked?

### Takeaway
Across all events, the channel mix has shifted from FM radio + word of mouth (2015) to Facebook/TikTok/YouTube first with radio/TV used for *verification* (2022 onward); in 2026 the state's own channels (NDRRMA Facebook page, Nepal Police Facebook, DHM SMS, PMO website) became primary sources but their initial delay opened space for AI-generated and recycled footage, debunked largely by Nepal Fact Check, Newschecker Nepal, AAP, BBC Verify and BOOM rather than by government.

### Cited Findings

**Baseline media behaviour (what channels people actually use)**
- Nepal Media Survey 2022 (Sharecast Initiative, 5,582 face-to-face interviews in 77 districts): 90% of adults own a mobile phone, 63% have internet access, TV reaches 43.5%, radio 45.2%, newspapers 15%. Facebook as primary source of *local* news rose from 21% (2020) to 41% (2022); YouTube 4%→15%; radio fell 44%→26%; TV 47%→31%. — [Spotlight Nepal, 1 Aug 2026](https://www.spotlightnepal.com/2026/08/01/rethinking-formal-communications-in-nepals-digital-era-where-should-leaders-speak/); [Nepali Times, 15 Oct 2023](https://nepalitimes.com/surveying-nepal-s-digital-landscape)
- Same survey authors: audiences "still relied on legacy media, mainly radio and TV news bulletins, to verify information" and "radio listenership spikes during socio-political events and natural calamities." — [Nepali Times, 15 Oct 2023](https://nepalitimes.com/surveying-nepal-s-digital-landscape)
- Trust: roughly one-third of respondents reported high trust in TV, radio and print; only a small proportion reported similar trust in social media or online portals (year of the trust figure not stated in the secondary source). — [Spotlight Nepal, 1 Aug 2026](https://www.spotlightnepal.com/2026/08/01/rethinking-formal-communications-in-nepals-digital-era-where-should-leaders-speak/)
- Nepal has an estimated 13–14 million social media users (~45% of population); over 80% of Nepalis aged 15–29 use social media daily. — [Spotlight Nepal, 1 Aug 2026](https://www.spotlightnepal.com/2026/08/01/rethinking-formal-communications-in-nepals-digital-era-where-should-leaders-speak/)
- Pre-2015 baseline: Internews/Interdisciplinary Analysts ran a Nepal Media Survey in Sept 2014 on urban and rural communication habits. — [ALNAP listing](https://alnap.org/help-library/resources/nepal-media-survey-2014-provides-valuable-data-on-nepalis-communication-preferences/)

**2015 Gorkha earthquake**
- Internews needs assessments (May 2015) in Sindhupalchok and Nuwakot found "an absence of communication between humanitarian actors working on earthquake response and the local communities." — [Internews project page](https://internews.org/areas-of-expertise/humanitarian/projects/natural-disaster-projects/nepal-earthquake-response/)
- Open Mic Nepal (Internews + Accountability Lab + Local Interventions Group, launched July 2015) tracked and debunked rumours via a network of 400 journalists in the 14 most-affected districts and 18 community radio stations; volunteers texted in rumours heard during daily aid work, the team verified and geolocated them, and published bulletins. — [Internews project page](https://internews.org/areas-of-expertise/humanitarian/projects/natural-disaster-projects/nepal-earthquake-response/); [ALNAP record of final report (2016, 13 pp.)](https://alnap.org/help-library/resources/open-mic-nepal-tracking-rumors-in-post-earthquake-nepal/); [CommInit summary](https://reference.comminit.com/content/open-mic-nepal)
- Accountability Lab's Mobile Citizen Helpdesks: teams of 5–10 volunteers in the 14 worst-affected districts gathered feedback from victims on relief and "close[d] the loop." — [Accountability Lab](https://accountabilitylab.org/?p=21256)
- Rumours: a rumour that "NASA predicted a magnitude 8 quake" circulated; "most rumours pass by word of mouth, some on social networks"; hundreds of thousands slept outdoors nightly in fear of a bigger quake; a Kathmandu aid worker publicly asked people to stop spreading "magnitude 9" rumours. — [El Mundo CR, 2015](https://elmundo.cr/mundo/los-rumores-de-nuevos-terremotos-complican-la-situacion-en-nepal/); EMSC stated plainly "there is no earthquake prediction today, it does not exist." — [EMSC special report, Apr 2015](https://www.emsc-csem.org/Special_reports/?id=233)
- Seismologists on Twitter noted "earthquake rumors (e.g. the imminence of a larger event)" and argued false information must be accounted for in real-time science communication. — [Lomax, Bossu, Mazet-Roux, AGU 2015](https://agu.confex.com/agu/fm15/webprogram/Paper77222.html)
- Twitter research: 201,457 tweets analysed for heritage damage (Kumar 2020); hashtag spatiotemporal analysis (Thapa 2016). — [ISPRS 2016](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLI-B2/567/2016/); [UCL/JCCM](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10146772/)
- Connectivity: 99 of 460 mobile sites down in Kathmandu; Ncell lost 400 sites outside Kathmandu; coordination updates urged SMS over voice to avoid congestion; Facebook Safety Check activated; Viber and Skype made calls to/from Nepal free. — [ALNAP CwC update 1, 28 Apr 2015](https://library.alnap.org/system/files/content/resource/files/main/nepal-earthquake-response-cwc-update-1-28-04-2015.pdf); [NPR, 27 Apr 2015](https://www.npr.org/sections/alltechconsidered/2015/04/27/402641220/how-tech-firms-are-helping-people-in-the-nepal-earthquake-zone); [Global Voices, 26 Apr 2015](https://globalvoices.org/2015/04/26/in-a-state-of-emergency-after-a-massive-earthquake-how-to-be-safe-in-nepal/)
- Nepal Police set up a web-based missing-persons desk listing name, age, gender, last known address, with toll-free and email contacts (May 2015). — [Kathmandu Post, 10 May 2015](https://kathmandupost.com/miscellaneous/2015/05/10/nepal-police-install-missing-person-desk)

**2021 Melamchi flood (15 June 2021)**
- Radio Melamchi's manager: "We issued the warning as soon as a government official informed us about the flood upstream," credited with saving hundreds of lives — but "especially helpful in the town area, whose inhabitants had the means to access the warning." Police used loudspeakers. — [PreventionWeb / UNDRR, Roshan Sedhai](https://undrr.org/quick/66278)
- A resident: "We neither received any early warning nor had any idea how to respond to the disaster and seek safety." — [Nepali Times](https://nepalitimes.com/on-high-monsoon-alert-in-nepal)
- Affected areas lost communication services due to road and ICT disruption. — [ICIMOD record, 2021](https://lib.icimod.org/record/35284)
- Practical Action (an implementer, so advocacy): "many lives could have been saved in 2021 if we'd had an early warning system." — [Practical Action](https://practicalaction.org/?p=114829)

**2023 Jajarkot earthquake (3 Nov 2023)**
- Relief hampered by poor communications complicating coordination (DPNet Sitrep 05, 8 Nov 2023). — [DPNet](https://www.dpnet.org.np/public/uploads/files/Daily%20Situation%20Report%20-05%202023-11-08%2018-04-45.pdf)
- A report on the Nov 2023 quake said both government and private alert mechanisms "failed to reach a considerable number of people who experienced palpable shaking." — [Nepali Telecom](https://nepalitelecom.com/how-to-get-earthquake-alerts-on-phone-steps)
- Recycled-footage pattern: Newschecker Nepal found 2015 Sundhara CCTV footage recirculated as a "Dolakha quake" and 2015 Bhaktapur footage as the Oct 2023 Bajhang quake. — [Newschecker Nepal (Dolakha)](https://nepal.newschecker.co/en/english/no-earthquake-has-rocked-dolakha-recently-viral-is-from-2015); [Newschecker Nepal (Bajhang)](https://nepal.newschecker.co/en/english/fact-check-2015-footage-falsely-shared-as-the-moment-when-an-earthquake-hit-bajhang-district-on-tuesday)

**September 2024 monsoon floods (26–28 Sept 2024)**
- Heaviest rain in the region since at least 1970; Kathmandu 240 mm on 28 Sept, breaking a 54-year record; ≥226–240 dead. — [WMO, 20 Dec 2024](https://wmo.int/resources/meteoworld/meteoworld-december-2024/improved-flood-preparedness-and-community-based-early-warnings-needed-nepal); [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Nepal_floods)
- DHM issued an alert about a week before and flood advisories on the evening of 26 Sept (West Rapti and Babai in red for 28 Sept); Red Cross volunteers in Bardiya/Banke reached 3,600 people with evacuation prep. — [IFRC sEAP operation update](https://www.anticipation-hub.org/Documents/EAPs/sEAp_Nepal_Flood_operation_update.pdf)
- NDRRMA said flood warning messages were sent to nearly one million people and ~2,000 relocated (24–27 Sept). — [Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/ampArticle/1040457)
- WFP anticipatory action in Sunsari and Saptari: 111,940 people reached with early-warning messages, 23,550 with cash, triggered by a DHM bulletin on 28 Sept. — [WFP](https://docs.wfp.org/api/documents/WFP-0000166165/download)
- A Kathmandu (Sanepa) resident: "We didn't even get a warning. We didn't know who to reach out to … No one came, not even from the ward office." Earlier (Aug 2022) Nepal Telecom's 486,000 SMS alerts reached only areas already affected. — [Kathmandu Post column, 5 Aug 2025](https://kathmandupost.com/columns/2025/08/05/nepal-floods-a-preventable-disaster)
- WMO post-event review: "critical need for understandable, actionable impact-based early warnings that reach the most vulnerable communities." — [WMO, 20 Dec 2024](https://wmo.int/resources/meteoworld/meteoworld-december-2024/improved-flood-preparedness-and-community-based-early-warnings-needed-nepal)
- Misinformation: a TikTok clip of a building falling into a river was traced to Panauti (Roshi river, 28 Sept 2024) and later re-shared as "recent"; Dec 2024 "monsoon flooding" video was actually Puerto Rico hurricane / Japan tsunami footage. — [Newschecker Nepal](https://nepal.newschecker.co/en/english/old-image-of-a-building-collapsing-into-a-river-goes-viral-with-misleading-claim); [Newschecker Nepal, Dec 2024](https://nepal.newschecker.co/en?p=16147645)
- Oct 2025: DHM sent 50 lakh (5 million) flood/landslide SMS in one day (Ashoj 18, 2082) via Nepal Telecom and Ncell. — [Nepali Telecom, Oct 2025](https://www.nepalitelecom.com/2025/10/nepal-government-sent-50-lakh-flood-landslide-awareness-messages-on-a-single-day-on-ashoj-18-2082.html); Ncell has broadcast DHM flood SMS since 2016 (4.4 million in two months in one period). — [Technology Khabar](https://www.technologykhabar.com/?p=145375)

**26 Aug 2026 Bhote Koshi / Rasuwa glacier-collapse flood**
- Timeline: 8:37 collapse in Lhende valley; Dept of Mines and Geology recorded a 4.4 seismic signal but did not alert ("we did not alert the public like we usually do"); 8:50 Syabrubesi gauge swept away; 9:00 Rasuwa DAO informs DHM; 9:15 SMS alerts to Rasuwa, Nuwakot, Dhading, Chitwan advising higher ground and listing hotlines. Delivery: SMS, caller tune, media broadcasts, police loudspeakers downstream; door-to-door in Rasuwa where towers were destroyed. Ex-NDRRMA chief Anil Pokhrel: "There is no such thing as an early warning system in Nepal … It comes down to the subjective judgement of individuals in authority." — [Kathmandu Post, 2 Sept 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/09/02/nepal-sent-its-disaster-alert-38-minutes-after-the-glacier-collapse-it-wasn-t-enough)
- DHM Flood Forecasting Division: ~600,000 SMS sent between 9:15 and 9:16. — [PreventionWeb](https://www.preventionweb.net/news/nepals-devastating-floods-10-seconds-could-save-lives); Nepali Times: an alert sent after the flood has entered the country "is not quite the same thing as an early-warning system." — [Nepali Times](https://nepalitimes.com/the-danger-of-living-downstream-from-the-tibetan-plateau)
- Official information channels: NDRRMA Facebook page (facebook.com/NDRMMA) cited as the place "for detailed information"; PMO bulletins point to opmcm.gov.np and donate.gov.np; Nepal Police unidentified-bodies portal udb.nepalpolice.gov.np/disaster (bodies in 21 hospitals); MoFA emergency hotline/WhatsApp. — [b360nepal](https://www.b360nepal.com/detail/29976); [MoFA updates 26 Aug 2026](https://www.mofa.gov.np/content/1865/latest-updates-on-flash-floods--26-august/); [Rabin Paudel OPMCM update](https://np.linkedin.com/in/rabinpaudel)
- Nepal Red Cross restored a generator at Dhunche after a 5-day power cut so ~150 people could charge phones and contact family: "We finally got the opportunity to connect with our family members." — [NRCS Rasuwa Situation Update 5](https://website-api.nrcs.org/media/highlights/files/Rasuwa_Situation_Update_5.pdf)
- RSF: "unconfirmed death tolls and contradictory information spread on social media," linked partly to "the initial delay in official crisis communications"; at least nine media offices affected, Radio Nuwakot and Radio Chimteshwor damaged, other stations off air from power cuts. — [RSF, 2 Sept 2026](https://rsf.org/en/floods-nepal-and-tibet-restrictions-and-disinformation-are-impeding-vital-news)
- Misinformation catalogue (all debunked by non-government fact-checkers): (a) Facebook page "Dudhauli FM" posted a 20-second clip claiming Home Minister Sudan Gurung in a disaster tunnel — actually Uttarakhand Fire Service footage from 14 Aug; spread to TikTok and X; (b) X account video 27 Aug, >7 million views, Hive Moderation 97.2% AI-likely, flagged by X community notes and BBC Verify; (c) AI "bridge collapse"; (d) "This is a GLOF" video traced by Nepal Fact Check to Columbia Glacier, Alaska; (e) fabricated before/after photos (AAP FactCheck); (f) a clip from Ha Giang, Vietnam; (g) AI "missile strike on dam" (VERA Files). — [Kathmandu Post, 31 Aug 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/08/31/in-disaster-aftermath-nepal-battles-a-flood-of-misinformation); [AAP FactCheck](https://www.aap.com.au/factcheck/fake-flood-footage-inundates-social-media-after-nepal-disaster/); [Alt News](https://www.altnews.in/nepal-floods-unrelated-videos-of-past-disasters-ai-visuals-falsely-viral/); [BOOM](https://www.boomlive.in/fact-check/viral-nepal-flash-flood-videos-claim-fact-check-old-unrelated-videos-32124); [VERA Files](https://verafiles.org/articles/fact-check-water-dam-collapse-did-not-cause-nepal-tibet-flood-clip-is-ai-generated); [Full Fact](https://fullfact.org/world/fake-footage-nepal-tibet-flood/)
- Nepal Fact Check's Chetana Kunwar: "AI-generated content and manipulated 'before and after' visuals make it significantly easier to create false impressions." Deepak Adhikari (Nepal Check): people share old "trapped" videos after the person is already rescued. — [Kathmandu Post, 31 Aug 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/08/31/in-disaster-aftermath-nepal-battles-a-flood-of-misinformation)
- Rumour that army/police hoarded or withheld relief: Nepal Police called it "false and baseless" (9 Sept 2026). — [Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/nepal/nepal-police-warns-against-rumours-over-relief-supplies-after-floods)
- Police identified 173 pieces of misinformation/rumour content, 168 of them on Facebook; arrested a 24-year-old for Reels mocking ethnic groups/celebrating deaths. — [Onlinekhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/police-intensify-crackdown-on-social-media-misinformation-and-rumours.html); [Onlinekhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/?p=589606)
- Press Council Nepal opened a "Special Complaints on the Bhotekoshi Incident" portal; 101 complaints by 5 pm Sat 29 Aug, 67 about personal accounts posting gruesome scenes, some against online news portals; ordered 72 (later >100) accounts to remove content, most complied; chair Umesh Shrestha: mainstream media "largely avoided misleading and excessively graphic content." Government's Social Media Regulation and Management Committee opened talks with Meta and TikTok on AI content, graphic images and fake QR codes. — [Kathmandu Post, 31 Aug 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/08/31/in-disaster-aftermath-nepal-battles-a-flood-of-misinformation); [AFP via The Star, 12 Sept 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/09/12/how-china-and-nepal-are-battling-flood-rumours-online)
- TikTok (July 2025, monsoon) launched a Nepal search-banner guide pointing users to BIPAD as "the country's national disaster information portal." — [Technology Khabar](https://www.technologykhabar.com/?p=207589)
- Cross-border: posts abroad claimed China lied about the toll or its dams caused the flood (scientists attribute to glacial collapse); China blocked the Nepal Police victim-identification site (RSF verified). — [RSF, 2 Sept 2026](https://rsf.org/en/floods-nepal-and-tibet-restrictions-and-disinformation-are-impeding-vital-news)
- Government communication critique (national): no regular on-camera briefings; PM's Facebook clarification on an eviction drive came "days after the rumour cycle had already set its terms"; author recommends "a Nepali-language fact-check portal on a gov.np domain." — [Onlinekhabar opinion, 2026](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/balen-governments-miss-communication.html)
- Routine of Nepal Banda (RONB): started 2011 for strike schedules, load-shedding and traffic; follower counts reported variously as 1.7M, 2.3M and 4M on Facebook; deactivated its page after backlash over a post seen as favouring then-Mayor Balen Shah. — [Onlinekhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/most-popular-social-media-pages-in-nepal.html); [Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/kathmandu/routine-of-nepal-bandh-page-shuts-down-after-mayor-balens-controversial-post)

### Inferences
- The "verification loop" is radio/TV → not government web. A verified-not-viral site competes for the verification role currently played by radio/TV bulletins and by foreign/independent fact-checkers; it is not competing with Facebook for discovery.
- The single biggest 2026 failure was the gap between the event and a trusted official voice; within that gap AI content got 7M views. Speed of a first verified post matters more than completeness.
- Rumours have a stable repertoire across 2015→2026: "bigger disaster coming", "relief being hoarded/misused", recycled footage, fake death tolls. A fixed "rumour tracker" section can be pre-templated.
- Facebook pages with "FM" in the name (Dudhauli FM) were vectors for misinformation — a brand that looks like local radio is not a trust marker in itself.

## Key Question 2: How do families search for missing persons, what privacy/trauma issues arise, and what was learned from Quakemap/KLL?

### Takeaway
In 2026 families searched in parallel through Nepal Police Facebook lists (names, DOB, nationality), a QR-linked police portal of photos of unidentified bodies, a physical "wall of missing" at TU Teaching Hospital, LED screens of corpse images at Bharatpur, NRCS Restoring Family Links hotline/social media, district police phone lines, and ad-hoc citizen-built trackers — with no centralised missing-persons database at the outset. Trauma-related harms (graphic images, families viewing corpse photos) were widely reported; explicit privacy/consent debate was not.

### Cited Findings

**2026 Bhote Koshi flood — search practices**
- Nepal Police published missing-person lists on its Facebook page containing "names, date of birth and their country" (e.g., 23 Malaysians on 27 Aug 2026) and "a list and photographs of unidentified dead bodies … accessed by scanning a QR code available on the Nepal Police Facebook page"; first-degree relatives asked to give DNA at Nepal Police Hospital, Maharajgunj, or nearest District Police Office. — [Bernama, Aug 2026](https://www.bernama.com/en/world/news.php?id=2599969); [The Star, 28 Aug 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/08/28/nepal-floods-police-release-names-of-missing-malaysians)
- Nepal Police unidentified-bodies portal: udb.nepalpolice.gov.np/disaster; bodies kept in 21 hospitals. — [Rabin Paudel OPMCM update](https://np.linkedin.com/in/rabinpaudel) (fetch of the portal failed: TLS certificate error, see Gaps)
- "Wall of missing": photos along a 25-metre wall and columns outside TU Teaching Hospital, Kathmandu; families also check the morgue and an open-air police courtyard; police had received 94 bodies and identified 12 by 1 Sept. Quotes: "I am here hoping to find their bodies" (Debu Sapkota); "I don't know anything more about what happened to him" (Raj Kumari Tamang). An unnamed "government website that compiles information on survivors and the missing" also used. — [AFP via The Star, 1 Sept 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/09/01/nepal039s-wall-of-missing-offers-last-hope-after-himalayan-flood)
- Bharatpur: "Distraught families … watched grisly images of mangled corpses on a screen" (LED screen) to identify relatives; morgues overflowed and temporary burials began. — [AFP via Tribune.net.ph, 31 Aug 2026](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/08/31/morgues-overflow-burials-begin)
- PM Shah in parliament (2 Sept 2026) defended temporary burial of unidentified bodies with DNA preserved: "we have temporarily managed underground only those bodies that could not be immediately identified." — [Kathmandu Post, 2 Sept 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/09/02/pm-shah-addresses-five-queries-over-government-s-handling-of-bhotekoshi-floods)
- NRCS Restoring Family Links: 116 tracing requests registered "through NRCS Hotline, and social media platform"; RFL volunteers at NRCS HQ, Bir Hospital, Trauma Center, TUTH, Manmohan; RFL help desks in Nawalparasi East, Kaski, Chitwan, Nuwakot; 1,930 body bags distributed; PFA given to 35 family members at the Bharatpur body-management centre; NDRRMA figures cited: 987 dead, 3,916 missing across 62 districts (as of update 5). — [NRCS Rasuwa Situation Update 5](https://website-api.nrcs.org/media/highlights/files/Rasuwa_Situation_Update_5.pdf); ICRC support described at [ICRC](https://www.icrc.org/en/article/nepal-operational-update-humanitarian-response-after-devastating-flood)
- Foreign Red Cross societies ran RFL intake by email/phone (Australia: tracing@redcross.org.au; Singapore: rfl@redcross.sg; US: +1 844-782-9441). — [Australian Red Cross](https://www.redcross.org.au/tracing-restoring-family-links); [Atlanta News First, 31 Aug 2026](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/08/31/desperate-search-survivors-continues-nepal-how-reach-loved-ones/)
- District police as intake: Kanchanpur Police published six phone numbers for people unable to reach relatives; Madhes provincial police compiled 180 missing "based on applications submitted and information provided by family members over the phone"; Makawanpur DAO held the official list. — [Onlinekhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/24-people-from-kanchanpur-reported-missing-after-rasuwa-floods.html); [Onlinekhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/rasuwa-flood-180-people-from-madhes-still-missing-2-confirmed-dead.html); [Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/nepal/five-makawanpur-residents-dead-61-missing-in-rasuwa-flood)
- Citizen-built tracker: Niraj Bhusal, IT officer at the Ministry of Finance, built overnight a portal integrating damage statistics, missing-persons list and relief efforts, coded with Grok and hosted on GitHub, pulling from NDRRMA, Nepal Police, Army, DAOs, Copernicus imagery and news; "verification stays human before anything sensitive goes live"; it took missing-person reports via a Google Form cross-referenced against official rescue lists; "a few days after Bhusal's bulletin went live, the government launched its own, [with] a missing-persons search, a help-request form, emergency contacts." — [Kantipur/ekantipur, 3 Sept 2026](https://ekantipur.com/technology/2026/09/03/en/neeraj-creates-bhotekoshi-flood-portal-with-the-power-of-ai-58-15.html); [BOOM Decode newsletter (search snippet; page returned 403)](https://decodemail.boomlive.in/p/how-do-you-find-someone-after-a-flood)

- Diaspora helpdesks: Sewa International USA collected info on missing/stranded Americans; Isha Foundation deployed ~25 Nepali volunteers for coordination. — [Sewa USA](https://sewausa.org/PressReleases/13681253); [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/?p=300750)

- Legal follow-up: government developing a procedure to officially recognise >5,000 people as missing so relatives can receive insurance. — [Ukranews aggregator (unverified, single source)](https://ukranews.com/en/news/1179362-nepal-has-called-off-search-efforts-following-massive-flooding-more-than-5-000-people-are-missing)

**Trauma / graphic content**
- 67 of 101 Press Council complaints concerned personal accounts posting gruesome scenes; "images and footage of bodies and injuries were shared widely, often by personal accounts without warnings." — [Kathmandu Post, 31 Aug 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/08/31/in-disaster-aftermath-nepal-battles-a-flood-of-misinformation)
- NRCS listed "Integrate RFL and PFA service for effective psychosocial support" as a key need. — [NRCS Update 5](https://website-api.nrcs.org/media/highlights/files/Rasuwa_Situation_Update_5.pdf)

**2015 — Quakemap.org / Kathmandu Living Labs**
- QuakeMap.org received 2,035 reports; 978 verified; 551 required action; 434 acted on; 309 fully closed. — [World Bank feature, 27 Oct 2015](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2015/10/27/harnessing-the-power-of-the-crowd-reflections-six-months-after-the-gorkha-earthquake-in-nepal)
- KLL (founded 2013) had pre-mapped >100,000 Kathmandu Valley buildings, ~350 health facilities and 2,300 schools under Open Cities (GFDRR, 2012) and trained Nepal Red Cross staff in 2013; after 25 Apr 2015 it worked from a parking lot, launched QuakeRelief.info on 1 May, printed maps because mobile maps were unreliable in the field, and the Nepal Army's GIS division approached KLL on 29 Apr for camp locations. Lessons: "mapping only affected districts is not enough — responders need to know where people live and how to reach them"; preparedness mapping and an on-the-ground coordinating organisation were decisive. — [HPN, "Mapping for resilience"](https://odihpn.org/magazine/mapping-for-resilience-crowd-sourced-mapping-in-crises/)
- >7,500 OSM contributors 25 Apr–8 Jun 2015; responding units needed OSM training which KLL provided; Ushahidi used for SMS/geolocated voice calls. — [OSM wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/2015_Nepal_earthquake); [AFD Open Cities](https://smartcity-guide.afd.fr/open-cities-project-une-cartographie-des-risques.html?lang=en)
- KLL described itself as "a bridge between victims with rescue and relief workers"; 36 locals + >4,300 remote supporters. — [Republica, 2015](https://myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/index.php/news/we-are-a-bridge-between-victims-with-rescue-and-relief-workers)
- Google Person Finder and Facebook Safety Check were used in 2015 to reunite people. — [NPR, 27 Apr 2015](https://www.npr.org/sections/alltechconsidered/2015/04/27/402641220/how-tech-firms-are-helping-people-in-the-nepal-earthquake-zone)

### Inferences
- Missing-person search in Nepal is multi-channel and duplicative by default; a platform that aggregates (police list + NRCS RFL + hospital lists) with provenance per entry fills the "no centralised database" gap identified by volunteers — but only if it links to official identifiers (police case, hospital) rather than replacing them.
- The state itself published photos of corpses and DOBs of the missing publicly in 2026; privacy norms in Nepali disaster practice are permissive, so a "verified, not viral" site can differentiate on consent and dignity (blurred/photo-less default, family-initiated entries, takedown on reunification) without being out of step with law or practice.
- Quakemap's 48% verification rate and 56% close-out rate are usable benchmarks for a volunteer verification queue.

### Gaps
- No Nepali source found discussing consent, data protection or ethics of publishing missing/deceased names and photos (2015 or 2026).
- Could not fetch the Nepal Police udb.nepalpolice.gov.np/disaster portal (TLS error), the BOOM Decode piece (403) or the Bhusal portal URL; portal name/traffic unknown.
- No KLL self-published lessons-learned on QuakeMap's verification workflow, false reports or privacy handling; no evidence of KLL involvement in 2024 or 2026 floods.
- No figures on how many of the 116 NRCS tracing requests were resolved.

## Key Question 3: How are needs and offers matched today, and what are the known failures (duplication, unreached wards, fraud, scams)?

### Takeaway
Matching still runs through CDO/DAO → ward chairs → security forces and NGO clusters, with Facebook/GoFundMe appeals and PM Disaster Relief Fund deposits on the donor side; documented failures are geographic (roadside/helicopter-accessible wards served first, remote wards left hungry for weeks), list-based (eligibility disputes, renters excluded), and fraud (fake PM-fund QR codes/e-wallets within 48 hours), rather than well-documented duplication.

### Cited Findings

**2023 Jajarkot**
- Relief "distributed only in the settlements closer to the roads"; named remote settlements had nothing until late Monday evening; CDO said relief had reached while acknowledging remote areas had not. — [Kathmandu Post, 7 Nov 2023](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2023/11/07/aid-dispersal-begins-in-quake-hit-areas)
- Tarpaulins given only to house owners; renters "initially considered eligible were later excluded." — [Himalayan Times, 8 Nov 2023](https://thehimalayantimes.com/nepal/even-after-five-days-since-the-disaster-earthquake-survivors-are-not-receiving-tarps)
- 62,012 tarpaulins needed vs 41,256 distributed (15–18 Nov 2023), >20,000 households without. — [DPNet Quad-Day report](https://www.dpnet.org.np/public/uploads/files/Quad-Day%20Jajarkot%20EQ%20Report%2015-18%20Nov%202023-11-19%2002-45-30.pdf)
- Coordination "to avoid duplicating efforts" among NGOs/INGOs/UN was stated as intent; CDO pledged ward-level distribution with local representatives. — [DPNet Sitrep 02/03](https://www.dpnet.org.np/uploads/files/Daily%20Situation%20Analysis%20Report%20-%2003%202023-11-06%2018-59-27.pdf)
- Junichande RM food crisis from transport difficulties (later). — [DPNet](https://dpnet.org.np/resource-detail/1927)

**2024 floods**
- Saptari (Tilathi Koiladi RM-5): UNICEF offered Rs 6.87 million cash for 458 families; ward chair refused because 1,470 households were affected and he "could not single out 457 families … fearing criticism"; initial list of 657 names did not represent the worst-hit Tikuliya and Banarjhula; money returned, nobody paid. — [Kathmandu Post via Asia News Network, Oct 2024](https://asianews.network/over-458-flood-affected-families-in-nepals-saptari-left-without-promised-aid/)
- 3 Oct 2024: rights watchdog criticised slow response; government formed a 15-day loss-assessment task force. — [Kathmandu Post, 3 Oct 2024](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2024/10/03/many-flood-hit-families-still-await-promised-aid)
- IFRC post-distribution monitoring report (Apr 2025) exists for the 2024 response. — [IFRC GO](https://go-api.ifrc.org/api/DownloadFile/E1250/MDRNP018_PDM%20Report_Final)
- OCHA Nepal floods update 22 Oct 2024. — [OCHA via FAO](https://www.fao.org/fileadmin/templates/tc/tce/pdf/nepal_OCHAFloods_22_Oct_final.pdf)

**2026 Bhote Koshi flood**
- Amachhodingmo RM, Rasuwa, 24 days on: ward 1 (~270 hh) got ~30 sacks of rice, given only to bereaved families; ward 2 (388 hh) ~70 sacks; ward 3 (~300 hh) not all reached; wards 4–5 got WFP food by large helicopter; small helicopters to wards 1–3 carry ≤15 sacks; some rice arrived rotten; ward 2 chair called the CDO who said relief "had already been sent" — nothing had arrived; CDO stopped answering calls; Gre school hostel (200 students) closed for lack of food. — [Kathmandu Post, 18 Sept 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/health/2026/09/18/24-days-after-flood-hunger-grips-three-rusuwa-wards)
- 3,702 households (14,461 people) isolated in Gosaikunda and Amachhodingmo RMs with road, power and communications cut up to six days; 41 motorable bridges washed away. — [NRCS Update 5](https://website-api.nrcs.org/media/highlights/files/Rasuwa_Situation_Update_5.pdf)
- Parliament criticism: over-reliance on the army rather than local/provincial governments; slow PDNA; a 31-person civil coalition demanded federal coordination with local authorities; survivors told Al Jazeera of delays in aid and in locating missing relatives. — [Kathmandu Post, 2 Sept 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/09/02/pm-shah-addresses-five-queries-over-government-s-handling-of-bhotekoshi-floods); [b360nepal](https://www.b360nepal.com/detail/29932)
- Coordination structure: government-led via NDRRMA/MoHA; UN RC coordinates partners; $49.6M Flash Appeal; CERF $2.5M; >64,000 reached; emergency declared in 15 municipalities. — [UNOG Newsroom](https://www.unognewsroom.org/story/en/3241/nepal-floods-aftermath-un-ocha-ifrc-un-women-who); [Integral Alliance alert, Sept 2026](https://integralalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2026-09-Nepal-alert-1.pdf)
- Logistics snapshot 6 Sept 2026: 83 trucks, 38 jeeps, 9 helicopter flights from Kathmandu, 173 APF flights; 21,465 security personnel; 3,652 people in 39 camps. — [b360nepal, 6 Sept 2026](https://www.b360nepal.com/detail/29976)

**Fraud and scams**
- Nepal Police (27 Aug 2026, <48 h after the flood): fake accounts/e-wallets in the name of "Prime Minister's Disaster Relief Fund," fake QR codes copying official logos, fake pages/groups on Facebook, WhatsApp, TikTok, Instagram and Viber, impersonation calls "registering" people for relief, OTP phishing, "I'm safe but need money now" messages; advice: verify only via PMO website or verified social accounts; report to Cyber Bureau. — [Kathmandu Post, 27 Aug 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/08/27/nepal-police-warn-of-scams-targeting-flood-relief-donations); [Onlinekhabar Cyber Bureau warning](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/cyber-bureau-warns-public-of-disaster-relief-scams.html)
- Nepal Rastra Bank: official PM-fund account details only on NRB, OPMCM and MoF websites; do not scan QR codes received via social media/messaging; check account name before paying by mobile banking or wallet. Nepal Bankers' Association issued a parallel warning. — [Onlinekhabar NRB](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/nrb-m-disaster-relief-fund.html); [ShareSansar NBA](https://www.sharesansar.com/newsdetail/nepal-bankers-association-warns-the-public-of-fundraising-scams-on-social-media)
- Police arrested a man for sharing a fabricated relief-fund donation appeal to promote his own account. — [AFP via The Star, 12 Sept 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/09/12/how-china-and-nepal-are-battling-flood-rumours-online)
- Authorities asked Meta and TikTok to remove fabricated footage and fraudulent donation appeals including QR codes. — [CADE Project](https://cadeproject.org/?p=155221)
- UK (Get Safe Online) issued a fake-charity-appeal warning for the Nepal flood. — [Get Safe Online](https://www.getsafeonline.org/personal/news-item/warning-about-fake-charity-appeals-following-nepal-tibet-flash-flood/)
- MoF set up PM-fund collection channels across ten commercial banks "to guard against fake QR codes and rogue collectors"; fund passed Rs 2.295 billion within days (single, unverified source). — [everhem.com](https://development.everhem.com/nepal-flood-relief-fund-racked-billions-fast)
- GoFundMe: used by NRNA UK (nearly Rs 5.2M / £13,903 of £50K), NRNA Iowa (deposits to own bank account, then PM fund), Prabas Nepali Samparka Samiti America (501c3). No source found stating GoFundMe is unavailable to Nepal-based organisers; all found campaigns were run by diaspora entities abroad. — [GoFundMe NRNA UK](https://www.gofundme.com/f/stand-with-nepal-support-families-affected-by-the-devastati); [GoFundMe NRNA Iowa](https://www.gofundme.com/f/help-nepal-recover-nrna-iowa-flood-relief); [Onlinekhabar, 4 Sept 2026](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/?p=588994)

**2015 PM fund trust history**
- Government's attempt to route donations through the PM Disaster Relief Fund was "a public relations disaster"; NRB seized local post-quake accounts and redirected them to the fund; only Rs 483M of ~Rs 1.5B released to ministries was reported spent (Aug 2015). — [The New Humanitarian, 5 May 2015](https://www.thenewhumanitarian.org/analysis/2015/05/05/nepal-quake-fund-move-pr-fiasco); [Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/kathmandu/ministries-cagey-about-pm-relief-fund-spending); [Kathmandu Post, 16 May 2015](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2015/05/16/fund-collection-should-be-transparent-finmin)

### Inferences
- The dominant matching failure is not duplication but *invisibility of remote wards* plus CDO-level claims that "relief has been sent" without ward-level confirmation. A ward-level receipt/acknowledgement ledger directly addresses the quoted dispute in Amachhodingmo.
- Eligibility-list politics (Saptari 2024, Jajarkot renters 2023) means a platform publishing recipient lists will face fairness disputes; publish criteria and counts, not just names.
- Scam vectors are QR codes and wallet numbers circulated on social media; a site that never displays payment QR/wallet numbers itself and instead deep-links to NRB/OPMCM-listed accounts avoids becoming a vector.

### Gaps
- No source documents duplicate relief to the same households in any of the five events; "duplication" is asserted as a risk, not measured.
- No eSewa/Khalti-specific scam reports; warnings refer generically to "e-wallets".
- No 3W matrix or OCHA sitrep for 2026 was retrieved; HDX usage by Nepali actors not evidenced.
- No evidence found about GoFundMe restrictions for Nepal-resident organisers.

## Key Question 4: What trust markers make a Nepali reader believe a relief/missing-person post is genuine?

### Takeaway
Evidence is indirect: Nepalis verify via radio/TV bulletins and official pages (NDRRMA, Nepal Police, OPMCM/NRB), fact-checkers win trust through traceable provenance (original upload date/source, AI-detection scores), and official bodies say "verified social media accounts" and gov.np domains are the markers to check; no survey directly measures which visual cues (logo, phone, ward chair quote, Nepali language) persuade.

### Cited Findings
- Verification behaviour: legacy radio/TV news bulletins are the verification channel; radio listenership spikes during calamities. — [Nepali Times, 15 Oct 2023](https://nepalitimes.com/surveying-nepal-s-digital-landscape)
- Trust gradient: ~one-third high trust in TV/radio/print vs "a small proportion" for social media/online portals; earlier (2018) survey reported the most trusted source was neighbours, friends and family. — [Spotlight Nepal, 2026](https://www.spotlightnepal.com/2026/08/01/rethinking-formal-communications-in-nepals-digital-era-where-should-leaders-speak/); [Nepali Times "Nepal's interface with information"](https://nepalitimes.com/nepal-s-interface-with-information)
- Official markers prescribed by authorities (2026): PMO official website or "verified social media accounts"; account details only on NRB/OPMCM/MoF sites; QR codes from social media are untrustworthy by default. — [Kathmandu Post, 27 Aug 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/08/27/nepal-police-warn-of-scams-targeting-flood-relief-donations); [Onlinekhabar NRB](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/nrb-m-disaster-relief-fund.html)
- Scammers copy exactly the markers people trust: "fake QR codes that copy the logos, names, or formats of official government agencies." — [Kathmandu Post, 27 Aug 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/08/27/nepal-police-warn-of-scams-targeting-flood-relief-donations)
- Fact-checker methods that carried weight: locating the original upload (Uttarakhand Fire Service page, 14 Aug), AI-detection scores (Hive 97.2%), X community notes, cross-checking with BBC Verify/AAP. — [Kathmandu Post, 31 Aug 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/08/31/in-disaster-aftermath-nepal-battles-a-flood-of-misinformation)
- Nepal Fact Check is run by CMR-Nepal and MySansar.com, launched 2020. — [RSF, 2 Sept 2026](https://rsf.org/en/floods-nepal-and-tibet-restrictions-and-disinformation-are-impeding-vital-news); South Asia Check launched 9 Sept 2015 as independent, non-partisan, non-profit. — [Global Voices, 5 Apr 2021](https://globalvoices.org/2021/04/05/fact-checking-in-nepal-in-the-time-of-pandemic/)
- Government itself asked public and media "to rely on officially verified information." — [MoFA briefing note](https://mofa.gov.np/content/1866/press-briefing-note-by-hon--minister-for/)
- Press Council chair judged mainstream media trustworthy relative to personal accounts (67 of 101 complaints against personal accounts). — [Kathmandu Post, 31 Aug 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/08/31/in-disaster-aftermath-nepal-battles-a-flood-of-misinformation)
- Open Mic Nepal 2015: "toxic/corrupted information" was countered by "reliable and verified information" delivered quickly via trusted local radio and journalists. — [CommInit](https://reference.comminit.com/content/open-mic-nepal)
- Opinion: a Nepali-language fact-check portal on a gov.np domain was recommended because citizens get news via TikTok/Facebook/X/Instagram "with no editorial standard." — [Onlinekhabar opinion](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/balen-governments-miss-communication.html)
- Official figures were themselves inconsistent (e.g., 903 dead/4,247 missing on 31 Aug vs 781/2,502 on 30 Aug vs 1,410/6,145 on 17 Sept), with NDRRMA stating figures "are provisional and subject to change with each bulletin." — [Prothom Alo/AFP](https://en.prothomalo.com/international/south-asia/pirey1r5s5); [Telangana Today, 17 Sept 2026](https://telanganatoday.com/nepal-floods-death-toll-rises-to-1410-more-than-6100-missing); [Rabin Paudel OPMCM](https://np.linkedin.com/in/rabinpaudel)

### Inferences
- The strongest trust markers evidenced are *provenance* (link to the original official post/bulletin with timestamp) and *institutional domain* (gov.np, verified badge), not design cues. Logos and official formats are actively forged, so a logo alone is a weak marker.
- Because official numbers change bulletin to bulletin, showing "as of [bulletin time], source: NDRRMA bulletin #N" is a stronger credibility signal than a single number.
- Named verifier + named local official (ward chair) mirrors how Kathmandu Post built credibility in the Rasuwa hunger story (named ward chairs, household counts).

### Gaps
- No survey or experiment found on which specific cues (phone number, ward chair quote, Nepali language, photo, verifier name) increase belief among Nepali readers.
- Trust-figure year in the Spotlight piece is unstated; 2022 survey trust tables were not directly retrieved.

## Key Question 5: Existing Nepali civic/disaster platforms and what users/reviewers say about them

### Takeaway
BIPAD is the official DIMS and is now what TikTok and officials point to, but assessments cite terminology, metadata, consistency and municipal-staff awareness gaps; DHM's forecasting is public but criticised for sparse stations (35 of ~40 functional) and warnings that arrive after the water; Nepal Police relies on Facebook + a QR-linked portal; NDRRMA's Facebook page functions as the de facto bulletin board; most "platforms" are Facebook pages plus PDFs.

### Cited Findings
- BIPAD (bipadportal.gov.np): launched by MoHA/NDRRMA; national portal with independent platforms for federal, provincial and municipal governments. Identified gaps in its loss/damage datasets: unclear indicator terminology, missing critical indicators, no systematic collection tools, missing metadata, data inconsistency, misinterpretation, missing historical data, visualisation issues. — [PreventionWeb, "Standardization of loss and damage datasets on BIPAD"](https://www.preventionweb.net/quick/50881); [DPNet copy, July 2023](https://mail.dpnet.org.np/uploads/files/Standardization%20of%20loss%20and%20damage%202023-07-24%2010-06-11.pdf)
- YI-Lab best-practice review recommends a glossary for terminology/symbology, more hazard categories, interface and download improvements; institutionalisation study across 13 municipalities analysed "challenges and shortcomings." — [PreventionWeb](https://preventionweb.net/quick/51690); [PreventionWeb PDF](https://www.preventionweb.net/files/76669_globalregionalandnationalbestpracti.pdf)
- Oxford Policy Management (Dec 2020) report on strengthening BIPAD for federal/provincial/local decision-making. — [OPM](https://www.opml.co.uk/sites/default/files/migrated_bolt_files/strengthening-the-bipad-portal-for-data-on-disaster.pdf)
- Mayors praised BIPAD's "usability and effectiveness in storing and retrieving disaster data" (Mar 2021) but a later account notes "not all municipal staff at grassroots levels are aware of the portal." — [Himalayan Times, 11 Mar 2021](https://thehimalayantimes.com/nepal/efforts-to-enhance-disaster-information-system-lauded); [Himalayan Times, Vyas Municipality](https://thehimalayantimes.com/nepal/vyas-municipality-launches-bipad-portal)
- TikTok's monsoon guide (July 2025) links users to BIPAD as the national disaster portal. — [Technology Khabar](https://www.technologykhabar.com/?p=207589)
- Yale analogy (COVID CMIS): local governments that failed to enter data "did not receive federal quarantine funds" and were "more likely to have reported technical staff shortage." — [Yale EGC brief](https://egc.yale.edu/sites/default/files/Nepal%20Research%20Briefs/CMIS_brief_200809.pdf)
- DHM (hydrology.gov.np): only 35 of ~40 water-level stations functional (2024-era); historically focused on aviation, "officials never thought about reaching out to the public then"; op-ed: predictions "inverted" and "little dissemination … to the people under threat of flash floods"; rainfall once per 24 h from 40 stations inadequate for flash floods (older). — [Republica](https://myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/inside-the-flood-forecasting-section-how-timely-forecast-could-save-lives); [Onlinekhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/nepal-flood-alert-better-technology-is-saving-lives-in-flood-prone-areas.html); [Himalayan Times op-ed](https://thehimalayantimes.com/opinion/monsoon-woes-quakes-portend-aggravation/)
- 2026: gauges "built to detect gradual rises, not fast floods"; northern side a "blind spot"; no Nepal–China warning protocol; Bhote Koshi hydropower warning gives 79 villages 5–8 minutes. — [Kathmandu Post, 2 Sept 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/09/02/nepal-sent-its-disaster-alert-38-minutes-after-the-glacier-collapse-it-wasn-t-enough); [Kathmandu Post column, 5 Aug 2025](https://kathmandupost.com/columns/2025/08/05/nepal-floods-a-preventable-disaster)
- NDRRMA: Facebook page is the cited "detailed information" channel; website ndrrma.gov.np/en used by press for figures. — [Rabin Paudel OPMCM](https://np.linkedin.com/in/rabinpaudel); [b360nepal](https://www.b360nepal.com/detail/29976)
- Nepal Police: Facebook page for missing lists + QR to udb.nepalpolice.gov.np/disaster; 2015 web missing-person desk. — [Bernama](https://www.bernama.com/en/world/news.php?id=2599969); [Kathmandu Post, 2015](https://kathmandupost.com/miscellaneous/2015/05/10/nepal-police-install-missing-person-desk)
- Nepal Red Cross: NRCS hotline + social media for RFL; CEA line answered 65 calls; "regular update on social media … public service messages." — [NRCS Update 5](https://website-api.nrcs.org/media/highlights/files/Rasuwa_Situation_Update_5.pdf)
- PrakopAlert app (ICIMOD + NRCS): weather/flood forecasts in Nepali and English. — [SERVIR/ICIMOD](https://servir.icimod.org/news/innovative-prakopalert-mobile-app-launched-to-strengthen-community-resilience-and-disaster-preparedness-in-nepal/)
- Ncell–DHM MoU to extend early warnings to 13 hazards. — [Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/environment/ncell-dhm-sign-mou-strengthening-early-warning-systems-to-cover-13-hazards)
- Nagarik App (MoCIT): single platform for government services, initially 40 of 64 bodies, includes local-government info and Hello Sarkar complaints (via phone, email, Facebook, X, Viber, WhatsApp). — [Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/business/mocit-launches-beta-version-of-nagarik-app); [Onlinekhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/govt-preparing-to-launch-new-app-to-address-public-complaints.html)
- Nagarik Alert (non-government, Lalitpur youth): report potholes/drainage with photo + ward-level location in Nepali/English, also shares flood/landslide warnings; claims to supply verified data to government and NGOs. — [Ratopati](https://english.ratopati.com/story/80549/citizen-alert-that-covers-everything-from-disasters-to-government-complaints)
- Hamro Patro: functions as a news aggregator (republishes Onlinekhabar/others, e.g. flood warning op-ed, NRNA donation news). — [Hamro Patro news](https://www.hamropatro.com/en/news/detail/781079746669864-himalayan-hazards-are-becoming-more-complex-our-warning-systems-need)
- Citizen-built 2026 flood portal (Bhusal) preceded the government's own missing-persons search/help-request site by days. — [Kantipur, 3 Sept 2026](https://ekantipur.com/technology/2026/09/03/en/neeraj-creates-bhotekoshi-flood-portal-with-the-power-of-ai-58-15.html)
- Accountability Lab Nepal: Open Mic / Mobile Citizen Helpdesks (2015), Coronavirus CivActs Campaign (2020) — rumour-debunking plus feedback loops; no 2026 flood programme found. — [Accountability Lab](https://accountabilitylab.org/?p=21256)
- KLL/HOT: 2015 QuakeMap, 2017 Lower Karnali flood-risk mapathons with IIASA/Practical Action. — [IIASA blog, 2017](https://blog.iiasa.ac.at/2017/05/29/from-the-himalayas-to-the-andes-crowdsourced-disaster-risk-mapping/)
- RONB: strike/holiday/traffic page since 2011; millions of followers; page deactivated after a controversy. — [Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/kathmandu/routine-of-nepal-bandh-page-shuts-down-after-mayor-balens-controversial-post)

### Inferences
- There is no Nepali platform that publicly tracks needs → offers → delivery at ward level; the state's "platform" during 2026 was a Facebook page plus PDF bulletins, and the first integrated dashboard was a civil servant's GitHub side project.
- BIPAD is institution-facing (data entry by municipalities); its critiques are about data standards, not citizen UX — a citizen-facing layer is open space, and linking to BIPAD confers legitimacy (TikTok already does this).

### Gaps
- No independent usability test, English-only/desktop-only complaint, or 2025–26 evaluation of BIPAD found; critiques are 2020–2023 and developer/donor-affiliated.
- Could not load bipadportal.gov.np, hydrology.gov.np or udb.nepalpolice.gov.np to assess UX directly.
- No evidence on Sajha Sawal, Open Nepal, "Nepal Flood Relief" sites, or Hamro Nepal as disaster platforms.
- No Nepali-language user reviews (e.g., app store comments) retrieved.

## Key Question 6: Does "loss and damage" / climate-compensation framing resonate or alienate Nepali audiences?

### Takeaway
Elite and official discourse embraced the framing after Thame (Aug 2024) and the 2026 flood — "a refrain for every segment of Nepali society", with the PM's UNGA trip "widely appreciated at home" — but commentators warn it risks becoming rhetorical cover for domestic failures (settlement policy, building codes, early warning); public awareness of "climate change" as a concept was only ~49% of households in 2016 and lower in mountain regions, so no survey evidence shows grassroots resonance of "loss and damage" as a term.

### Cited Findings
- Thame GLOF (16 Aug 2024, Solukhumbu) confirmed as glacial lake outburst; then-FM Dahal: "We are facing consequences for a problem for which we are not responsible. I have strongly raised this issue at the UNGA and COP28, calling for climate justice." Thame RM chair: "People here are still worried about the day they will be hit by avalanches." — [Himalayan Times, 16 Aug 2024](https://thehimalayantimes.com/nepal/flash-flood-in-thame-village-confirmed-to-be-caused-by-glacial-lake-outburst); [Stimson event](https://www.stimson.org/event/the-thame-glof-disaster-climate-related-disaster-risk-management-in-the-high-himalayas/)
- COP29 (Nov 2024): Nepal presented "glaring examples of climate change-induced risks." — [Himalayan Times, 11 Nov 2024](https://thehimalayantimes.com/nepal/cop29-kicks-off-today-nepal-presenting-glaring-examples-of-climate-change-induced-risks)
- 2026 flood: grief "but also anger"; activist Tashi Lhazom: "What has Nepal done to deserve this?"; former FM: "an opportune time … to highlight glacier melting and climate justice issues in international climate forums"; Kathmandu asked the Loss and Damage Fund for help but it "has not yet begun distributing its limited cash." — [Khaleej Times/AFP](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/asia/nepal-climate-change-justice-glacier-collapse-floods-disaster?amp=1); [Dhaka Tribune/AFP](https://www.dhakatribune.com/world/asia/419139/nepal-flood-disaster-puts-climate-justice-under)
- RDNA damage estimates ~US$2.66B damage/losses; reconstruction ~US$4.72B. — [Down to Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/climate-change/warming-himalayas-nepals-climate-justice-test)
- Domestic critique (Prakash Bhattarai, Centre for Social Change, 9 Sept 2026): loss and damage "has become something of a refrain for every segment of Nepali society"; PM's UN trip "widely appreciated at home"; but "A country can become so absorbed in the fund that it loses the agenda the fund was meant to serve"; Nepal treated Koshi 2008, Seti 2012, Melamchi 2021, Thame 2024 "as an accident rather than a condition"; calls to "publish ward-level damage and relief lists" and involve Rasuwa/Nuwakot municipalities in relocation decisions; "Loss and damage is the claim. The agenda is everything Nepal does before the claim is paid." — [Annapurna Express, 9 Sept 2026](https://theannapurnaexpress.com/story/66973/)
- Awareness baseline: 2016 National Climate Change Impact Survey (CBS): 49.33% of households had heard of climate change; 63.59% of mountain-region households had not. — [Open Data Nepal](https://opendatanepal.com/dataset/national-climate-change-impact-survey); Gallup 2009: 39% aware (urban 49%, rural 36%). — [Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/124658/high-risk-low-awareness-climate-change-nepal.aspx); Kathmandu Valley 2013: aware of climate disasters but "in their daily lives, climate change does not play a significant role." — [JNRD 2013](https://doaj.org/article/2ef9970373f547768b0586d6b3bf0633)
- Government framed 2026 as "beyond the scope of disaster forecasting" (PM Shah) while critics blamed late warnings. — [Kathmandu Post, 2 Sept 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/09/02/pm-shah-addresses-five-queries-over-government-s-handling-of-bhotekoshi-floods)

### Inferences
- Compensation framing is safe with urban/elite and diaspora audiences and is politically rewarded; the risk flagged domestically is that it reads as deflection if not paired with accountability for local failures (warnings, zoning, relief reaching wards). Content pairing "compensation" with "what our wards needed and didn't get" matches the Annapurna Express critique.
- Low baseline literacy for the term means plain-Nepali explanation (glacier → flood → who pays) is needed for a rural/affected audience.

### Gaps
- No opinion poll on Nepali public reaction to loss-and-damage framing; evidence is official quotes and one op-ed.
- Climate Asia (BBC Media Action 2012–13) full findings not retrieved.

## Key Question 7: What do Nepali/diaspora donors and NGOs/INGOs need to trust and use a platform?

### Takeaway
Donors' revealed behaviour in 2026 was to route money to the PM Disaster Relief Fund via official bank channels (and GoFundMe → PM fund), after official warnings that only NRB/OPMCM-listed accounts are safe; NGOs/INGOs ran on cluster/sitrep PDFs, household counts and ward-level logistics data. Transparency complaints centre on untraceable parallel collections and historically opaque PM-fund spending.

### Cited Findings
- UK diaspora (4 Sept 2026): >25 organisations each deposited Rs 100k–5.2M mostly to the PM fund; NRNA UK via GoFundMe; IME London waived fees; "because individuals and organisations collect separately, it is hard to know exactly how much has reached Nepal or is still being raised"; NRNA UK figure described itself as a "watchdog" against misuse. — [Onlinekhabar, 4 Sept 2026](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/?p=588994)
- NRNA transferred Rs 10 million to the PM fund (first phase); former NRNA president RK Sharma Rs 2.5M; African Nepali communities Rs 10M; diaspora-coordinated Rs 1.53B incl. Nvidia-linked pledge. — [ekantipur, 31 Aug 2026](https://ekantipur.com/news/2026/08/31/en/nrna-transfers-rs-10-million-to-prime-ministers-disaster-relief-fund-in-first-phase-59-07.html); [Hamro Patro news](https://www.hamropatro.com/en/news/detail/95863177408113-former-nrna-president-rk-sharma-donates-npr-2-5-million); [ekantipur, 11 Sept 2026](https://ekantipur.com/diaspora/2026/09/11/en/nepali-community-in-africa-donates-rs-10-million-to-flood-victims-00-58.html); [ekantipur, 5 Sept 2026](https://ekantipur.com/news/2026/09/05/en/nepali-diaspora-raises-rs-15-billion-in-relief-from-nvidia-15-40.html)
- Donor-side verification instructions: PMO website/verified accounts; NRB/OPMCM/MoF account listings; never trust QR from messaging apps; check account name; report to Cyber Bureau or NRB Financial Consumer Protection Unit. — [Kathmandu Post, 27 Aug 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/08/27/nepal-police-warn-of-scams-targeting-flood-relief-donations); [Onlinekhabar NRB](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/nrb-m-disaster-relief-fund.html)
- Historical trust deficit: 2015 PM-fund routing was a "PR fiasco"; ministries didn't disclose spending; Finance Minister admitted inefficiency; donors "reluctant to put money into the fund." — [The New Humanitarian, 2015](https://www.thenewhumanitarian.org/analysis/2015/05/05/nepal-quake-fund-move-pr-fiasco); [Himalayan Times, 2015](https://thehimalayantimes.com/kathmandu/ministries-cagey-about-pm-relief-fund-spending); [PRI, 2016](https://www.pri.org/stories/2016/07/30/aid-nepal-falling-way-short-amid-concerns-corruption)
- GoFundMe campaign disclosures that donors read: "deposited directly into NRNA Iowa's organizational bank account … used only for verified flood-relief … through Prime Ministers Relief Fund"; US 501(c)(3) status; "subject to due diligence." — [GoFundMe NRNA Iowa](https://www.gofundme.com/f/help-nepal-recover-nrna-iowa-flood-relief); [GoFundMe PNSSA](https://www.gofundme.com/f/nepal-flood-disaster-relief-2026)
- NGO data needs visible in practice: NRCS sitreps report by district/municipality, household counts with sex/age disaggregation (3,702 hh; 7,453 M / 7,008 F; 3,650 children; 1,204 elderly), holding-centre counts, bridges destroyed; DPNet Jajarkot reports tracked tarpaulin need vs distributed. — [NRCS Update 5](https://website-api.nrcs.org/media/highlights/files/Rasuwa_Situation_Update_5.pdf); [DPNet](https://www.dpnet.org.np/public/uploads/files/Quad-Day%20Jajarkot%20EQ%20Report%2015-18%20Nov%202023-11-19%2002-45-30.pdf)
- IFRC DREF lists 93,000 affected; PHASE Nepal and LWR publish their own sitreps — i.e., each INGO duplicates situation reporting. — [IFRC DREF](https://go-api.ifrc.org/api/DownloadFile/96508/MDRNP022do); [LWR Sitrep 2](https://lwr.org/technical-resources/nepal-flash-floods-situation-report-no-2); [PHASE Nepal](https://phasenepal.org/?p=2119)
- Volunteer coordination gap: no centralised missing database; volunteers resorted to social media calls; Bhusal's portal consolidated NDRRMA/Police/Army/DAO data because nobody else had. — [LinkedIn, Bishal Khadka](https://np.linkedin.com/in/bishalkhadka); [Kantipur, 3 Sept 2026](https://ekantipur.com/technology/2026/09/03/en/neeraj-creates-bhotekoshi-flood-portal-with-the-power-of-ai-58-15.html)
- Press Council and police used takedown + arrest; UK donors warned by Get Safe Online — diaspora donors face scam warnings from both ends. — [Get Safe Online](https://www.getsafeonline.org/personal/news-item/warning-about-fake-charity-appeals-following-nepal-tibet-flash-flood/)

### Inferences
- Diaspora donors default to the PM fund despite its 2015 reputation because it is the only channel officials vouch for; a platform earns trust by *pointing to* those vouched channels and adding what the fund lacks — an itemised, ward-level "what arrived where" ledger — not by collecting money itself.
- INGO users need district/municipality/ward filters, household counts disaggregated by sex/age, and export, matching the fields they already publish in sitreps.

### Gaps
- No survey of Nepali or diaspora donors' stated trust criteria; evidence is behavioural (where money went) and from campaign disclosures.
- No 2026 audit or spending report of the PM Disaster Relief Fund found.
- NRNA governance/transparency controversies not surfaced by English searches.

## Key Question 8: How do ward/municipality officials share and consume information, and would they use a third-party platform?

### Takeaway
Local officials communicate by phone with the CDO, through security forces and door-to-door, and the state increasingly treats Facebook as an official channel; municipalities enter BIPAD data unevenly and staff awareness is patchy; there is no direct evidence on whether palikas would adopt a third-party platform, but 2026 shows ward chairs publicly contesting CDO claims and demanding ward-level visibility.

### Cited Findings
- Ward chairs' information practice in 2026: ward 2 chair called the CDO, was told relief "had already been sent," found nothing; CDO stopped answering phones; chair's household counts exclude families who fled to Kathmandu. — [Kathmandu Post, 18 Sept 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/health/2026/09/18/24-days-after-flood-hunger-grips-three-rusuwa-wards)
- PM Shah: federal government "in continuous contact with provincial and local governments and elected representatives"; NDRRMA and provinces agreed all tiers must take more responsibility. Critics: army-first, local-government-second. — [Kathmandu Post, 2 Sept 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/09/02/pm-shah-addresses-five-queries-over-government-s-handling-of-bhotekoshi-floods)
- 147 local representatives/staff/teachers/security personnel themselves went missing — local administrations lost their own information nodes. — [Kathmandu Post, 2 Sept 2026](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/09/02/147-local-representatives-government-staff-teachers-and-security-personnel-missing-after-bhotekoshi-floods)
- 2015 flood warning chain: Radio Melamchi acted "as soon as a government official informed us"; police loudspeakers. — [UNDRR](https://undrr.org/quick/66278)
- 2024 Saptari: ward chair controlled the beneficiary list and vetoed partial coverage. — [Kathmandu Post via ANN](https://asianews.network/over-458-flood-affected-families-in-nepals-saptari-left-without-promised-aid/)
- Jajarkot 2023: CDO pledged "all ward levels, in coordination with local representatives" would distribute. — [DPNet Sitrep 03](https://www.dpnet.org.np/uploads/files/Daily%20Situation%20Analysis%20Report%20-%2003%202023-11-06%2018-59-27.pdf)
- Government communication norms: Facebook used for official clarifications; Hello Sarkar accepts complaints via Facebook, X, Viber, WhatsApp; Spotlight argues formal communication should be judged by "accuracy, accountability, timeliness, transparency and clear attribution to public institutions," not medium; citizens "still rely on traditional media for official confirmation." — [Onlinekhabar opinion](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/balen-governments-miss-communication.html); [Spotlight Nepal, 1 Aug 2026](https://www.spotlightnepal.com/2026/08/01/rethinking-formal-communications-in-nepals-digital-era-where-should-leaders-speak/)
- BIPAD at municipal level: mayors praise it; grassroots staff unaware; municipal reporting gaps elsewhere (CMIS) tracked staff shortages. — [Himalayan Times, 2021](https://thehimalayantimes.com/nepal/efforts-to-enhance-disaster-information-system-lauded); [Yale EGC](https://egc.yale.edu/sites/default/files/Nepal%20Research%20Briefs/CMIS_brief_200809.pdf)
- Nagarik Alert (third-party) claims it already supplies "verified data to government and non-government organizations" from ward-tagged citizen reports — a precedent for palikas consuming third-party civic data. — [Ratopati](https://english.ratopati.com/story/80549/citizen-alert-that-covers-everything-from-disasters-to-government-complaints)
- Local officials' capacity: 2024 study in Lumbini/Madhesh/Koshi found knowledge gaps about planning provisions and recommended training including information dissemination. — [NepJOL, 2024](https://nepjol.info/index.php/hj/article/view/70669)
- Annapurna Express recommends municipalities "publish ward-level damage and relief lists." — [Annapurna Express, 9 Sept 2026](https://theannapurnaexpress.com/story/66973/)

### Inferences
- Ward chairs are the most motivated local-government users: in 2026 they wanted a public record proving relief had *not* arrived. A tool that lets a ward chair log "received X sacks, need Y for Z households" gives them leverage against CDO claims; that is a concrete adoption hook.
- Municipal staff capacity is the binding constraint; anything requiring more than a phone and Facebook-level skills will stall.

### Gaps
- No study of palika/ward Facebook pages' content, reach or practice in Nepal (only a 2017 NTNU thesis arguing for adoption).
- No interview or survey evidence on willingness of ward/municipal officials to use third-party platforms.
- No documentation of Nagarik App's local-government content or disaster features in practice.
