# Nepali online news portals and information services: traffic, distribution, business models (2022-2026)

Research date: 2026-10-14. Scope: Nepal only. Every number below carries the URL it came from and the date of that source. Where a source is pre-2022 it is flagged as background and should be treated as dated.

## Key Question 1: Traffic rankings and estimates for top Nepali portals; share of traffic by channel

### Takeaway
Onlinekhabar is the undisputed traffic leader (Similarweb Nepal rank #7, ~6.8M visits in the latest 3-month window; Ahrefs ~500-600K monthly organic search visits), followed at a distance by Hamro Patro's web property, Setopati, eKantipur and Ratopati. Public Similarweb data shows the big Nepali portals are overwhelmingly **direct/app/brand-driven** on desktop (69-83% direct), with organic search second and Facebook the dominant social referrer; full channel splits (incl. Google Discover, which Similarweb can't see) are behind paywalls and no Nepali portal publishes its own breakdown.

### Cited Findings
Similarweb (free tier, September 2026 data; "visits" figure is the "Total Visits Last 3 Months" widget, so treat as a 3-month aggregate, not one month; traffic-source split is desktop-only):
- onlinekhabar.com: 6.8M visits (3-month widget), global rank #9,803 (up from 10,525 three months earlier), Nepal country rank #7, News & Media category rank #882, Direct 69.46% of desktop traffic, then Organic Search, then Organic Social; top social networks Facebook > X > Reddit; bounce 53.66%, 3.38 pages/visit, 3:47 avg visit — [Similarweb onlinekhabar.com](https://www.similarweb.com/website/onlinekhabar.com/) (Sept 2026 data, fetched 2026-10-14)
- hamropatro.com (web only; the app is the real product): 3.5M visits (3-month widget), global #18,473, Nepal #11, Organic Search 49.41% of desktop visits (the only big Nepali site where search beats direct), top social networks YouTube > Facebook > Discord; bounce 55.58%, 2.81 pages/visit, 2:31 — [Similarweb hamropatro.com](https://www.similarweb.com/website/hamropatro.com/) (Sept 2026)
- setopati.com: 2.2M visits (3-month widget), global #30,579, Nepal #21, Direct 82.82%, then Organic Search, then Paid Social; top social Facebook > X > LinkedIn; bounce 56.45%, 2.37 pages/visit, 1:52 — [Similarweb setopati.com](https://www.similarweb.com/website/setopati.com/) (Sept 2026)
- ekantipur.com: 1.4M visits (3-month widget), global #46,622, Nepal #46, Direct 76.83%, then Organic Search, then Paid Social; top social Facebook > X > YouTube; bounce 67.87%, 2.12 pages/visit, 1:26 — [Similarweb ekantipur.com](https://www.similarweb.com/website/ekantipur.com/) (Sept 2026)

Ahrefs (organic *search* traffic estimates only, not total visits; not comparable to Similarweb):
- onlinekhabar.com ~601K monthly organic search visits (May 2025, +46K m/m) and ~521K (Aug 2025); ekantipur.com ~104K (May 2025) and ~162K (Aug 2025); kathmandupost.com ~88K; setopati.com ~58K (May 2025, -2.6K m/m); ratopati.com ~47K; nagariknetwork.com ~39K — [Ahrefs onlinekhabar competitors](https://ahrefs.com/websites/onlinekhabar.com/competitors), [Ahrefs setopati.com](https://ahrefs.com/websites/setopati.com), [Ahrefs nagariknetwork competitors](https://ahrefs.com/websites/nagariknetwork.com/competitors) (2025 snapshots via search results)

Self-reported / third-party audience claims:
- Onlinekhabar's About page claims "more than 700,000 unique visitors and more than 2.5 million page views every day" (undated, self-reported) — [Onlinekhabar About us](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/about-us)
- Wikipedia: Onlinekhabar founded 2006 by Dharma Raj Bhusal; "by 2020 it became the most viewed news portal from Nepal" (cited to an archived 2021 Alexa page); it bought Himalaya TV (2019) for converged content — [Wikipedia: Online Khabar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_Khabar)
- Onlinekhabar launched an Australian edition in Sydney (diaspora play) — [Onlinekhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/onlinekhabar-nepals-no-1-news-portal-launches-aussie-edition.html) (undated in snippet)

Context on the audience base:
- Nepal had 16.5M internet users (55.8% penetration) and 14.3M social-media user identities (48.1%) in Jan 2025; Digital 2026 restates 16.6M internet users (56.0%) and 14.8M social identities (50.0%) in Oct 2025; 32.4M cellular connections — [DataReportal Digital 2025 Nepal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-nepal), [DataReportal Digital 2026 Nepal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-nepal)
- Facebook ad-reach in Nepal: 17,777,200 users = 55% of population (Sept 2026); 25-34 is the biggest cohort (36%), 18-24 next (29.3%); 56.7% male — [NapoleonCat](https://napoleoncat.com/stats/facebook-users-in-nepal/2024/06) (page now shows Sept 2026 data)
- Kathmandu Post (May 2026) cites 13.5M registered Facebook users and ~3.6M Instagram users in Nepal — [Kathmandu Post via ANN](https://asianews.network/what-the-prime-ministers-asset-disclosure-tells-us-about-nepals-quietly-growing-influencer-economy/) (2026-05-12)

### Inferences
- The desktop "Direct" share of 70-83% almost certainly includes app traffic and dark-social (Messenger/Viber/WhatsApp link pastes) that Similarweb can't attribute; it should not be read as "people type the URL". Still, the pattern says the leading brands have habit-level return visits, not just search/social spikes.
- Hamro Patro's web property being search-led (49% organic) fits its utility content (dates, horoscope, forex) ranking for evergreen Nepali queries, whereas news sites get little durable search value.
- "Paid Social" appearing as the #3 channel for Setopati and eKantipur implies both buy Facebook boosts for distribution.
- A realistic benchmark for a mid-tier portal: Setopati ~0.7M visits/month; a top-tier one: Onlinekhabar ~2.3M visits/month (dividing 3-month widget by 3). A civic-information site reaching 50-100K visits/month would already be a visible niche player.

### Gaps
- No public Similarweb country-level "top news sites in Nepal" list (the free page only shows the global list). Ratopati, Nagarik, Kathmandu Post, Nepal Press, Ujyaalo not individually fetched; Ahrefs organic numbers above are the only third-party figures found.
- No source gives any Nepali portal's share of traffic from Google Discover, Facebook vs. search, or push notifications. The full Similarweb/Semrush channel mix is paywalled. No Nepali publisher publishes its analytics.
- No Reuters Institute Digital News Report covers Nepal; no Google News Initiative Nepal case study was found.

## Key Question 2: Distribution channels (Facebook, Viber/WhatsApp, YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, push, SEO, Google News/Discover, AMP, RSS)

### Takeaway
Facebook is the primary news gateway (41% of survey respondents cited it in 2022, up from 21% in 2020; 62% of social users), YouTube is the fastest-growing (news-source share quadrupled 2020-2022; Kantipur TV ~3.5M subs), and TikTok/Instagram dominate Gen Z. Both platforms were blocked at different times (TikTok Nov 2023-Aug 2024; Facebook/YouTube/X/WhatsApp 4-9 Sept 2025), which is the single biggest distribution risk for any Nepal site that relies on social referrals. Evidence on Viber/WhatsApp channels, push, AMP, RSS and Nepali-language SEO is essentially absent from public sources.

### Cited Findings
Audience surveys (Sharecast Initiative Nepal Media Survey, NMS; face-to-face, nationwide):
- NMS 2022 (n=5,582 adults): share saying they get information from Facebook rose 21% to 41% in two years (caveat in article: many are clicking mainstream-media links on Facebook); YouTube as a local-news source rose 4% to 15% in two years; radio as main local-news source fell 44% to 26%; TV news viewing fell 47% (2020) to 31% (2022); regular newspaper reading fell 32% (2020) to 15% (2022) — [Nepali Times "New news"](https://nepalitimes.com/new-news) and related NMS coverage [Nepali Times "Nepal's new digital landscape"](https://nepalitimes.com/nepal-s-new-digital-landscape), [Nepali Times "Surveying Nepal's digital landscape"](https://nepalitimes.com/surveying-nepal-s-digital-landscape) (2022)
- Among social platforms, Facebook 62.0% and YouTube 44.4% lead; in the 16-24 cohort 82.6% rely on Facebook as primary news source and 51.5% on YouTube — academic synthesis citing NMS data — [NPRC Journal](https://nepjol.info/index.php/nprcjmr/article/view/83835) (2025)
- NMS 2020 (n=4,830, 48 districts, fieldwork Jan-Feb 2020): 88.7% had Facebook accounts; YouTube use 60% (up from 34% in 2019); TikTok 3%; only 2% trusted information on social media — [Nepali Times "Mobile Nepal is hooked on YouTube"](https://nepalitimes.com/mobile-nepal-is-hooked-on-youtube) (2020-03-05) [background]
- NMS 2019: 99% go online via mobile; 88% of internet users on Facebook; Hamro Patro app use grew 1.3% to 4% in one year; 64% rely on family/friends/neighbours for local news; "paywalls fail, online ads are limited and low-yield, and many new portals compete for the same ads" — [Nepali Times editorial](https://nepalitimes.com/new-news) (2019-04-12) [background]
- Press Council Nepal survey (n=176, May 2020): online media was top daily source (31.8%), TV most trusted (50%), social networking sites trusted by 1.1%, online news trusted by 13.1%; >90% said online portals violated the code of conduct — [ShareSansar](https://www.sharesansar.com/newsdetail/a-survey-conducted-by-press-council-nepal-online-media-in-priority-to-get-daily-information) (2020-05-31) [tiny sample]

YouTube:
- Kantipur TV HD ~3.52M subscribers (VidIQ, 2025-01-22); Khabar Bulletin TV ~1.08M (2025-04-26); Sagarmatha TV ~720K (2025-06-13); TV Today HD ~682K (2025-06-24); Nepal Live ~305K (2025-07-03) — [VidIQ Kantipur](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UC3yDoaqQzOd1bNP74ZrGPTA), [VidIQ Khabar Bulletin](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UC3ff2RW28HqBXOET--F98oA), [VidIQ Sagarmatha](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCR0sb6TDx2vY0DGsLlDr5BA), [VidIQ TV Today](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCNSPYrl_sdPfOIS4e1HjhMg), [VidIQ Nepal Live](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCXHgSsTbbojsyot6Kg_q2mg)
- "In-Depth Story", a YouTube explainer channel, and RONB are named by Kantipur's editorial director as the prominent social-native outlets Gen Z uses; he says they "are not full newsrooms and often rely on mainstream reporters or hearsay" — [CJR interview with Anup Kaphle](https://www.cjr.org/the-interview/anup-kaphle-nepal-gen-z-protest.php) (2025-10-08)
- Gen Z "mostly got news from Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, and a narrow set of online outlets" during Sept 2025 — same CJR source

TikTok ban and return:
- Banned Nov 2023 (government cited social harmony; >1,600 TikTok-related cybercrime cases cited); 2.2M Nepali TikTok users at the time per ISPAN; cabinet lifted the ban 2024-08-22 with a 3-month compliance window (register, local contact point, promote tourism, invest in digital literacy); NTA directed ISPs to restore access about two weeks later — [Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2024/08/23/nepal-lifts-its-ban-on-tiktok), [Daily Star](https://d11.thedailystar.net/tech-startup/news/nepal-lifts-tiktok-ban-after-8-months-3684506), [NepaliTelecom](https://nepalitelecom.com/tiktok-ban-lifted-in-nepal), [ANN](https://asianews.network/nepal-lifts-ban-on-tiktok/)

September 2025 ban (the Gen Z trigger):
- 2025-09-04: government blocked 26 unregistered platforms (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, YouTube, X, LinkedIn...) under the 2023 Directive on Regulating the Use of Social Media, after a 7-day registration deadline; TikTok and Viber were NOT blocked because they had registered (also exempt: WeTalk, Nimbuzz, Poppo Live) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/04/nepal-blocks-facebook-instagram-youtube-x-over-rule-breach-amid-censorship-concerns), [Jurist](https://www.jurist.org/news/2025/09/nepal-blocks-26-social-media-platforms-in-historic-crackdown/)
- Protests 2025-09-08 were organised on Discord and Instagram; 19 killed on day one; ban lifted and PM Oli resigned 2025-09-09; total deaths 74 and >2,000 injured per CMR-Nepal; a Hami Nepal Discord server (>160,000 members; >10,000 in the live debate; ~78,000 poll votes) chose Sushila Karki as interim PM — [CMR-Nepal report PDF](https://research.butmedia.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/GenZ_Protest_Misinformation_Report.pdf) (Oct 2025), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/9/15/more-egalitarian-how-nepals-gen-z-used-gaming-app-discord-to-pick-pm) (2025-09-15), [CJR](https://www.cjr.org/the-interview/anup-kaphle-nepal-gen-z-protest.php)
- Nepal Telecom opened a Viber community for customer grievances during the ban, showing Viber as the fallback channel when Meta apps are blocked — [NepaliTelecom](https://www.nepalitelecom.com/2025/09/nepal-telecom-viber-community-contact-for-grievances.html) (Sept 2025)
- Supreme Court (Aug 2025) had ordered the government to ensure registration of social media and online platforms (contempt-of-court case involving an online media), which government cited as basis — CMR-Nepal report citing [Anadolu](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/asia-pacific/nepals-top-court-orders-all-social-media-online-sites-must-register/3661965) (2025-08-16)

Viber (background):
- BBC used a Viber public channel after the 2015 quake targeting "three to four million" Nepali Viber users; a 2015 estimate put Nepal Viber users at ~5M; a Viber exec (2020) said usage grew 20% in Nepal in 12 months and that news outlets run subscribable channels — [Journalism.co.uk](https://www.journalism.co.uk/bbc-launches-nepal-lifeline-on-viber-to-aid-quake-survivors/) (2015), [B360](https://b360nepal.com/over-the-course-of-last-12-months-we-have-seen-that-the-usage-of-viber-has-grown-by-20-in-nepal), [Kathmandu Post interview](https://kathmandupost.com/interviews/2020/01/06/anubhav-nayyar-viber-is-the-only-messaging-app-with-end-to-end-encryption) (2020-01-06)

Google Discover (global, not Nepal-specific): NewzDash analysis of 400+ publishers says Discover was ~two-thirds of Google traffic to news publishers in Q4 2025, up from ~37% in 2023 — [secondary citation](https://inblog.ai/zh-CN/glossary/google-discover); some sites get up to 50% of traffic from Discover — [Newsifier](https://www.newsifier.com/blog/news-seo/what-is-news-seo-95-of-news-websites-are-doing-seo-wrong)

### Inferences
- The two bans prove a platform-concentration risk unique to Nepal: Meta + Google were dark for five days in Sept 2025 while TikTok and Viber stayed up. A civic site should keep at least one registered-platform channel (Viber community, TikTok) and owned channels (web, email/SMS, push).
- Youth attention has shifted to Instagram/TikTok/YouTube/Discord; Facebook remains the mass channel for 25-45. Both must be covered to reach "all Nepal".
- The strong "Direct" share on big portals and the 1-4 minute dwell times suggest portals are consumed as habit (homepage refresh) rather than via deep search; evergreen utility content (Hamro Patro model) is what earns search.

### Gaps
- No Nepali data on Google Discover share, AMP adoption, RSS use, push-notification opt-in, Nepali-language SEO practices, or Google News Showcase availability in Nepal.
- No current subscriber counts for Nepali media Viber/WhatsApp channels; no NapoleonCat Instagram/TikTok Nepal figures fetched.
- No 2024-2026 edition of the Sharecast NMS was found; the latest full survey data is 2022.

## Key Question 3: Routine of Nepal Banda (RONB) case study

### Takeaway
RONB grew from a 2011 student Facebook page posting banda/load-shedding notices into Nepal's most-followed page (2.3M FB likes, 978K Instagram, 196K Twitter by April 2021) on a formula of short Nepali/Roman-Nepali posts, volunteer citizen-sourcing, verification before posting, and ad revenue from films/events; it is now treated by Gen Z as a primary news source but criticised by mainstream editors as "not a full newsroom", relying on mainstream reporters or hearsay.

### Cited Findings
- Started 2011 by Victor Paudel (then 26, a college bridge-course student) as a Facebook page sharing Nepal banda and load-shedding info plus jokes; Paudel credits the unique page name for part of its popularity — [Annapurna Express](https://theannapurnaexpress.com/news/a-peek-into-the-popularity-of-routine-of-nepal-banda-2602) (2020-06-27)
- Mid-2020: >1.8M Facebook followers, >700K Instagram; page self-reports reaching 3.5M people a month (unverified) — same source
- April 2021: 2.3M Facebook likes, 978K Instagram followers, 196.3K Twitter followers, ranked #1 most popular social page in Nepal (ahead of Meme Nepal 1.2M, Stories of Nepal 449K) — [Onlinekhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/most-popular-social-media-pages-in-nepal.html) (2021-04-17)
- Operating model: volunteer teams across the country doing "citizen journalism"; verifies via local authorities, residents and other outlets; says it prioritises accuracy over speed unless urgent; uses 6-7 line paragraphs, simple language, related photos, some Roman Nepali; two-way comms through comments/messages, readers correct mistakes; promotes Nepali photography and tourism — [Annapurna Express](https://theannapurnaexpress.com/news/a-peek-into-the-popularity-of-routine-of-nepal-banda-2602) (2020)
- Revenue (2020): advertising from movies, events and private businesses; had registered as a media company and planned a full news portal and app — same source
- Self-identified limitation: as a Facebook page rather than registered outlet it had trouble covering political content — same source
- Reader quotes: a Kathmandu accountant said news is "kind of 'confirmed'" once RONB posts it; a Chitwan student said it "spreads correct news, not rumors" — same source
- Nepali Times ran "RONB's media model, decade in the making" (2022-07-14) describing it as having grown over 11 years into one of Nepal's largest media outlets — [Nepali Times tag page](https://nepalitimes.com/tag/routine-of-nepal-banda) (article body not retrievable, 404)
- Criticism: Kantipur Group editorial director Anup Kaphle (Oct 2025) names RONB and In-Depth Story as the social-native outlets Gen Z relies on, but says they "are not full newsrooms and often rely on mainstream reporters or hearsay" — [CJR](https://www.cjr.org/the-interview/anup-kaphle-nepal-gen-z-protest.php) (2025-10-08)
- Context on misinformation during the Sept 2025 uprising (not RONB-specific): five categories of false claims (misattributed foreign videos, exaggerated casualties e.g. "Bhatbhateni skeleton", miscontextualised army movements, religious/Hindu-nation narratives, false leadership claims such as ex-PM's wife death); misinformation also spread inside closed groups of opinion leaders; NepalFactCheck.org "with limited resources" could not check all claims and prioritised malinformation likely to incite violence — [CMR-Nepal report](https://research.butmedia.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/GenZ_Protest_Misinformation_Report.pdf) (Oct 2025, author Ujjwal Acharya)

### Inferences
- RONB's moat is brand + habit + brevity, not reporting. Its "confirmed once RONB posts it" status is exactly the trust position a verified-information site should want, and it was earned by (a) a narrow useful beat (bandas, power cuts) that created daily checking behaviour, then (b) broadening.
- Being a Facebook page first made it hostage to the Sept 2025 Meta ban; its Instagram/X presence and later website/app are the hedge.
- The mainstream criticism (hearsay, no original reporting) is the opening for a site that shows sources and verification status on every item.

### Gaps
- No 2023-2026 follower counts found from a dated source (the 2.3M figure is April 2021). No independent audit of RONB's accuracy record, no revenue figures, no documented controversy/correction episodes found in English-language sources.

## Key Question 4: Hamro Patro case study

### Takeaway
Hamro Patro (launched 2010 iOS / 2012 Android as a Nepali calendar) is Nepal's most-downloaded app, with 1.7M DAU / 11M MAU and 10M+ downloads by 2022 (later reports: 1.8M DAU, 12.2M MAU, 15M downloads), and it monetises the daily-habit utility bundle by layering remittance (Rs 100M+/month within seven months of launch) and other services on top; the web property alone draws ~3.5M visits per quarter, mostly from search.

### Cited Findings
- Launched Nepali calendar/date conversion on iOS in 2010 and Google Play in 2012 — [Ratopati English](https://english.ratopati.com/story/24734/hamro-patro-remittance-100-million-one-month) (2022-06-06)
- June 2022: 1.7M daily active users, 11M monthly active users, passed 10M App Store downloads "last year"; remittance feature passed Rs 100M in a single month about seven months after launch; first three transfers free for new users; "Instant Transfer" to banks/wallets planned — same source
- Hamro Patro Remit received Rs 10M in its first two months — [Karobar Daily](https://english.karobardaily.com/hamropatro-remit-receives-10-millionin-2-month/) (undated)
- Later figures: 1.8M DAU, 12.2M monthly users, 15M downloads — [NepaliTelecom](https://nepalitelecom.com/hamro-patro-remittance) (summary via search; the Oct 2025 article itself states ">10 million Google Play downloads", "most popular mobile application in Nepal")
- Oct 2025 (re)launch of US remittance inside the app: free first three transfers during Dashain/Tihar, 2-3 working day settlement with "instant" promised within a month, US first then Japan, South Korea, Australia; wallet for utility bill payments planned — [NepaliTelecom](https://nepalitelecom.com/hamro-patro-remittance) (2025-10-19), [Arthadabali](https://www.arthadabali.com/2025/09/25/78573) (2025-09-25, zero-fee US relaunch)
- Web: hamropatro.com 3.5M visits (3-month widget), Nepal rank #11, 49.41% organic search, social referrals from YouTube, Facebook, Discord — [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/hamropatro.com/) (Sept 2026)
- Survey: Hamro Patro app use as a news/info source rose from 1.3% to 4% of respondents between 2018 and 2019 — [Nepali Times](https://nepalitimes.com/new-news) (2019-04-12) [background]

### Inferences
- The bundle (calendar/tithi, horoscope, forex, FM radio, news, remittance) creates a reason to open the app daily independent of news, then news rides on that habit. DAU/MAU of ~15% (1.7/11) is modest, so the daily core is a fraction of the installed base.
- Hamro Patro is the only Nepali information brand monetising transactions (remittance margin) rather than ads; the diaspora is the paying segment.
- For a civic site: a small utility that Nepalis check daily (e.g., a verified status board, alerts, dates) is a proven habit hook in this market.

### Gaps
- No revenue, ARPU, or ad-vs-remittance split disclosed anywhere. No audited DAU figure after 2022 from a primary source.

## Key Question 5: Business models (ad market, AdSense, local networks, government ads, sponsored content, paywalls, membership, YouTube, diaspora)

### Takeaway
Nepali digital news runs almost entirely on advertising (private + government "public welfare" ads), there is no proven paywall (Kantipur launched its first-ever digital subscription in Aug 2026, priced for diaspora), the sole ad-free membership experiment (The Record) went on hiatus in 2022 for financial reasons, and platform payouts (Google/YouTube 67% of Rs 3.53B inflows in FY2024-25) now rival what independent portals earn, which is why newsrooms shrank (100 outlets closed, 243 journalists quit in 2023).

### Cited Findings
Market/economics:
- Freedom Forum annual report (released 2024-01-02): 2023 was a "huge financial crisis" for Nepali media; advertising slump drove large-scale layoffs; 100 media outlets closed, 243 media persons quit; 52 press-freedom violations (45 in 2022); advertising shifting to digital platforms and apps flagged as a worrying trend; recommended fair, proportional government ad distribution — [Himalayan Times/RSS](https://thehimalayantimes.com/kathmandu/nepali-media-faces-sustainability-crisis-says-report) (2024-01-02), [B360](https://b360nepal.com/detail/21607/nepali-media-faces-sustainability-crisis-says-report-2024-Jan-02-760000)
- Platform inflows to Nepal (Nepal Rastra Bank, newly separated BoP line): Rs 3.53B (~US$23.1M) received from digital platforms in FY2024-25; Rs 2.90B in first 8 months of FY2025-26 vs Rs 2.24B same period prior year; by source Google/YouTube 67%, Meta 12.21%, film & animation 6.33%, other (TikTok, DistroKid etc.) 14.10%; NRB says this undercounts because brand deals and some remittances are booked elsewhere — [Kathmandu Post via ANN](https://asianews.network/what-the-prime-ministers-asset-disclosure-tells-us-about-nepals-quietly-growing-influencer-economy/) (2026-05-12)
- Meta launched view-based content monetisation in Nepal in September 2025 — same source
- Creator economics: The Nepali Comment (442K YouTube subs) earns Rs 300-400K/month from YouTube against ~Rs 350K/month costs for a 10-person team; Project Kura (320K subs, 7 full-time staff) earns US$3-4K (~Rs 400-500K)/month; YouTube pays ~60% of ad revenue; CPM varies massively by viewer location (a Jumla viewer ~Rs 50 vs far more for a viewer in London/NYC) — same source
- Ncell "League of Creators" drew 1,300+ applicants, 50 selected; Daraz prioritises influencer campaigns at Dashain and 11.11 — same source
- PM Balendra Shah's disclosure lists Facebook, YouTube and TikTok as main income sources (4.6M FB followers, 1.3M YouTube subs, 465K TikTok) — same source
- Historical ad market: Advertising Association of Nepal reported Rs 4.3B total turnover in FY2010/11 (Rs 3.5B in 2009/10) — [ShareSansar](https://www.sharesansar.com/newsdetail/ad-industry-logs-20-rise-in-annual-turnover) [dated; no post-2022 AAN turnover figure found]. A Substack post claims a "$2.5B ad economy with <10% captured domestically" but offers no source — [Investor from Nepal](https://investorfromnepal.substack.com/p/increasing-revenue-for-government) (treat as unverified)
- Media Landscapes (2018): "No Nepali digital outlets use paywall or subscription systems"; revenue is advertising; digital ad share "not available" — [Media Landscapes Nepal](https://medialandscapes.org/country/nepal/media/digital-media) [background]
- IFJ South Asia Press Freedom Report 2022-23 has a chapter "Media Sustainability: The Digital Pivot" — [IFJ SAMSN](https://samsn.ifj.org/SAPFR22-23/media-sustainability/) (page returned 404 on fetch; content not verified)

Government / public-welfare advertising:
- Advertisement (Regulation) Act 2076 (2019) created the Advertisement Board; from FY2083/84 newspapers (and other media) not listed and renewed with the Board will not receive public-welfare ad funds; the listing duty was amended by Section 71(4) of the Some Nepal Laws (Amendment) Act 2082 — [Ratopati English](https://english.ratopati.com/story/77190/newspapers-that-do-not-list-and-renew-their-advertising-on-the-advertisement-board-will-not-receive-public-welfare-advertising-funds) (2026)
- Minister Gurung (Sept 2025 appointment of new Board chair) pressed for "scientific, objective, transparent" welfare-ad distribution after Auditor General questions; in March 2025 committed to provincial/local ad mechanisms — [B360](https://www.b360nepal.com/detail/24730/minister-gurung-urges-local-levels-to-channelise-advertisements-through-official-system)
- Supreme Court struck down the government's "one-door" policy routing public ads exclusively through state media; private media now compete equally (petition April 2026, report July 2026) — [Republica](https://myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/sc-strikes-down-govts-one-door-policy-for-public-advertisements-40-97.html) (2026-07-14)
- Policy critique: "it is the media that is benefiting the most from the distribution of the advertisements as opposed to the public" — [ASD Nepal](https://asd.org.np/publications/rethinking-public-welfare-advertisement-nepal-media-welfare-public-welfare/)
- Local-level open data exists on welfare-ad distribution to electronic media (e.g., Birgunj) — [Birgunj data portal](https://data.birgunjmun.gov.np/datasets/distribution-of-public-welfare-advertisements-on-electronic-media)

Subscriptions / membership:
- Kantipur launched "the first digital subscription in Kantipur's history" (Aug 2026): a digital-only tier aimed at diaspora readers (Gulf, US, UK, Australia; price not stated) and "Kantipur One" bundled free for print subscribers (ad-free web/app, early access to investigations, e-paper on WhatsApp, human-narrated audio, English translations, weekly newsletter); rationale: "advertising alone can no longer carry the weight"; >100 journalists; first print price rise (to Rs 20) in 15 years — [Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/opinion/2026/08/25/note-from-group-editorial-director) (2026-08-25)
- The Record (ad-free, independent, English): founders announced an "extended hiatus" on 2022-07-02 citing financial challenges; archives stay free; editor Pranaya Rana continued the "Off the Record" Substack newsletter; site shows a stray Aug 2026 article but no membership/donate messaging — [The Record founders' note](https://recordnepal.com/a-message-from-the-founders-of-the-record), [Kalam Weekly Substack](https://kalamweekly.substack.com/p/off-the-record-069-a-roundup-of-the), [recordnepal.com](https://recordnepal.com/) (fetched 2026-10-14)
- Setopati launched 2013-04-01 by Ameet Dhakal with Narayan Wagle and Yubaraj Ghimire with a small team and no major funding; "If we had started a newspaper, we would never have got this kind of readership within one year" — [Nepali Times](https://nepalitimes.com/setopatis-one-year) (2014) [background]

AdSense:
- Google once listed Nepal among ~14 countries where Western Union AdSense payments were wrongly declined (~5% of payments); an AdMob forum post says Nepal has no international payment service and only a few banks offer SWIFT — [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/?p=61124), [Google AdMob community](https://support.google.com/admob/threads) (both undated/old)

### Inferences
- Realistic revenue stack for an independent Nepali site in 2026: (1) direct-sold banners to banks/telcos/remittance firms, (2) government welfare ads only if registered with DoIB, Press Council and now the Advertisement Board, (3) YouTube/Meta payouts if there is a video arm, (4) diaspora-targeted subscriptions (Kantipur's bet) because diaspora CPMs and willingness to pay are far higher. Programmatic AdSense alone is low-yield for Nepali-language traffic.
- The Record's hiatus and Freedom Forum's closure counts say an ad-free, English-only, membership-funded model did not reach sustainability in Nepal by 2022; no one has since demonstrated one.
- The creator figures (10-person team on Rs 350K/month) are a useful cost benchmark: a small civic newsroom of 5-10 people costs roughly Rs 3-5M/year.

## Key Question 8: Regulatory levers affecting distribution

### Takeaway
A Nepali information site must register with DoIB (fee ~NPR 5,000, annual renewal ~NPR 2,500), get listed by Press Council Nepal (3,000+ online outlets listed), and now also list with the Advertisement Board to receive government ads; the 2025 Social Media Bill was withdrawn in Feb 2026 but the 2023 Social Media Directive (which enabled the Sept 2025 blocks) and ETA Section 47 (routinely used to arrest journalists, incl. an April 2026 warrant) remain live risks; the Media Council Bill (passed Feb 2025) replaces Press Council with a government-chaired Media Council.

### Cited Findings
- Registration path: company registration > PAN > bank account > DoIB online-media registration > Press Council Nepal listing; registration ~NPR 5,000, renewal ~NPR 2,500 (law-firm figures); DoIB's role returned to DoIB at end of 2025; renewal due annually within 3 months of fiscal-year end — [Alpine Law](https://lawalpine.com/blog/online-media-registration-nepal) (2026), [AHN Legal](https://www.ahnlegal.com/legal-updates/legal/online-media-regulation-in-nepal)
- Counts: 2,325 online portals registered with DoIB (early 2021); 2,760 listed with Press Council (c. FY2077/78); 3,120 online news media enlisted with Press Council per CMR-Nepal book (2022) — [Onlinekhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/nepal-has-over-2325-online-news-portals-now.html), [Radio Nepal](https://radionepalonline.com/?p=208877), [CMR-Nepal](https://research.butmedia.org/?p=1149)
- Advertisement Board listing now a precondition for public-welfare ad payments from FY2083/84 — [Ratopati English](https://english.ratopati.com/story/77190/newspapers-that-do-not-list-and-renew-their-advertising-on-the-advertisement-board-will-not-receive-public-welfare-advertising-funds)
- Social Media Bill 2081 (introduced National Assembly 2025-02-09): platform registration, takedown of "indecent/misleading" content, fines up to NPR 500,000/jail for content against sovereignty, fines up to NPR 2.5M for unregistered companies; criticised by CPJ, IFJ, ARTICLE 19, CIVICUS; interim government approved its withdrawal from Parliament 2026-02-03 — [CIVICUS Monitor](https://monitor.civicus.org/explore/nepal-restrictive-social-media-legislation-targeting-of-journalists-and-excessive-force-at-protests-put-freedoms-at-risk/), [UCA News/AFP](https://www.ucanews.com/amp/nepal-social-media-bill-undermines-expression-says-press-group/107977), [CPJ joint statement](https://cpj.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Final-statement-Nepal_05032025-1.pdf), [CollegeNP](https://www.collegenp.com/news/understanding-the-proposed-social-media-bill-implications-and-concerns)
- Media Council Bill passed Feb 2025: replaces Press Council Nepal with a Media Council led by a government-appointed chair, covering "digital, online and print journalism" — [IFJ SAMSN](https://samsn.ifj.org/?p=9553), [CIVICUS](https://monitor.civicus.org/explore/nepal-restrictive-social-media-legislation-targeting-of-journalists-and-excessive-force-at-protests-put-freedoms-at-risk/)
- 2023 Directive on Regulating the Use of Social Media: platforms must register, open a local liaison office, appoint a grievance officer, self-regulate; basis for the 2025-09-04 block of 26 platforms; writ petitions filed at Supreme Court — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/04/nepal-blocks-facebook-instagram-youtube-x-over-rule-breach-amid-censorship-concerns), [Jurist](https://www.jurist.org/news/2025/09/nepal-blocks-26-social-media-platforms-in-historic-crackdown/)
- ETA 2008 Section 47 (electronic publication of material "contrary to public morality or decent behaviour"; up to 5 years and NPR 100,000): Feb 2024 arrests of journalists Aishwarya Kunwar and Puskar Bhatt (Kanchanpur) over social posts; April 2026 arrest warrant against Paschimkhabar.com publisher Basudev Dhami after a municipal official's complaint; warrant against Dil Bhusan Pathak over a YouTube video on Cyber Bureau complaint — [CPJ](https://cpj.org/2024/02/nepali-journalists-aishwarya-kunwar-puskar-bhatt-arrested-under-cybercrime-law/amp/) (2024-02), [IFJ](https://ifj.org/media-centre/news/detail/article/journalist-arrested-under-controversial-nepalese-electronic-transaction-act), [FORUM-ASIA](https://asianhrds.forum-asia.org/en/entity/aeq2d4f2lck) (2026)
- National ID / Nagarik app: National ID viewable in Nagarik app via NIN; 16.1M citizens had NINs; NIN enables passports, bank accounts, SIMs; January 2026 outage of the National ID service — [Onlinekhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/national-id-now-integrated-with-nagarik-app-passport-applications-made-easier.html), [NepaliTelecom](https://www.nepalitelecom.com/2026/01/national-id-outage-on-nagarik-app-find-other-affected-services.html)
- Interim government scheduled general elections for 2026-03-05 — [CMR-Nepal](https://research.butmedia.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/GenZ_Protest_Misinformation_Report.pdf)

### Inferences
- Registration is cheap and is the gate to government ad money and to Press Council protection (Freedom Forum argues complaints should go to Press Council rather than police under Sec 47). An unregistered "page" has neither.
- The regulatory trend since 2023 is toward registration-and-takedown regimes; the Bill's withdrawal is a pause, not a reversal, given the Supreme Court's Aug 2025 order and the still-standing 2023 Directive.

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 official count of Press Council-listed online media; no documentation of whether the Media Council Act is in force post-protest; no source on National ID/Nagarik integration with media/comment systems.

## Key Question 9: New entrants (2023-2026) and civic-tech / verified-information sites

### Takeaway
Public reporting on how Nepal Live, Ukaalo, Kendrabindu, Bizmandu, Himal Khabar, Setopati English or Pahilo Post grew is essentially nonexistent in English sources; the only measurable newcomer signal is Nepal Live's ~305K YouTube subscribers. Nepal's fact-checkers (South Asia Check since 2015, NepalFactCheck.org since April 2020) remain tiny (SAC published 27 fact-checks in 2021) and were overwhelmed during the Sept 2025 crisis.

### Cited Findings
- Nepal Live ("Nepal's independent digital media") ~305K YouTube subscribers (2025-07-03) — [VidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCXHgSsTbbojsyot6Kg_q2mg)
- Himal Khabar organic search presence listed on Ahrefs (no number captured) — [Ahrefs himalkhabar.com](https://ahrefs.com/websites/himalkhabar.com)
- Khoj Samachar: small online outlet sharing verified information in Nepali and English — [Fair Observer](https://www.fairobserver.com/region/central_south_asia/digital-self-reliance-in-nepal-how-grassroots-media-is-empowering-a-new-generation/)
- The Himalayan Times took strategic investment from Team Ventures to "accelerate growth and IPO journey" (Sept 2025 dateline) — [Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/kathmandu/the-himalayan-times-secures-strategic-investment-from-team-ventures-to-accelerate-growth-and-ipo-journey)
- South Asia Check: founded by Damakant Jayshi under Panos South Asia (2015); bilingual; Nepal's only verified IFCN signatory; published 27 fact-check reports in 2021 — [Nieman Reports](https://niemanreports.org/damakant-jayshi-nf-07-brings-political-and-media-factchecking-to-nepal/), [IFCN assessment](https://mail.ifcncodeofprinciples.poynter.org/application/public/south-asia-check/40E853D2-2EE5-00AC-0224-D3A409CAD984)

- During Sept 2025, NepalFactCheck.org "with limited resources and fact-checkers" could not check all claims; Indian fact-checkers' debunks helped; recommendations: government crisis-communication unit, law-enforcement transparency, platform collaboration, long-term media & information literacy — [CMR-Nepal report](https://research.butmedia.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/GenZ_Protest_Misinformation_Report.pdf) (Oct 2025)

- Civic-information surge during the crisis happened on Discord (Hami Nepal), Reddit (where youths "found out that their individual experience... was a shared experience"), Instagram and TikTok, not on news portals — [CMR-Nepal report](https://research.butmedia.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/GenZ_Protest_Misinformation_Report.pdf), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/9/15/more-egalitarian-how-nepals-gen-z-used-gaming-app-discord-to-pick-pm)

### Inferences
- Fact-checking in Nepal is a two-organisation, donor-dependent niche with output measured in dozens of items per year; a civic site that publishes verification status at scale (hundreds of items/month) would be unprecedented in reach.
- The Sept 2025 vacuum ("questions regarding trustworthy sources were raised by many", "even information-literate people shared misinformation in closed groups") is the clearest demand signal for a fast, verified civic-information service; the CMR-Nepal recommendation for "rapid, verified official communication" describes exactly that gap.

No reach figures for BIPAD portal or "Hamro Nepal". No audience data for South Asia Check or NepalFactCheck.org.
