# Nepali online news audience: habits and demographics (2024-2026 fact base)

Research date: 2026-10-14. Scope: who the Nepali online news audience is (inside Nepal and abroad) and how they consume. Every number carries a URL; older data is marked with its year. Where a figure reached me only through a search-engine summary rather than a fetched page, it is flagged "(snippet, unverified)".

Dataset guide, for the writer:
- DataReportal "Digital 2026: Nepal" (pub. 8 Nov 2025, data Oct 2025) is the freshest national connectivity/platform baseline.
- Sharecast Initiative "Nepal Media Survey" (NMS) is the only recurring, nationally sampled face-to-face audience survey. NMS 2024 figures (pub. Dec 2024) are the most recent I could reach; NMS 2022 only second-hand; NMS 2018/2019/2020 fully reachable. No 2025/2026 NMS release found.
- There is no Reuters Institute Digital News Report chapter for Nepal; no 2025/26 national news-trust survey was found.

## Q1. Internet, smartphone and network penetration; data costs; devices

### Takeaway
About 56% of Nepal's 29.6M people are online (16.6M, Oct 2025) and growth has nearly stalled (+0.3% YoY), while mobile connections exceed the population (109%). Access is overwhelmingly mobile and Android (75%); fixed broadband is fast (80 Mbps median) but concentrated in cities. Regulator (NTA) figures count SIMs, not people, and run far higher than user-based estimates.

### Cited Findings
- Population 29.6M (Oct 2025); 23.0% urban / 77.0% rural by UN definition; median age 25.3; 18-24 = 13.6%, 25-34 = 16.7% of population — [DataReportal Digital 2026: Nepal, 8 Nov 2025](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-nepal)
- Note the urban share conflicts with the 2021 census administrative definition: 66.17% live in urban municipalities, 33.83% in rural municipalities — [Wikipedia: 2021 Nepal census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Nepal_census). Use the census figure for "municipal" targeting, the UN figure for "settlement density".
- Internet users 16.6M = 56.0% penetration (Oct 2025); +42K (+0.3%) YoY; 13.0M (44%) offline — [DataReportal Digital 2026: Nepal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-nepal)
- Prior year: 16.5M users, 55.8% penetration (Jan 2025) — [DataReportal Digital 2025: Nepal, 3 Mar 2025](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-nepal)
- Cellular connections 32.4M = 109% of population (end 2025); 82.8% of connections are 3G/4G/5G "broadband"; DataReportal cautions many connections may be voice/SMS only — [DataReportal Digital 2026: Nepal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-nepal)
- The 2025 report had restated 39.0M connections (132%) and 80.5% broadband share; not comparable with 2026 figures — [DataReportal Digital 2025: Nepal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-nepal)
- NTA MIS (cited second-hand): mobile broadband penetration 93.85% of population; fixed/fiber broadband "51.62" (unit not given in article); NTC claims 4G in >98% of local levels and fiber in all 77 districts; explanation of gap vs DataReportal = multiple SIMs, eSIMs, voice-only SIMs, home Wi-Fi users — [NepaliTelecom, 18 May 2026](https://www.nepalitelecom.com/2026/05/only-56-percent-use-intermet-in-nepal-sharp-reality-vs-expection-in-datareportal-figures.html)
- Older NTA MIS (Ashar 2081 = mid-2024) put "total data coverage" at 149.23% population penetration (snippet, unverified) — [NepaliTelecom](https://www.nepalitelecom.com/?p=106560)
- Historic split (2020-21 NTA): fixed broadband 19% of internet market, wireless 76% (2021 data) — [ShareSansar](https://www.sharesansar.com/newsdetail/net-users-on-mobile-increase-nta)
- Median fixed download speed 79.79 Mbps (Aug 2025), +11.4% YoY; no mobile speed given — [DataReportal Digital 2026: Nepal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-nepal)
- Mobile OS share: Android 74.99%, iOS 25.0% (Sept 2026) — [StatCounter, mobile OS Nepal](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/nepal)
- Mobile vendor share (June 2026): Apple 24.98%, Xiaomi 19.49%, Samsung 17.73%, Realme 6.19%, Vivo 5.58%, Unknown 10.54%; Sept 2026 snapshot very different (Unknown 36.1%, Apple 16.47%, Samsung 14.25%, Xiaomi 13.84%, Oppo 3.65%), so monthly values are noisy — [StatCounter, mobile vendor Nepal](https://gs.statcounter.com/vendor-market-share/mobile-device/nepal)
- Household/phone ownership (NMS 2020, 4,830 respondents, 48 districts, Jan-Feb 2020): 96% of households own a mobile; 61.5% of phone owners have a smartphone; 26% use the internet daily; daily use Bagmati 41% vs Far-West 6% — [Nepali Times, 5 Mar 2020](https://nepalitimes.com/mobile-nepal-is-hooked-on-youtube)
- NMS 2022 (as cited in 2026): 90% of adults own a mobile, 63% have internet access — [Spotlight Nepal, 1 Aug 2026](https://www.spotlightnepal.com/2026/08/01/rethinking-formal-communications-in-nepals-digital-era-where-should-leaders-speak/)
- NMS 2024: 73% have internet access — [Nepali Times, 27 Dec 2024](https://nepalitimes.com/what-do-nepalis-think-of-the-media)
- Connection type (2019, NMS): 99% browse on mobile; 59% use mobile data vs 34% broadband/Wi-Fi; mobile-data reliance highest in Far West 80%, Province 2 (Madhesh) 76%, rural 72%, Tarai Dalits 95%; 66%+ of users spend 2 hours or less browsing — [Nepali Times, 12 Apr 2019](https://nepalitimes.com/nepalis-are-drifting-to-digital-media)
- Smartphone share by province (2019): Province 3 (Bagmati) 65%, Karnali 39%; feature phones 56% in Karnali; smartphones 76% among 16-24 — [Nepali Times, 12 Apr 2019](https://nepalitimes.com/nepalis-are-drifting-to-digital-media)
- Data cost: Nepal ranked 10th cheapest per 1 GB globally in a Cable.co.uk ranking (page date ambiguous, 2022 or 2026); global average $4.21/GB in 2021 ranking — [NepaliTelecom](https://www.nepalitelecom.com/nepal-ranks-cheapest-cost-per-1-gb-data); Ncell "Sadhai" packs Rs 99-1,499 for 1-100 GB with up to 15 GB free social-media data (undated) — [NepaliTelecom](https://www.nepalitelecom.com/?p=51600)
- Peak usage: ISPs report home and mobile demand peaks 8-11 pm (2020) — [NepaliTelecom, Mar 2020](https://www.nepalitelecom.com/2020/03/internet-usage-increases-corona-speed-slow.html); NTA peak-hour rule (ISPs must deliver >=25% of subscribed speed) targets the 6-10 pm window; most ISPs share one line among 4 or 8 subscribers (Feb 2023) — [NepaliTelecom](https://www.nepalitelecom.com/internet-speed-at-peak-hours); NPIX national traffic peaked at 109 Gbps around 10 pm on FIFA World Cup 2026 opening day and fell below 50 Gbps by morning — [NepaliTelecom, Jun 2026](https://www.nepalitelecom.com/2026/06/nepals-internet-traffic-peaked-109-gbps-during-fifa-world-cup-2026-opener.html)

### Inferences
- User growth of +0.3% YoY means the online audience is essentially saturated among those who can afford/reach it; growth now comes from deeper use (more hours, more video), not new users.
- Three in four phones are Android and the "Unknown" vendor bucket is large: assume low-to-mid-range Android, mobile-data users on shared or congested links in the evening. Pages must be light and work on spotty 4G.
- Prime attention window is 6-11 pm Nepal time; morning radio-bulletin habit (6-9 am) persists among older/rural users (see Q7).

## Q2. Which platforms Nepalis get news from

### Takeaway
Facebook is the universal layer (14.8M ad reach = 89.6% of internet users; 62% use it for news), YouTube is second (44.4% use it for news), Messenger reaches 11M. TikTok's reach is large (56% of NMS 2022 sample used it) but it is a "go-to news source" for only 1.4%; Hamro Patro, a calendar app, is the go-to for 6.2%. X is marginal (342K). Among 16-24s, 82.6% name Facebook as their primary news source.

### Cited Findings
- Social media user identities 14.8M = 50.0% of population, 75.6% of adults 18+; 89.3% of internet users use at least one platform; gender 43.9% F / 56.1% M (Oct 2025) — [DataReportal Digital 2026: Nepal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-nepal)
- Platform ad reach, late 2025: Facebook 14.8M (50.0% pop, 89.6% of internet users, 43.9% F); Messenger 11.0M (37.1%); Instagram 4.35M (14.7%, +7.4% YoY); LinkedIn 2.30M; Reddit 557K; X 342K (1.2% pop, 82.7% male, -18.9% YoY). YouTube and TikTok reach NOT published for Nepal in this report — [DataReportal Digital 2026: Nepal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-nepal)
- Jan 2025 comparison: Facebook 14.3M (86.2% of internet users), Instagram 3.90M, Messenger 10.9M, X 390K, LinkedIn 2.00M — [DataReportal Digital 2025: Nepal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-nepal)
- NapoleonCat (ad-tool data, Sept 2026): Facebook 17.78M (55% pop; largest group 25-34 at 36%; 43.3% F); Messenger 16.29M; Instagram 5.69M (largest group 18-24 at 41.7%; 40.2% F); LinkedIn 2.72M (25-34 = 47.8%) — [NapoleonCat](https://napoleoncat.com/stats/social-media-users-in-nepal/). Higher than DataReportal because of methodology; treat as upper bound.
- NMS 2024: 62.0% use Facebook for news and 44.4% use YouTube for news; go-to news source: Hamro Patro 6.2%, print 5.3%, TikTok 1.4%; 46.8% of internet users read news on mobile/computer (Gandaki 69.5%, Koshi 57.0%, Bagmati 53.2%, Madhesh 19.9%) — [Nepali Times, 27 Dec 2024](https://nepalitimes.com/what-do-nepalis-think-of-the-media)
- NMS 2024: ages 16-24: 82.6% rely on Facebook as primary news source, 51.5% YouTube, 23.6% radio, 21.3% TV; ages 60+: 38.4% rely equally on radio and TV — [Nepali Times, 27 Dec 2024](https://nepalitimes.com/what-do-nepalis-think-of-the-media)
- NMS 2024: when asked what "media" means, 21.5% said TV, 20.6% radio, 19.9% Facebook, 16.9% YouTube — [Nepali Times, 27 Dec 2024](https://nepalitimes.com/what-do-nepalis-think-of-the-media)
- NMS 2022 (5,582 face-to-face interviews): 56% of sample used TikTok (snippet, unverified) — [Nepali Times](https://nepalitimes.com/nepal-s-interface-with-information); radio audiences moved to Facebook and linear TV audiences to YouTube and TikTok — [Nepali Times, 15 Oct 2023](https://nepalitimes.com/surveying-nepal-s-digital-landscape)
- NMS 2022 primary source of local news, 2020 -> 2022: Facebook 21% -> 41%; YouTube 4% -> 15%; radio 44% -> 26%; TV 47% -> 31% — [Spotlight Nepal, 1 Aug 2026](https://www.spotlightnepal.com/2026/08/01/rethinking-formal-communications-in-nepals-digital-era-where-should-leaders-speak/)
- NMS 2020 platform use: Facebook 88.7% (men 94%, women 82%; rural 88.6% vs urban 87.5%; over-60s 81%); YouTube ~60% (urban 71%, rural 48%, Far West 22%; most popular among over-60s); Messenger 35.6%; WhatsApp 7%; TikTok 3%; Instagram 3%; Twitter 1.3%; IMO female users nearly double male — [Nepali Times, 5 Mar 2020](https://nepalitimes.com/mobile-nepal-is-hooked-on-youtube)
- NMS 2019: Facebook 88%, Messenger 45%, IMO 35%, YouTube 34% regular use; ~8.5M Nepalis on Facebook — [Nepali Times, 6 Sep 2019](https://nepalitimes.com/nepal-s-new-digital-landscape)
- NMS 2018: Hamro Patro named "favourite" by 1.3% of internet users — [Nepali Times, 13 Apr 2018](https://nepalitimes.com/banner/how-nepals-media-landscape-is-being-transformed/)
- Hamro Patro: 15M downloads and 1.8M daily active users (company claim, July 2023); premium ad-free tier launched Feb 2025, Android-first — [NepaliTelecom](https://www.nepalitelecom.com/hamro-patro-app-subscription-membership); Wikipedia says >10M downloads as of 2024 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamro_Patro)
- Press Council Nepal COVID survey (176 respondents, May 2020, small and non-random): online media the top daily source at 31.8%, social networks ~24%, radio ~23%, TV 18.8%, newspapers 2.3% — [ShareSansar, 31 May 2020](https://www.sharesansar.com/newsdetail/a-survey-conducted-by-press-council-nepal-online-media-in-priority-to-get-daily-information)
- Portal traffic (third-party estimates): onlinekhabar.com monthly visits fell from 6.39M (Sep 2025) to 3.89M (Oct) to 3.27M (Nov 2025); ~50% of visits from Nepal, then US and Australia — [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/website/onlinekhabar.com/overview/); Ahrefs organic search traffic: onlinekhabar ~601K, DR 77 — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/websites/onlinekhabar.com/competitors); setopati ~58K (May 2025) — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/websites/setopati.com); ratopati ~47K — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/websites/nepalkhabar.com/competitors)
- Third-party inferred estimate: 1.35M TikTok users in Nepal, ~94% aged 18-34, 85% male (Start.io, Feb 2026; device-data inference, not a TikTok disclosure) — [Start.io](https://www.start.io/audience/tiktok-users-in-nepal)
- TikTok Nepal published a 2025 "top searches" list (creators, athletes, places, TV shows, songs, foods) but no user totals — [eKantipur, 31 Dec 2025](https://ekantipur.com/feature/2025/12/31/en/what-did-nepalis-search-for-the-most-on-tiktok-in-2025-12-05.html)
- Kathmandu youth literature review (2025): Facebook, YouTube and X named primary political-news sources; no percentages — [NPRC Journal, 2 Sep 2025](https://nepjol.info/index.php/nprcjmr/article/view/83835)

### Inferences
- The September 2025 onlinekhabar traffic spike (6.39M) then halving by November fits an event-driven audience: people go to portals during crises (protests, floods), then return to Facebook/YouTube as the default feed.
- Hamro Patro's 6.2% "go-to" share (NMS 2024) exceeds TikTok and print: a utility app with a news tab out-performs dedicated news apps. Distribution inside utility surfaces (calendar, remittance, FM) matters.
- Nepal's social graph skews male (56-57% on Facebook, 60% on Instagram, 83% on X); women are underrepresented online relative to the 52.3% female population.
- X is negligible for reach (1-2% of internet users) but overrepresents journalists/politicians; useful for sourcing, not audience.

### Gaps
- No official or ad-tool YouTube and TikTok reach figures for Nepal in 2025-26 (DataReportal omits them for Nepal).
- No data on Viber/WhatsApp group news sharing or Google Discover share of portal traffic.
- No 2025/2026 Nepal Media Survey release found; the 2024 article does not give sample size.

## Q3. Language: Nepali vs English vs Romanized Nepali vs other national languages; literacy

### Takeaway
Nepali is the mother tongue of only 44.9% of Nepalis; Maithili (11.1%), Bhojpuri (6.2%), Tharu (5.9%) and Tamang (4.9%) follow. National literacy is 76.2% (men 83.6%, women 69.4%), and NMS 2024 shows only 13.1% of illiterate respondents consume news digitally versus 83.3% of the higher-educated. Romanized Nepali dominates informal online text, per NLP researchers; no survey quantifies reader preference.

### Cited Findings
- Mother tongue, 2021 census: Nepali 44.86%; Maithili 11.05%; Bhojpuri 6.24%; Tharu 5.88%; Tamang 4.88%; Bajjika 3.89%; Avadhi 2.96%; Nepal Bhasha (Newar) 2.96%; Magar Dhut 2.78%; Doteli 1.70%; 124 mother tongues total — [Wikipedia: 2021 Nepal census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Nepal_census); Nepali share fell from 58.3% (1981) to 44.9% (2021) — [NepJOL, Trends in Nepal's Linguistic Demography](https://www.nepjol.info/index.php/jj/article/view/83296)
- Literacy (5+), 2021 census: 76.2% total; male 83.6%, female 69.4%; up ~10 points in a decade — [Wikipedia: 2021 Nepal census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Nepal_census); 20,341,623 of 26,675,975 aged 5+ can read and write (76.3%) — [Onlinekhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/official-size-nepal-population-2021.html)
- Madhesh province literacy: Republica reports 76.2% in 2021 (up from 65.9% in 2011) but this equals the national figure and may be a mix-up; Wikipedia lists 63.5% (2024) — [MyRepublica](https://myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/rs-36-billion-spent-on-literacy-campaign-but-112-local-units-still-await-de-75-77.html); [Wikipedia: Madhesh Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhesh_Province)
- Province populations (2021): Bagmati 20.97%, Madhesh 20.97%, Lumbini 17.56%, Koshi 17.01%, Sudurpashchim 9.24%, Gandaki 8.46%, Karnali 5.79% — [Wikipedia: 2021 Nepal census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Nepal_census)
- Education gradient in news use (NMS 2024): 99.1% of higher-educated respondents use news in some form vs 52.1% of illiterate; 83.3% of higher-educated consume news digitally vs 13.1% of illiterate; 45.2% of illiterate respondents are "uncertain" about trusting media — [Nepali Times, 27 Dec 2024](https://nepalitimes.com/what-do-nepalis-think-of-the-media)
- Madhesh (the Maithili/Bhojpuri heartland) is the outlier on digital news: 19.9% of internet users read news on devices vs 69.5% Gandaki; only 60.6% engage with news at all vs 81.7% nationally — [Nepali Times, 27 Dec 2024](https://nepalitimes.com/what-do-nepalis-think-of-the-media)
- Romanized Nepali: NLP researchers state "Romanized text prevails in Nepali online exchanges"; they curated 6,500+ Romanized social-media comments and found converting them to Devanagari improves classifier accuracy — [ACL Anthology, CHiPSAL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.chipsal-1.12/)
- Anecdotal: young Nepalis favour English or Romanized Nepali typing; a vocal minority chastise Romanized use (opinion column, 2019) — [Kathmandu Post, 2 Dec 2019](https://kathmandupost.com/columns/2019/12/02/of-millenials-and-mangled-nepali-on-social-media)
- In a 2024 survey of 108 Nepali TikTok users (urban, educated, 18-34), 84 answered in English and 24 in Nepali when offered both — [arXiv 2510.07200, Oct 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07200v1)
- Print readership is tiny and urban: 13.8% read a newspaper/magazine in six months (urban 28.2%, Karnali 5.7%) — [Nepali Times, 27 Dec 2024](https://nepalitimes.com/what-do-nepalis-think-of-the-media); 2019: 59% don't read print because it isn't available locally; 94% of readers read physical copies, 14% apps, 6% websites — [Nepali Times, 12 Apr 2019](https://nepalitimes.com/nepalis-are-drifting-to-digital-media)

### Inferences
- Roughly a quarter of adults cannot read at all and over half have no mother-tongue relationship with Nepali Devanagari; the "read a long article" audience is the educated, urban, Bagmati/Gandaki/Koshi slice. Everyone else is reached by video/audio and by Facebook, which is where the literacy gradient flattens (Facebook use is near-identical rural/urban).
- Madhesh (21% of the population) is systematically the least served by digital Nepali-language news; Maithili/Bhojpuri video or audio is an under-served channel.
- Urban youth are comfortable in English and Romanized Nepali on UI chrome and comments; Devanagari matters for body text aimed at older and rural readers.

### Gaps
- No survey quantifying reader preference between Devanagari, Romanized Nepali and English for news.
- Province-by-province literacy table from the 2021 census not retrieved (CBS/NSO portal not fetched).
- No audience data for Maithili/Bhojpuri/Newar/Tharu-language news outlets.

## Q4. Format preference and the 2025 shift (short video, live, long-form, audio); September 2025 ban and Gen Z protests

### Takeaway
Nepal has moved to video-first consumption: YouTube went from near-zero (2018) to 60% use (2020) and 44% use it for news (2024); TikTok reached 56% of a national sample in 2022. The September 2025 ban of 26 platforms (TikTok and Viber excepted) and the Gen Z uprising showed the information system runs on TikTok, Instagram, Discord and live streams; legacy outlets (Kantipur, Annapurna Post, NTV) were physically attacked, and VPN downloads rose 8,000%.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube regular use: near zero 2018, 34% 2019, ~60% 2020 — [Nepali Times, 5 Mar 2020](https://nepalitimes.com/mobile-nepal-is-hooked-on-youtube); 44.4% use YouTube for news (2024) — [Nepali Times, 27 Dec 2024](https://nepalitimes.com/what-do-nepalis-think-of-the-media)
- Among 108 Nepali TikTok users (2024, convenience sample, 78% urban, 54% aged 18-24): 68.8% used TikTok multiple times a day; 66.7% primarily for entertainment, 13.9% product discovery, 8.3% shopping; 51.9% only watched; top concern was fake news/misinformation (25.1%); during the Nov 2023-Aug 2024 TikTok ban, Instagram and Facebook were the main substitutes, YouTube often mentioned; 13.9% kept using TikTok via VPN/DNS; 36.4% stopped after the ban; 23.5% returned when lifted — [arXiv 2510.07200 (CSCW 2025), 8 Oct 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07200v1)
- TikTok ban background: imposed 13 Nov 2023, lifted after ~9 months — [Global Voices, 22 Nov 2023](https://globalvoices.org/2023/11/22/nepals-tiktok-ban-is-the-first-step-towards-more-government-control-on-social-media/)
- Sept 2025 ban: on 4 Sept 2025 government blocked 26 platforms incl. Facebook, YouTube, WhatsApp, X, Instagram for failing a 7-day registration deadline; TikTok and Viber stayed accessible; ban reversed 9 Sept — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/nepal-lifts-social-media-ban-after-deadly-gen-z-protests-80561.htm); [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/asia/at-least-9-dead-in-nepal-protests-state-media-says)
- VPN downloads rose 8,000% within days of the ban; protests began 8 Sept, PM Oli resigned 9 Sept; 19 dead in first two days, >70 by 14 Sept; Instagram accounts @HamiNepal and @GenZ.nepal issued protest calls by 6 Sept; a Facebook Live stream carried the Discord leadership discussion on 10 Sept; a British travel vlogger's protest video drew >25M YouTube views; journalists fielded WhatsApp/Messenger queries asking whether claims were true; legacy newsrooms "failed to decode" TikTok's political grammar (#NepoKids) — [Nieman Reports, 19 Sep 2025](https://niemanreports.org/nepal-gen-z-protests-memes-discord-revolution-oli-one-piece/)
- Discord server "Youth Against Corruption", moderated by NGO Hami Nepal, had >160,000 members — [Kathmandu Post via Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/how-nepals-enraged-gen-z-turned-discord-into-a-political-arena/); organiser Sudan Gurung used Discord and Instagram to mobilise — [Reuters via Malay Mail, 15 Sep 2025](https://www.malaymail.com/amp/news/world/2025/09/15/what-happens-when-gen-z-takes-to-the-streets-with-discord-in-nepal-it-led-to-a-government-collapse/191232); "how to protest" videos were uploaded with instructions on what to bring and wear — [Business Today, 9 Sep 2025](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/nepal-protests-gen-z-protestors-mobilising-through-discord-app-to-coordinate-actions-heres-what-they-discussed-493190-2025-09-09)
- Media houses attacked 9 Sept 2025: Annapurna Post office set on fire ~3:35 pm; Kantipur stormed and partly burned (a Discord forum reportedly discussed "attacking Kantipur"); OnlineKhabar threatened and locked down; Nepal Television and Radio Nepal vandalised and set on fire, though the 8 pm NTV news stayed on air via a regional link; journalists hid press IDs — [Global Voices / Nepali Times (Arun Karki), 2 Oct 2025](https://globalvoices.org/2025/10/02/it-is-hard-to-feel-neutral-how-nepals-media-covered-the-gen-z-uprising/)
- Kantipur's and Kathmandu Post's websites went down, cutting online news access nationally; the group later resumed print — [Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/world/gutted-vandalised-nepals-biggest-media-house-building-lies-in-shambles-as-aftermath-of-gen-z-protests-surfaces)
- Short video is "dominant among younger audiences" (TikTok, Facebook Reels, YouTube Shorts); >80% of Nepalis aged 15-29 use social media daily; urban internet penetration >80% (cited to DataReportal and Gurkha Technology, not independently verified) — [Spotlight Nepal, 1 Aug 2026](https://www.spotlightnepal.com/2026/08/01/rethinking-formal-communications-in-nepals-digital-era-where-should-leaders-speak/)
- Industry self-critique: Nepali newsrooms have "no culture of audience research" and limited experimentation with vertical video, podcasts or explainers (opinion) — [Onlinekhabar English](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/nepali-journalism-outdated-outpaced-out-of-touch.html)
- Radio listening time (2019): most listen under 2 hours/day; 54% tune in for two daily bulletins, mostly 6-9 am; listening spikes during elections and disasters; 56% listen on mobile phones — [Nepali Times, 12 Apr 2019](https://nepalitimes.com/nepalis-are-drifting-to-digital-media)
- TV viewing time (2019): 91% watch in the evening — [Nepali Times, 12 Apr 2019](https://nepalitimes.com/nepalis-are-drifting-to-digital-media); 2018: 86% evening; 85% watch Nepali TV for news — [Nepali Times, 13 Apr 2018](https://nepalitimes.com/banner/how-nepals-media-landscape-is-being-transformed/)
- Regulatory backdrop: Media Council Act adopted Feb 2025; Social Media bill proposing up to 5 years' prison for "harmful" content; arrest warrant for journalist Dil Bhusan Pathak under the Electronic Transaction Act — [Nieman Reports, 19 Sep 2025](https://niemanreports.org/nepal-gen-z-protests-memes-discord-revolution-oli-one-piece/)

### Inferences
- The Gen Z audience's news "stack" is TikTok/Instagram for discovery, Discord/WhatsApp/Messenger for coordination and verification, YouTube/Facebook Live for live events. Text portals are the last stop, visited to confirm.
- Both bans (TikTok 2023-24, 26 platforms 2025) trained a sizeable minority to use VPNs; expect a floor of VPN traffic (geo-location of readers becomes less reliable) and resilience planning (mirror on multiple platforms, Viber/TikTok as fallbacks).
- Vertical short video with Nepali captions is the native format for under-35s; live streams carry breaking news; long text serves the educated urban minority and search.

### Gaps
- No quantitative post-September-2025 survey of where Nepalis get news (none found as of Oct 2026).
- No Nepal-specific share of news consumption by format (short video vs text vs audio).

## Q5. Trust: which sources Nepalis trust; misinformation

### Takeaway
Nepalis consume news on social media but do not trust it: only 2-3% trust social-media content and ~8% online portals (NMS 2020/2022), against roughly a third who highly trust TV/radio/print. Yet 67% say they saw misinformation in the past week (CMR Nepal 2024), 81% are concerned, and the 2025 uprising and 2026 Bhote Koshi flood both triggered waves of fake footage and police action against rumours.

### Cited Findings
- NMS 2020: only 2% trust information on social media; radio, TV and newspapers trusted more (no figures given) — [Nepali Times, 5 Mar 2020](https://nepalitimes.com/mobile-nepal-is-hooked-on-youtube)
- NMS 2022: only 3% trust social-media content; 8% trust online portals; radio, TV, print top credibility (snippet, unverified, attributed to Nepali Times coverage) — [Nepali Times](https://nepalitimes.com/nepal-s-interface-with-information)
- NMS (year not specified, cited 2026): roughly one-third express high trust in TV, radio and print; only a small proportion trust social media or online portals — [Spotlight Nepal, 1 Aug 2026](https://www.spotlightnepal.com/2026/08/01/rethinking-formal-communications-in-nepals-digital-era-where-should-leaders-speak/)
- NMS 2018: trust in TV and radio about one-third; newspapers 3%; online information under 2% — [Nepali Times, 13 Apr 2018](https://nepalitimes.com/banner/how-nepals-media-landscape-is-being-transformed/); NMS 2019: 32% named TV most trusted; radio "most reliable" for >25% overall, 55% in Karnali, 40% in Far West; "trust does not depend on audience size" — [Nepali Times, 12 Apr 2019](https://nepalitimes.com/nepalis-are-drifting-to-digital-media)
- NMS 2024: Bagmati has highest trust (47.2% "somewhat trust"); higher-educated report 55.4% trust; 45.2% of illiterate respondents uncertain; 16-24s show highest trust in digital platforms, 60+ lowest with more uncertainty; 38.5% think media cares about their needs, 31.2% do not — [Nepali Times, 27 Dec 2024](https://nepalitimes.com/what-do-nepalis-think-of-the-media)
- Users rely on radio/TV bulletins to verify what they saw on social media (NMS 2022 commentary) — [Nepali Times, 15 Oct 2023](https://nepalitimes.com/surveying-nepal-s-digital-landscape)
- Press Council Nepal 2020 (176 respondents): TV most trustworthy for 50%, online news 13.1%, social networks 1.1%; >90% said online portals violated the code of conduct; Facebook considered the most credible social platform (80.7%) — [ShareSansar, 31 May 2020](https://www.sharesansar.com/newsdetail/a-survey-conducted-by-press-council-nepal-online-media-in-priority-to-get-daily-information)
- CMR Nepal 2024 general-population survey: ~67% witnessed misinformation "last week" (sources: local meeting places, mainstream media, and a considerable share from social media); 81% very concerned/concerned; 97% think it is or will be a big problem; CMR 2022: 92% of heavy social-media users exposed — [Kathmandu Post (Ujjwal Acharya), 23 Jan 2024](https://kathmandupost.com/columns/2024/01/23/the-challenges-of-misinformation)
- CMR Nepal 2020: 95.5% of internet users exposed to disinformation — [CMR Nepal, 13 Sep 2020](https://research.butmedia.org/95-percent-nepali-internet-users-exposed-to-disinformation/)
- Bhote Koshi flood (26 Aug 2026, Rasuwa; 626 bodies and ~2,400 unaccounted by 29 Aug): Foreign Ministry asked media to rely on officially verified information — [MoFA Nepal](https://mofa.gov.np/content/1866/press-briefing-note-by-hon--minister-for/); viral TikTok video was actually a 2025 Uttarakhand flood — [Newschecker Nepal](https://nepal.newschecker.co/en/english/2025-video-from-uttarakhand-falsely-shared-as-recent-bhotekoshi-flash-flood-in-nepal); Press Council Nepal asked >100 accounts to remove content, most complied; Kathmandu posts death-toll updates via social media — [AFP via The Star, 12 Sep 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/09/12/how-china-and-nepal-are-battling-flood-rumours-online); Nepal Police intensified monitoring, arrested a 24-year-old over flood posts incl. a Facebook Reel — [Onlinekhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/police-intensify-crackdown-on-social-media-misinformation-and-rumours.html); police denied relief-hoarding claims as "false, fabricated and misleading" — [Himalayan Times, 9 Sep 2026](https://thehimalayantimes.com/nepal/nepal-police-warns-against-rumours-over-relief-supplies-after-floods)
- Gen Z movement and misinformation: commentary piece on the "misinformation storm" — [Diplomat Nepal, Jul 2026](https://diplomatnepal.com/diplomacy/public/nepal-gen-z-movement-and-the-misinformation-storm/) (not fetched; title only)

### Inferences
- The trust gap is the strategic opening: a Nepali audience that uses Facebook/YouTube daily but trusts neither will reward a source that is verifiably accurate, visibly sourced and arrives in their feed, i.e. "radio-bulletin credibility, distributed on social".
- Disasters now produce an official-channel reflex (MoFA, Police, NDRRMA post figures on social) plus fact-checkers (Newschecker Nepal, Soch) correcting viral clips; a verified-information product should surface official figures with timestamps and flag recycled footage.

### Gaps
- No single 2024-2026 survey giving trust percentages per medium in one table; NMS 2024 article omits them. Sharecast's full 2024 report (sharecast.org.np) should be requested.
- CMR Nepal 2024 sample size and platform-level misinformation breakdown not available (PDF unreadable).

## Q6. Diaspora: how migrants and students abroad follow Nepal news

### Takeaway
About 3.8M Nepalis work abroad excluding India, ~85% in Malaysia and the GCC; FY2025/26 remittances were Rs 2.36 trillion (~$16.2B, +37%). Quantitative media-use evidence is thin: a MIDEQ survey of 281 Nepalis in Malaysia found digital use mainly for calls, news, messaging and video, with Facebook/Messenger/WhatsApp/YouTube daily go-tos for >75%; the Nepal Embassy in Malaysia's weekly Facebook sessions draw "hundreds of thousands" of viewers; onlinekhabar's non-Nepal traffic comes chiefly from the US and Australia.

### Cited Findings
- ~3.8M Nepalis working abroad excluding India; Malaysia and GCC = 85% of labour migration (2024 protocol) — [Sussex Figshare](https://sussex.figshare.com/articles/online_resource/_Study_Protocol_Health_and_wellbeing_issues_of_Nepalese_migrant_workers_in_Gulf_Countries_and_Malaysia_a_systematic_review/23472047)

- Remittances FY2025/26: Rs 2,363.13 billion (+37.1%), ~$16.19B, exceeding the Rs 1.964 trillion national budget — [B360 Nepal](https://www.b360nepal.com/detail/29887/remittance-inflows-to-nepal-surge-371-pc-to-over-rs-2363bn-in-fy-202526-nrb); ten-year average remittance/GDP 24.8% (to FY2023/24) — [NepJOL, The Remittance Engine](https://www.nepjol.info/index.php/nprcjmr/article/view/92475); Gulf 48.9% of remittances, Malaysia 10% (survey year not given) — [ShareSansar](https://www.sharesansar.com/newsdetail/half-of-nepali-households-have-member-working-abroad-survey)

- MIDEQ Malaysia survey (281 respondents, 98.2% migrants, 96.1% Nepal-born; online, COVID-era): digital tech used mainly for audio/video calls, news updates, messaging and entertainment video; disliked for device and airtime cost; "very often" use rose from 3.2% at migration decision to 66.1% while in Malaysia — [MIDEQ working paper](https://www.mideq.org/en/resources-index-page/uses-digital-technologies-nepali-migrants-malaysia/); across MIDEQ's Malaysia/Nepal/South Africa surveys, Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, WhatsApp and YouTube were daily go-tos for >3/4 of respondents, migrant-specific apps barely used — [MIDEQ blog](https://www.mideq.org/en/blog/how-and-why-do-migrants-use-digital-tech); a Nepal-side MIDEQ survey (266 people) found the vast majority have smartphones and access the internet very frequently — [MIDEQ](https://www.mideq.org/en/resources-index-page/uses-digital-technologies-nepali-migrants-and-their-families)
- Nepal Embassy in Malaysia holds weekly Facebook sessions viewed by "hundreds of thousands" of Nepali migrants; migrants used Facebook Live to seek help when stranded during COVID; information sought = news from home, village/neighbour updates, whether newcomers from their village had arrived (2020) — [Nepali Times (Upasana Khadka), 17 Aug 2020](https://nepalitimes.com/from-letters-to-landlines-phones-to-facebook)
- IMO is a women-skewed diaspora-contact app (female users nearly double male, NMS 2020) — [Nepali Times, 5 Mar 2020](https://nepalitimes.com/mobile-nepal-is-hooked-on-youtube); IMO used by 35% in 2019 — [Nepali Times, 6 Sep 2019](https://nepalitimes.com/nepal-s-new-digital-landscape)

- Gulf/Malaysia workers: Facebook (incl. - US/Australia/UK diaspora is the main English-language and desktop-leaning overseas audience for portals, and the likeliest donor/remitter segment during disasters.

## Q7. When and where they consume; behaviour during disasters and outages

### Takeaway
Attention peaks in the evening (TV 86-91% evening viewing; ISP traffic peaks 8-11 pm; NPIX peak ~10 pm), with a secondary radio-bulletin habit at 6-9 am among older/rural audiences. Radio listening spikes during elections and disasters, and the 2025-26 crises show portals and official social accounts surge while fake footage spreads within hours.

### Cited Findings
- 91% of TV viewers watch in the evening (2019) — [Nepali Times, 12 Apr 2019](https://nepalitimes.com/nepalis-are-drifting-to-digital-media); 86% evening, women more in evenings, men more in mornings (2018) — [Nepali Times, 13 Apr 2018](https://nepalitimes.com/banner/how-nepals-media-landscape-is-being-transformed/)
- Radio: 54% tune in to two daily bulletins, mostly 6-9 am; listening spikes during elections and disasters; Karnali listenership 70% vs Madhesh 40% (2019) — [Nepali Times, 12 Apr 2019](https://nepalitimes.com/nepalis-are-drifting-to-digital-media); rural FM listenership ~28%, nearly half rely on FM for local news, 90% listen to radio on mobiles (2020) — [Nepali Times, 5 Mar 2020](https://nepalitimes.com/mobile-nepal-is-hooked-on-youtube)
- Internet peak 8-11 pm (ISPs, 2020) — [NepaliTelecom](https://www.nepalitelecom.com/2020/03/internet-usage-increases-corona-speed-slow.html); NTA peak window 6-10 pm (2023) — [NepaliTelecom](https://www.nepalitelecom.com/internet-speed-at-peak-hours); NPIX 109 Gbps peak ~10 pm, <50 Gbps by morning (June 2026) — [NepaliTelecom](https://www.nepalitelecom.com/2026/06/nepals-internet-traffic-peaked-109-gbps-during-fifa-world-cup-2026-opener.html); 2022 World Cup final peak 37 Gbps, 9-11 pm — [NepaliTelecom](https://www.nepalitelecom.com/nepal-hits-high-internet-traffic-during-football-world-cup-final)
- Crisis surge: onlinekhabar.com 6.39M visits in Sept 2025 (protest month) vs 3.27M in Nov 2025 — [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/website/onlinekhabar.com/overview/)
- During the Sept 2025 uprising, Kantipur/Kathmandu Post sites went offline, "disrupting access to online news for readers across the country" — [Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/world/gutted-vandalised-nepals-biggest-media-house-building-lies-in-shambles-as-aftermath-of-gen-z-protests-surfaces); NTV's 8 pm bulletin stayed on air via regional link — [Global Voices, 2 Oct 2025](https://globalvoices.org/2025/10/02/it-is-hard-to-feel-neutral-how-nepals-media-covered-the-gen-z-uprising/); citizen livestreams and influencers narrated faster than outlets could verify — [Nieman Reports, 19 Sep 2025](https://niemanreports.org/nepal-gen-z-protests-memes-discord-revolution-oli-one-piece/)
- Bhote Koshi flood 2026: casualty figures changed rapidly (359 bodies/910 missing on 27 Aug vs 626/2,400 on 29 Aug); fresh flood alerts issued 28 Aug — [Morung Express](https://morungexpress.com/nepal-authorities-issue-fresh-flood-alert-as-debris-laden-water-flow-rises-in-bhote-koshi); [MoFA Nepal](https://mofa.gov.np/content/1866/press-briefing-note-by-hon--minister-for/); fake TikTok footage circulated within a day — [Newschecker Nepal](https://nepal.newschecker.co/en/english/2025-video-from-uttarakhand-falsely-shared-as-recent-bhotekoshi-flash-flood-in-nepal)
- COVID lockdown 2020: online media became the top daily source (31.8%) in Press Council's small survey — [ShareSansar, 31 May 2020](https://www.sharesansar.com/newsdetail/a-survey-conducted-by-press-council-nepal-online-media-in-priority-to-get-daily-information); migrants stranded abroad used Facebook Live for help; Nepalis in Lebanon used social media to report safety after the Beirut blast — [Nepali Times, 17 Aug 2020](https://nepalitimes.com/from-letters-to-landlines-phones-to-facebook)

### Inferences
- Publish and push between 6 and 10 pm Nepal time for maximum reach; a 6-9 am slot catches the radio-habit audience (and Gulf workers before shifts, since Gulf is 2h45m behind).
- During disasters the audience shifts to (a) official social accounts, (b) live video, (c) radio where power/data fail; a trustworthy portal needs a low-bandwidth live-updates page with timestamps and official-figure provenance, mirrored to Facebook/YouTube/TikTok/Viber because any single platform may be blocked.

- No study of information behaviour during power/network outages in 2024-26 (load shedding ended in 2017-18 as a routine phenomenon; no source found linking it to media habits).
- No audience study of the 2015 earthquake or Sept 2024 floods found in this search round.
