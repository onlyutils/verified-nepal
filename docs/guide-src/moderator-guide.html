What the site does for the public, and what changes when you step in as a moderator.

What is VerifiedNepal?

VerifiedNepal is an independent, volunteer-run website built to help during Nepal's disasters — floods, landslides, fires and more, each tracked as its own event. It connects people who need help with people who can give it, checks missing-person reports against official records, and makes sure the information the public sees has been looked at and confirmed by a real person, not just posted automatically. Everything moderators do is done openly, so anyone can see that the work is honest and fair.

ANYONE VISITING THE SITE

What the public can already do

No sign-in is needed for most of this. It's what every visitor sees and can use today.

  • See the bigger picture — a live map and list of affected districts across every active disaster, with official counts of people rescued, missing, and confirmed safe.
  • Search for a missing person by name, checked against official lists, plus a step-by-step guide for worried families.
  • Ask for help — request food, shelter, transport, medical care, skilled labour, or funds, for themselves or on behalf of someone else, tagged to the disaster it's about — and track the request with a reference code.
  • Offer help — browse open requests and offer to fulfil one. This one step needs a quick Google sign-in, just so offers can be tracked reliably.
  • Report a problem listing — flag anything that looks fake, already resolved, or posted twice.
  • Read and share community articles from the ground, or submit their own for review.
  • Browse verified relief and rebuilding projects — bridges, water systems, schools, and more — see safe ways to donate, and register a new project.
  • See where aid actually went — a public record of help delivered, area by area.
  • See a public log of every moderator action — nothing happens behind closed doors.
  • Find emergency numbers, safe donation guidance, and official government links.
  • Chat with a built-in help assistant.
  • Read the site in Nepali or English, and adjust text size for easier reading.
SIGNED IN, TRUSTED VOLUNTEER

What a Moderator does

Moderators are the trusted volunteers who make sure everything on the site is real. After signing in and agreeing to the moderation guidelines, a moderator gets access to a private workspace and can:

  • Check new help requests are genuine — usually by a quick phone call — before they go live for the public to see.
  • Match people who need help with people offering it, and issue a claim code so help can be handed over safely and only once.
  • Print simple paper claim sheets for use in the field where there's no internet, and record what was handed out afterwards.
  • Verify organizations — review new registrations, usually with a phone call, and confirm the trust tier a drop center or delivered need is shown under.
  • Look into anything the public has flagged as fake, resolved, or duplicated, and decide what to do about it.
  • Check and approve (or reject) new relief projects and community articles before they're published.
  • Choose a district focus. A moderator can scope their own queue, boards and claims to specific districts from their profile, instead of seeing every request nationwide.

Accountability by design: every action a moderator takes is recorded and shown publicly under their name in the site's audit log. That's what makes the whole system trustworthy — the community can always see who did what.

A SMALL NUMBER OF MODERATORS

Growing into an Admin role

A few of the most experienced moderators go on to become Admins. Admins do everything a moderator does, plus manage who else has moderator or admin access, review and approve new disasters before they're selectable on the site, and keep an eye on how the overall response is going — for example, how many requests are still waiting and for how long.
