Need Help?
Here's What to Expect

A simple guide to asking for help — what to submit, what happens next, and how you'll safely receive it.

SEEKING HELP

What you can ask for

You can ask for one of these kinds of help — for yourself, or on behalf of someone else (with their agreement):

  • Goods — food, water, clothing, and other essentials.
  • Shelter — a safe place to stay.
  • Transport — getting somewhere safely.
  • Medical — care or supplies.
  • Skilled labour — someone with hands-on skills to help rebuild or repair.
  • Funds or guidance — money or advice on what to do next.
SEEKING HELP

How it works

  1. Fill in a short form. Your name (or the name of the person you're helping — only with their agreement), your area (district and ward), which disaster this is about — pick from the list, or flag your own if it isn't listed yet — and a short description of what you need.
  2. Submit it. You'll immediately get a one-time reference code on screen.
  3. A real person checks your request. A trusted moderator confirms it's genuine — usually within about a day.
  4. Once approved, it goes public — safely. Your request appears on the site so helpers can see it, but only with your general area shown. Your name and contact details are never shown publicly.
  5. When someone is matched to help you, you'll get a claim code. Show this in person when you collect your aid — a moderator on the ground checks it before anything is handed over.
  6. Running out of time? If your request is about to expire before it's fulfilled, you can renew it using your reference code.

Save your reference code. It's the only way to check on your request or renew it later — write it down, screenshot it, or keep it somewhere safe. There's no other way to look it up.

Your privacy is protected. Only agree to register someone else if they've said yes. Your phone number and exact identity are never posted publicly — a moderator handles matching and the in-person handover, so you're not asked to share personal contact details with strangers.
