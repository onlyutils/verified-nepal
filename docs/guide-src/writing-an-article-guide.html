WRITING AN ARTICLE

What's an article?

An article is a short update from the ground — a situation report, a report from the field, or everyday community life, tagged by topic. Unlike a Story (a single photo and caption), an article has a title, a cover image, and a body you build block by block: paragraphs, headings, quotes, and sourced images or videos.

What you'll need

  • A Google sign-in. Writing requires signing in — it's what lets you save drafts and come back to finish later.
  • A title. Short and clear.
  • A cover image, with a source and an optional caption.
  • Your body text, built from blocks — at least one paragraph, plus any headings, quotes, images or videos you want to add. Every image or video block needs a source.
  • Your display name — how readers will see you — and, optionally, the place you're writing from.
  • 1 to 3 tags describing what it's about, and your language — English or Nepali.
WRITING AN ARTICLE

How it works

  1. Sign in and start writing. Your work saves as a draft automatically as you go — close the tab and come back anytime.
  2. Build it block by block. Add a cover, then paragraphs, headings, quotes, and sourced images or videos, in whatever order tells the story.
  3. Submit it for review once your title, cover, at least one paragraph, and tags are filled in.
  4. A moderator reviews it before it goes live — this keeps what's published honest and grounded. If it's rejected, you'll see the moderator's reason and can revise and resubmit.
  5. Once approved, it's published under your display name and place — readers can view, like and share it, and it keeps a simple count of each.

Good to know: your account email is never shown publicly — only your display name and place appear on the published article. Write about what you've actually seen or experienced; that's what makes an article worth reading.
